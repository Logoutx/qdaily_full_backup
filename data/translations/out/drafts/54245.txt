@@QD_BODY@@
How do architecture, art, commerce, politics, and culture get all tangled up together?

This year's Serpentine Pavilion, designed by Mexican architect Frida Escobedo, is a courtyard enclosed by a "lattice" screen.

[The Serpentine Gallery](http://www.serpentinegalleries.org), located in Kensington Gardens in London's Hyde Park, is one of Britain's best-known museums of modern and contemporary art. Every summer since 2000, it has invited an architect (sometimes an artist) or a design team to build a temporary pavilion on the lawn in front of the gallery's main entrance — known as the Serpentine Pavilion — which stays on view for three months and is free and open to the public. (We have covered [pavilions from previous years](https://www.qdaily.com/articles/22844.html).)

![Serpentine Gallery. Photo: [HG2](http://hg2.com/venue/serpentine-gallery/)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614130221gZLxJYwu0apyB69n.jpg-w600)

Today, the pavilion is a hot destination in London each summer, and the program has become one of the world's most prestigious architectural commissions. The invited architects also come under intense scrutiny — because, as [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/02/09/arts/design/serpentine-pavilion-london.html) puts it, the project is seen as a leading indicator of which international masters are about to emerge.

The designer in the first year was the late Zaha Hadid, who received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2004. In 2012, the invitees were Ai Weiwei and Herzog & de Meuron (HdM), the firm behind the Bird's Nest. Olafur Eliasson, the artist we profiled a few days ago who designed a headquarters called "Fjord House" for a financial company, was also invited to design a pavilion in 2007.

![Serpentine Pavilion by Zaha Hadid, 2000. Photo: [Dezeen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VOqphsT2rE)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614130406zxPDjQMu0cANoSfk.jpg-w600)

![Serpentine Pavilion by Ai Weiwei & HdM, 2012. Photo: HdM via [Arch2o](https://www.arch2o.com/serpentine-gallery-pavilion-2012-herzog-de-meuron/arch2o-serpentine-gallery-pavilion-2012-herzog-de-meuron-07/)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614130716nLME7HmxFROcSpt3.jpg-w600)

### London

This year's Frida Escobedo is the youngest architect the program has had to date — 38 — and the first woman since Zaha Hadid. When the email from the Serpentine arrived, Escobedo's [first thought](https://www.ft.com/content/73a242d4-657e-11e8-bdd1-cc0534df682c) was that she had been put on a mass mailing list.

Up close, the walls of her "lattice" pavilion turn out to be made of many concrete tiles "stacked" together — but not stacked flat: each pair of tiles is set at 90 degrees to each other, and because the tiles are curved, light passes through the gaps between them. ["(You can see) the influences of Mexico and Britain converging here,"](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2018/jun/11/serpentine-pavilion-2018-review-breaks-down-barriers-by-putting-up-walls) is how gallery director Hans-Ulrich Obrist and CEO Yana Peel describe Escobedo's work.

![Photo: [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2018/06/11/frida-escobedo-serpentine-pavilion-2018-woven-tapestry-concrete-tiles/)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614130855HxzgCo5Sha3428JL.jpg-w600)

![Photo: [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2018/06/11/frida-escobedo-serpentine-pavilion-2018-woven-tapestry-concrete-tiles/)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614130943njgIArLW3V0FE2bz.jpg-w600)

The Mexican touch refers to the perforated walls, called "celosías," inspired by the breeze walls of Mexican architecture — walls that let the wind through, in other words. The gray concrete tiles, meanwhile, are mass-produced industrially and are a common roofing material in Britain.

![The architect's earlier work featuring "breeze walls." La Tallera art gallery in Cuernavaca, Mexico, designed by Frida Escobedo. Photograph- © Frida Escobedo, Taller de Arquitectura](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614131038QmE2hiyXlkWaJz05.jpg-w600)

Beyond the walls, two design features inside the pavilion draw the eye. Overhead is a curved reflective ceiling; underfoot is a shallow pond. The art review in [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2018/jun/11/serpentine-pavilion-2018-review-breaks-down-barriers-by-putting-up-walls) writes: "Escobedo has got the jump on a treacherous summer — by designing an indoor pool to make sure enthusiastic visitors leave with wet socks."

At the press preview before the public opening, the article's author noticed some people tiptoeing into the pool — "as if trespassing into the pristine water of a luxury apartment." He hopes that by the official public opening over the weekend, "more people can turn their feet into paddles — go for a paddle — and bring this serene, elemental place back down to earth."

![Photo: [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2018/06/11/frida-escobedo-serpentine-pavilion-2018-woven-tapestry-concrete-tiles/)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614131120zdaEWeDvoKtwfFUh.jpg-w600)

Looking at the pavilion's plans, [it is made up of two overlapping triangles](https://www.dezeen.com/2018/06/11/frida-escobedo-serpentine-pavilion-2018-woven-tapestry-concrete-tiles/). The outer wall aligns with the edge of the gallery behind it, while the inner courtyard is aligned with the Prime Meridian in Greenwich, ten miles away. [Escobedo explains](https://thespaces.com/2018/06/11/frida-escobedo-serpentine-pavilion-2018/) that through this gesture of homage she can resolve an inherent contradiction of the temporary pavilion: going by past experience, the "temporary" pavilion will in the end be permanently collected somewhere (by a museum, perhaps, or some other institution).

![Photo: [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2018/06/11/frida-escobedo-serpentine-pavilion-2018-woven-tapestry-concrete-tiles/)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201806141313550zXjeIJxsKNinybS.jpg-w600)

![Photo: [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2018/06/11/frida-escobedo-serpentine-pavilion-2018-woven-tapestry-concrete-tiles/)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614135123v5jQrKVHMBihFfCE.jpg-w600)

The architect's reading seems a bit of a leap. But her words remind us of one fact: for these temporary buildings — one per year, each built within six months and on view for three — the temporality may be little more than talk. It only adds to their talking points and strengthens the influence of this summer architecture program: being invited to design it has become a kind of accolade and honor in the architecture world — the icing on the cake for those already famous, and an extra booster for those on the rise (though already well known).

This judgment, however, does not slight these architects and artists in the least. These summer pavilions really are fine works — especially in the sense of exploring and pushing the boundaries of architecture.

In the program's 11th year, British architecture critic Rowan Moore, [trained in architecture at Cambridge](http://www.nybooks.com/articles/2014/06/05/insolence-architecture/), wrote in [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2010/may/23/serpentine-pavilions-ten-years-on): "The project is a huge success built on some (rather) dubious ideas — among them that architecture can be a work of art worth collecting, and that architecture can work its magic detached from function and purpose."

Moreover, a pavilion is usually more than a building or an installation; it also has [sociability](https://openartsjournal.files.wordpress.com/2013/11/oaj_issue2_junyk_final.pdf) — people gather here for events, talks, and so on — further extending its influence.

![Park Nights: Alexander Kluge. [Serpentine Pavilion](https://www.google.co.uk/url?sa=i&rct=j&q=&esrc=s&source=images&cd=&cad=rja&uact=8&ved=2ahUKEwjY-cT4stLbAhXFbRQKHdo-B-oQjhx6BAgBEAM&url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.serpentinegalleries.org%2Fexhibitions-events%2Fpark-nights-alexander-kluge&psig=AOvVaw3xWqSdSlT6ZZW58yGQ5pby&ust=1529039699027083)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614131539T6qx80t4wguGzAem.jpg-w600)

The gallery has benefited as well. Before the program launched in 2000, the Serpentine Gallery — though called a gallery, it is not quite what "gallery" usually means in the American sense; think of it as an art museum, or more broadly an art space — was not as influential as it is today. The Serpentine was founded in 1970 and occupies a building completed in the 1930s, which before the gallery was founded had served as a royal tea house.

In 1998, [with the backing of Princess Diana](http://www.serpentinegalleries.org/about), curator and gallery director Julia Peyton-Jones, a graduate of the Royal College of Art (RCA), began a major renovation of the gallery. ([She stepped down in 2016, after 25 years](http://art.ifeng.com/2015/1102/2585372.shtml).)

In 2000, she succeeded in inviting Zaha Hadid to build a temporary pavilion, and afterwards made the practice a fixture, setting one rule: only architects or design teams who had never completed a building in Britain would be invited — though over 18 years a few exceptions appeared. (To be charitable, the reason may be that the standard Jones originally set was never having completed a [commissioned building](http://www.thersa.org/events/speakers-archive/p/julia-peyton-jones), while both [the official site](http://www.serpentinegalleries.org/about) and the common account phrase it as never having completed a building at all — the difference lying in "commissioned.")

Objectively, this has given the gallery's program a singular position at the very frontier of the architecture world: imagine — in most cases — it amounts to introducing a highly acclaimed architect to Britain for the first time, and because it is a pavilion, there are no particular constraints on what the building has to be. The architect can play to their strengths and concentrate on presenting their architectural ideas.

First-rate architects, a concentrated statement of ideas, plus a first entry into Britain — naturally the work will not be perfunctory; in fact, it often seeks to push further. With this layered, self-selecting process in place, it is no surprise that the program, and the gallery itself, have attained their current standing.

### Beijing

Precisely because of its fame, when the gallery announced in late January that "the first Serpentine Pavilion overseas" would be sited in Beijing, the [news](https://www.archdaily.com/888170/jiakun-architects-selected-to-design-first-foreign-serpentine-gallery-pavilion-in-beijing) drew immediate attention. In early February, the Serpentine announced the architect of the Kensington Gardens pavilion. Before that announcement came out, some Chinese-language reports were rather "excited," using descriptions like "[Serpentine Gallery debuts in China](http://www.archiposition.com/information/item/2110-first-foreign-serpentine-pavilion-will-be-built-in-beijing-this-year.html)" and "[In 18 years, not a single Chinese architect has been invited](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/T5CwhedNSQerddG-v8PtCg)," and often simply juxtaposed it with pavilions of past years.

As things stand, these are two systems running in parallel.

![Source: [WF Central](https://hk.prnasia.com/story/212371-2.shtml)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614132316myw9X0WtAUBQYcZs.jpg-w600)

On May 29, the Serpentine's [Pavilion Beijing](http://www.serpentinegalleries.org/sites/default/files/press-releases/serpentine_pavilion_beijing_cn_31jan.pdf) (Serpentine Pavilion Beijing) was unveiled at WF Central in Beijing. The Beijing pavilion was designed by architect Liu Jiakun, whose representative works include the [Luye Yuan Stone Sculpture Art Museum](http://www.jiakun.com/index.php/Home/Index/pjct_details.html?id=3) and [Xicun · Beisen Courtyard](http://www.jiakun.com/index.php/Home/Index/pjct_details.html?id=2) (Xibei Courtyard); we have written about this Chengdu ["mega-courtyard"](https://www.qdaily.com/articles/26553.html).

According to [the Serpentine's official site](http://www.serpentinegalleries.org/exhibitions-events/serpentine-pavilion-beijing-2018-0), the Beijing pavilion's most distinctive feature is its overall form: like multiple bows drawn but not yet released — curved cantilevered steel beams lined up in a row, their far ends connected to steel cables. This somewhat resembles *With the Wind 2015: Your Choice*, Liu Jiakun's entry at the 2015 Venice Biennale. [Liu Jiakun explains](https://www.archdaily.cn/cn/888184/lun-dun-she-xing-hua-lang-shou-ci-chu-jing-dao-bei-jing-liu-jia-kun-zhu-dao-she-ji-qing-ying-gong-xing-zhan-ting) that the pavilion's design draws on the Confucian "way of the gentleman," expressing the spirit of "overcoming hardness with softness" through its unique structure.

![*With the Wind 2015: Your Choice*. Source: [Jiakun Architects' official site](http://www.jiakun.com/index.php/home/index/extion_details.html?id=1)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614132411mC6oMUbsFjYEg7Lk.jpg-w600)

"Although Beijing's modern architecture already has sufficient technology to cope with strong winds and earthquakes," [the official site](http://www.serpentinegalleries.org/exhibitions-events/serpentine-pavilion-beijing-2018-0) continues, "the pavilion's internal structure — like a tai chi master — conquers the effects of those external forces in a soft manner."

"I am an architect; force is something I pay attention to in my daily work, and force is everywhere," Liu Jiakun said in an interview with [Phoenix Art](http://art.ifeng.com/2018/0605/3424954.shtml). "How to treat force — to resist it, to submit to it, or to steer it to one's advantage — is not just a technical attitude but a cultural one."

Liu Jiakun can be called a representative of China's ["critical regionalism" and "vernacular school"](http://www.idc.net.cn/sjs/gnsjs/a_111767.html) (this is not an established label — it is simply how this article sums it up). Similar architects include Pritzker laureate Wang Shu. Whether in Liu Jiakun's early Luye Yuan Stone Sculpture Art Museum or the more recent Xibei Courtyard, one can see elements of China's rural traditions and indigenous architecture.

![Luye Yuan Stone Sculpture Art Museum. Source: [Jiakun Architects](http://www.jiakun.com/index.php/Home/Index/project.html)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614132732rMum5c7kLlTvf64C.jpg-w600)

![Xicun · Beisen Courtyard. Source: [Jiakun Architects](http://www.jiakun.com/index.php/Home/Index/project.html)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614132748MG0jWoYLHuNJCQVO.jpg-w600)

This can also be seen in the [firm's](http://www.jiakun.com/index.php/home/index/about.html) philosophy — "live in symbiosis with nature; respect history, attend to reality, learn from folk wisdom; the here-and-now, a realistic spirit of adapting measures to local conditions; dedicated to a contemporary architectural interpretation of Eastern sensibilities."

"The idea of poised readiness comes from the East... making an installation like this (drawn but not yet released) aims to expand the boundaries of contemporary architecture," [the South China Morning Post](http://www.scmp.com/culture/arts-entertainment/article/2148839/serpentine-gallery-londons-beijing-pavilion-and-architect) quotes Liu Jiakun, and then comments: "Liu's style is a far cry from the gaudy, flamboyant architecture that has sprouted across China in recent years to showcase the country's growing economic strength and power."

Making a similar association is Aric Chen, head curator of art and architecture at Hong Kong's M+ museum — itself a museum that cannot be ignored in China's contemporary art world in recent years; in 2016 it showed more than 1,500 works from the Sigg collection in [a retrospective](https://www.westkowloon.hk/tc/siggcollection) of forty years of Chinese contemporary art — probably the most prominent exhibition on Chinese contemporary art before the 2017 Guggenheim show.

Chen [wrote](https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/serpentine-pavilion-beijing-jiakun-architects/index.html): "For the hugely successful first Serpentine pavilion overseas, you might expect a bit more flamboyance... (But) you will be struck by its daring simplicity." He sees Liu's style as a sharp contrast to the dazzling, tangled steel structure of the Bird's Nest from the 2008 Olympics — a period when the Games marked China's return to the international stage as a global power (concentrated, as it happens, in the Bird's Nest's design).

This is also a common view in (Western) art circles in recent years regarding the development of Chinese contemporary art in the broad sense, including architectural forms; that view is also embodied in Cai Guo-Qiang's fireworks made for the Olympics (see the 2017 Guggenheim show).

!["Theater of the World": the Chinese contemporary art exhibition at the Guggenheim in New York, 2017. Photo by our reporter](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614133806wjHqGhzm4kyrpiV8.jpg-w600)

The author classifies Liu's work as realist and "low-tech" — Liu's own words — and as something that can connect with ordinary people. In the words of the Serpentine's current director [Hans Ulrich Obrist](https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/serpentine-pavilion-beijing-jiakun-architects/index.html), it "connects with the local context, linking Chinese public life with urban life."

In 2008, the year fireworks blossomed over the Bird's Nest, Liu Jiakun was working on a project in Wenchuan called ["Rebirth Bricks"](http://www.jiakun.com/index.php/home/index/pjct_details.html?id=5), using rubble from the post-earthquake ruins and straw, an agricultural waste material, to make bricks for post-disaster self-rescue and self-built production.

![Liu Jiakun. Source: [Jiakun Architects](http://www.jiakun.com/index.php/Home/Index/project.html)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614132456vmkHq9E8QhLzgtOM.jpg-w600)

![Rebirth Bricks. Source: [Jiakun Architects](http://www.jiakun.com/index.php/Home/Index/project.html)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201806141325074tUOGXzBT7PKcH5b.jpg-w600)

Chen's article also "astutely" makes a judgment: the Beijing pavilion marks Chinese architecture's entry into a new era.

What does that mean? The article notes that although those exaggerated, grand, kaleidoscopic, "anti-gravity" buildings are not yet out of date — one need only glance at Beijing's big office towers — beneath the surface, the focus has already shifted, and methods and ideas rooted in Chinese culture are drawing more attention. Small-scale building practices that use mostly local materials have been pursued all along by Liu Jiakun, Wang Shu, and others, and by younger architects including Zhang Lei and Zhang Ke. From the growing cultural confidence to specific government policies for developing the countryside, these practices have all received encouragement, which has also pushed their work to the fore.

![The Xiangshan campus of the China Academy of Art designed by Wang Shu. Photo by our reporter](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614134950LxBKi2GYguz7pkNT.jpg-w600)

"He is the right person appearing at the right time," gallery CEO [Yana Peel said](https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/serpentine-pavilion-beijing-jiakun-architects/index.html).

British Prime Minister "Auntie May" [visited China](http://wap.art.ifeng.com/?app=system&controller=artmobile&action=content&contentid=3406160) earlier this year, bringing a package of new UK-China cultural cooperation agreements, and, because of the Beijing pavilion project, met the gallery's director Hans Ulrich Obrist and CEO Yana Peel.

If those judgments still belong to the realm of art-world or critical commentary, the influence of commerce and real estate is plainly visible.

The Beijing pavilion is a commissioned collaboration between the Serpentine Gallery and WF Central. The pavilion sits on a 6,400-square-meter green plaza on the west side of WF Central. [WF Central](http://www.wfcentral.cn/index.php?m=content&c=index&a=lists&catid=42) is a "fashionable high-end lifestyle center" that Hongkong Land spent six years building on the Wangfujing commercial street, beginning trial operations at the end of last year. Hongkong Land is a "market leader" in luxury retail; its best-known project in Hong Kong is Landmark.

![Source: [WF Central](https://hk.prnasia.com/story/212371-2.shtml)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614132633i8CQ5yFNzdI1l4Kt.jpg-w600)

![Source: screenshot from Phoenix Art](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614133456s0LlNTqeGVHnb1Qt.jpg-w600)

According to [People's Daily Online](http://bj.people.com.cn/n2/2018/0530/c82840-31645388.html) and [Beijing Daily](http://www.bjd.com.cn/zc/shq/201711/29/t20171129_11075825.html), both WF Central and the pavilion's opening are regarded as an important step in the transformation and upgrading of the Wangfujing commercial district — "Since last year, the Wangfujing commercial street has undergone sweeping environmental renovation, eliminating some business formats that did not fit the street's positioning and upgrading facilities such as pedestrian walkways... The goal for the Wangfujing commercial street is to become a commercial street with international flair, Beijing flavor, a sense of fashion, and cultural richness." At the [unveiling ceremony](http://art.ifeng.com/2018/0605/3424954.shtml), numerous figures from business, culture, and the performing arts were in attendance. After the pavilion's unveiling, the WF Central shopping center also opened at the same time. Afterwards, a series of events and talks will be held at this drawn-but-unreleased, bow-shaped pavilion.

"Art and architecture can enrich the experience of luxury shopping," said Raymond Chow, executive director of Hongkong Land, [in a statement](http://www.scmp.com/culture/arts-entertainment/article/2148839/serpentine-gallery-londons-beijing-pavilion-and-architect).

![Source: [WF Central](https://hk.prnasia.com/story/212371-2.shtml)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180614132608wnbTVOyCZ6sJaIjL.jpg-w600)

Closely tied to commerce and real estate, this kind of architecturally driven art project is not the first, and one imagines will not be the last.

According to [Phoenix Art's video](http://art.ifeng.com/2018/0605/3424954.shtml), the Serpentine Gallery plans to invite a designer to create the Beijing pavilion every year from now on — just like the London pavilion's summer program. Hans Ulrich Obrist also said the Beijing pavilion model could be exported to other cities.

Architect Ma Yansong took part in activities around the pavilion's unveiling. He later posted on Weibo as [@MAD_Ma Yansong](https://www.weibo.com/2180047725/GiX03j36E):

"At the opening of the Beijing Serpentine pavilion designed by Liu Jiakun, we all gathered again for #Yuhe Night Talk#, discussing the influence art and architecture bring to society. I believe a profession must maintain its own independent value; only with clear discourse and methods can it define the meaning of its own existence, even decide its own life and death — death is also for the sake of rebirth. Architecture should emphasize individual feeling; art and architecture subordinated to politics, commerce, or other cultures will instead make the public overlook the inherent power of the profession. Our shared consensus: art is hope."

The London and Beijing pavilions are open June 15–October 7 and May 30–October 31, respectively.
