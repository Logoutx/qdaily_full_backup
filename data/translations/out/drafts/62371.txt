Public opening days run from the 26th to the 29th, though most exhibitions will continue through to the end of the season.

With the opening of the third Gallery Weekend Beijing on the 22nd, a large number of Beijing art institutions are holding their first shows since the Lunar New Year.

This year's gallery week brings together 27 galleries and non-profit institutions. New participants include the Faurschou Foundation, Hunsand Space, Song Art Museum, Star Gallery, and Tabula Rasa Gallery—more in number than last year, but the shift also reflects a turnover between old and new galleries.

[According to Beijing Business Today](http://www.sohu.com/a/302938392_115865), Yang Gallery, winner of last year's Best Exhibition award, has closed this year; Pace Beijing, which created the viral "Flower Forest" exhibition, has reduced its space and is not taking part in this year's gallery week either. Most of the new participants arrived only recently—Star Gallery, for instance, has just set up in the 798 Art District, and its sign is still temporary.

Wang Yanling, founder of the 798 Art District, says Gallery Weekend Beijing is positioned to present China's most avant-garde contemporary art to the world within an international-dialogue context. Now in its third edition, Gallery Weekend Beijing still appears less established than Shanghai or Hong Kong. This year, the first "Beijing International Art Summit Forum" is being held at the same time, aiming to create a more influential discursive field.

Three special sections have been set up this year. The Visiting Section has partnered with Zurich Art Weekend for an exchange, bringing two mid-sized Zurich galleries into 798 Art District exhibition spaces for the first time; the New Forces Section focuses on the work of a group of young Chinese artists; and the Public Section presents large-scale sculptures and installations in the 798 Art District's outdoor spaces and at The Opposite House in Beijing.

Gallery Weekend was originally designed to facilitate exchange among galleries and serve collectors. Gallery Weekend Beijing is scheduled each year just before the opening of Art Basel Hong Kong so that collectors, gallerists, and artists can gather efficiently. For ordinary viewers, there is no need to feel pressured to see everything at once; here is a guide to some exhibitions we think are worth seeing.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20190325214158ka68Kr1Pb9GN5xFW.jpeg-w600)

Two documentary photography exhibitions running concurrently turn their lenses toward the faces of unknown people.

Leila Alaoui was a French-Moroccan photographer whose work addressed identity construction, cultural diversity, and migration in the Mediterranean region. In 2016, while working in Burkina Faso in West Africa on Amnesty International's women's human rights program, she was killed in a local terrorist attack. Her sister wrote [these words in her memory.](http://www.yasminaalaoui.com/i-forgive-you)

> The mafia sold them the dream of Paris, Madrid, Milan, or Brussels, and sold you the dream of paradise: seventy-two young girls, rivers flowing with wine, trees dripping with honey...

> You see, you who killed me, I would want to know your name, your story, and fragments of your life, and then record them through photography and show them to the world—perhaps... it could save you.

Galleria Continua's photography exhibition *You Who Are Leaving* 《离别的你》 is Alaoui's first solo show in China. The gallery has devoted a vast wall in its expansive exhibition space to her portrait series *The Moroccans*, in which men and women, the elderly and children, their richly varied traditional dress, are all solemnly recorded through a unified visual language.

Her work gives a direct gaze to marginalized and voiceless people. *No Crossing* 《禁止穿越》 shows the faces encountered by a young Moroccan fleeing to Europe; *Devil's Island* 《恶魔之岛》 centers on immigrant workers who labored in French factories on Gorée Island, known as "Devil's Island"; and the *Crossings* 《穿越》 series comprises portraits of sub-Saharan African migrants. Displaced by circumstances beyond their control and lumped under a single label, these individuals are rendered in plain, quiet images through which Alaoui seeks to convey their individual dignity and cultural diversity.

Star Gallery's solo photography exhibition *Spring Breeze* 《春风》 comes from Liu Heung Shing, a Pulitzer Prize-winning documentary photographer. His iconic work narrates two of the twentieth century's most momentous historical episodes: the rise of China and the dissolution of the Soviet Union.

The works on view are selected from the 1983 Penguin Books photography collection *China After Mao*, taken by Liu while working as an Associated Press correspondent in China between 1976 and 1983. Though he was present at a pivotal historical moment, Liu's photographs are not confined to major events or political statements. In fact, most of the works chosen for this exhibition adopt the perspective of everyday urban life—during a period of tremendous change, people were full of anticipation for the new, and history's grand narrative seeps through the vivid daily lives of ordinary people.

*Spring Breeze* won the Best Exhibition award at this year's Gallery Weekend Beijing. At Art Basel Hong Kong, opening on March 28, Star Gallery will also present Liu's solo exhibition *A Life in a Sea of Red*. [The monograph of the same name, published by German publisher Steidl](https://steidl.de/Books/A-Life-in-a-Sea-of-Red-0311164760.html) is about to be released in Hong Kong. It documents the political and cultural transformations of China and Russia between 1976 and 2017, and the influence of ideology on the social landscape.

This year's Gallery Weekend still presents a large number of paintings, from German contemporary artist Franz Ackermann's abstract psychedelic style to Sun Daliang's surreal canvases that blend the erotic with the fairy-tale-like. Among them, the solo exhibition of cartoonist anusman at Tabula Rasa Gallery, *The Market: A Year of anusman* 《市井： anusman 的一年》, is especially lively.

The entire exhibition space is imagined as a marketplace, a street corner, a mahjong parlor; a recessed area is roped off as a ring-toss game ground, and in one corner lies a "happy-foot board"—"happy foot" meaning "happy to wash one's wife's feet"—encouraging visitors to try sliding on it. The walls are covered with the cartoonist anusman's (Wang Shuo's) musings and mutterings.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201903260020324eJC67RNmrH3k2bZ.jpg-w600)

> Every three days you need to choose toilet paper once. There are four kinds, at different prices; one kind is enough for daily use. I prefer the coreless roll because it doesn't take up space and the shipping cost shouldn't be high either. Mainly, though, it's cheaper.

> Everything on China United Airlines costs money. A blanket is 20 yuan and you can take it with you. The meals come in two tiers: 35-yuan curry chicken and 58-yuan braised chicken rice. The design is thoughtful. On the surface it looks like there's a choice, but the underlying message is that what's inside the 35-yuan meal is definitely worth only that much.

anusman previously published [the bestselling comic book *Mr. Door* 《门先生》](https://book.douban.com/subject/27118076/). His distinctive ink-wash style and rambling asides let viewers relax, easing the pressure to "interpret art"—especially amid the dense viewing experience of Gallery Weekend.

Whether the cartoon scenes are set in a train station, a vegetable market, a park, or a restaurant, they all depict the familiar "marketplace" world; the author is casual yet occasionally a little awkward in an endearing way, unafraid to lay bare his own small thoughts, "scribbling down some blunt truths."

During the first weekend of Gallery Weekend, the phrase heard most often from curators and artists was "they really pushed themselves." Compared with previous years, when galleries had simply coordinated their openings for the same period, the third Gallery Weekend Beijing had a longer preparation cycle, and most galleries mounted notably heavyweight shows. The international artists brought in by several non-profit institutions were all "A-list."

The Faurschou Foundation is presenting a solo exhibition by American visual artist Doug Aitken, known for large-scale digital-technology installations and multi-media applications; the show features three works, *The New Era* 《新纪元》, *Three Modern Figures (Don't Forget to Breathe)* 《三个现代人(不要忘记呼吸)》, and *Crossing Boundaries* 《越界》, with immersive audio-visual installations that overwhelm the senses. M Woods Museum is hosting a solo exhibition by minimalist artist Richard Tuttle, *Return* 《回赠》, with works in sculpture, easel oil painting, works on paper, and poetry. The Song Art Museum is presenting a solo exhibition by French-American artist Louise Bourgeois, *The Eternal Thread*; her expressive large-scale installations and sculptures create a world that is fantastical and bewildering, intimate and uncanny, earning her the nickname "spider-woman."

Long March Space and Galerie Urs Meile are respectively presenting solo painting exhibitions by Wu Shanzhuan and Wang Xingwei, important painters and conceptual artists who emerged after China's '85 New Wave. Both artists' works are saturated with political symbols and dense metaphorical imagery. From recognizable human faces to the big-character slogans of propaganda posters, their works are at once satirical and exaggerated, while also carrying traces of revolutionary-art style.

This year a New Forces Section has been specially established, aiming to fill the absence of young artists' voices that is often felt at large-scale art events.

The New Forces Section is presented as a group exhibition on the first floor of the 798 Art Center. Centered on the theme "Here Be Lions," it features nearly 40 works by 31 artists. The works vary widely in subject matter and medium, but like many group exhibitions, the proposition is grand, the interpretation opaque, and there is an unavoidable suspicion of patchwork.

The exhibition introduction explains that "hic sunt leones" ("here be lions") was originally a Latin phrase used by ancient sailors to mark unknown territories on maps—places where dangerous lions, dragons, sea serpents, and other beasts lurked. Borrowing this meaning to evoke a constantly changing and increasingly unfamiliar world, the works appear more like young artists' reactions to and reflections on the reality they inhabit, whether addressing immigrant communities, climate issues, political events, or labor relations under new modes of production...

From the enlightenment wave of Chinese contemporary art in the 1980s to the present, the works presented are mostly closely connected to reality. Another important '85 New Wave artist, Wang Huanqing, is holding a major solo exhibition at Hive Center for Contemporary Art spanning his work since 1982; a chronological narrative reveals the gradual abstraction and internalization of the artist's language.

In the preface to the second chapter he writes: "I don't want to pretend to be untouched by social, political, and other issues, but I don't want to pretend to be an expert and deal with them superficially either. I care more about my own sense of situation; that sense constantly stimulates me to feel social and political forms from the inside out."

### A Complete Exhibition Guide:

1. Doug Aitken

![**Venue: Faurschou Foundation**

**Dates: March 23 – August 4, 2019**](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201903252215469vyGR48cCztwrIND.jpeg-w600)

Through immersive video, sound, and sculptural installations, the exhibition reveals the strange facets of modern society and culture, exposing both the positive and negative sides of life in today's digital-internet state.

2. Richard Tuttle Solo Exhibition: *Return* 《回赠》

![**Venue: M Woods Museum**

**Dates: March 16 – June 16, 2019**](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201903252218020ZjsQNoWAwyl5PuI.jpg-w600)

Looking back across the artist's fifty-year career, more than 100 of Tuttle's best-known works will unfold in the gallery around symbols designed by the artist.

3. *Quasi-Nature—Bio Art, Boundaries and Laboratory* 《准自然 —— 生物艺术，边界与实验室》

![**Venue: Hyundai Motorstudio**
**Dates: March 22 – June 16, 2019**](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/2019032522203680Sut1DLCePR7cgJ.jpg-w600)

Young curator Wei Ying won the "Creativity" award at the second Hyundai Blue Prize for this exhibition. Centered on the prize's theme "Future Humanity," it presents and discusses, from a global perspective, the relationships between life and the planet, society and the environment, existence and extinction, in an accessible yet profound way. It also speculates on the various possibilities of art-and-technology integration in the future.

4. Wu Shanzhuan: *Today Became a Festival* 《今天后来成了节日》

![**Venue: Long March Space**
**Dates: March 16 – October 13, 2019**](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20190325222343WUc5G2N0RFQntPaB.jpg-w600)

This exhibition presents Wu Shanzhuan's two-dimensional paintings from the past thirty years—more than 200 works form the field of this "festival." An important figure in the '85 New Wave, Wu founded the "Red Humor" group in 1986 and the "International Red Humor Organization" in 1990, reflecting on political themes through humor and satire.

5. Wang Xingwei: *The Mystery of Countenance* 《面相之谜》

![**Venue: Galerie Urs Meile, 798 Art District**

![**Venue: Tang Contemporary Art**
**Dates: March 9 – April 28, 2019**](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20190325223044d1pyfbzgDkXjvimJ.jpeg-w600)

Chen Yujun's most important large-scale solo exhibition in the past decade, curated by Cui Cancan. The exhibition will be presented across both of Tang Contemporary Art's Beijing spaces. The first space explores the relationship between Chen's works and space, presenting the sources and trajectory of the artist's creative thinking; the second space will focus on his large-scale two-dimensional works, showing his advances in artistic language and ongoing experimentation.

7. André Butzer Solo Exhibition

This is Wang Huanqing's first retrospective, presented in five interconnected sections that encompass and distill more than three decades of the artist's paintings, video, and installation works.

His works take international travel, globalization, and urbanization as their themes, reflecting the social transformations and political issues brought about by global cultural homogenization.

20. Liu Shiyuan: *What Lies Beyond Words* 《言外之意》

![**Venue: White Space Beijing**
**Dates: March 9 – May 1, 2019**](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201903252337441Gnou3ypMh6XLSPR.jpeg-w600)

The exhibition presents the artist's newest video, installation, painting, and photography works, opening up reflections on a series of propositions concerning the boundaries and connections between the individual and the collective, their existence, rights, and value.

21. *The Mushroom at the End of the World*

![**Venue: Taikang Space**
**Dates: March 16 – May 19, 2019**](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20190325233916nzPfaRuDoXW93Ops.jpeg-w600)

The title is borrowed from anthropologist Anna Tsing's book *The Mushroom at the End of the World*, in which she examines the rich connections between the ancient species of the matsutake mushroom, which grows in perilous primeval forests, and the Anthropocene. Mushrooms have also become an inescapable narrative symbol in many primitive religions, shamanic practices, and Taoist legends. The exhibition attempts to connect the contemporary revelations of the Anthropocene with certain philosophical and spiritual contents derived from non-Western civilizations. It is built using installation, video, sculpture, and VR.
