### Industry news:

**[Yoox Net-A-Porter to delist on June 20](http://uk.fashionnetwork.com/news/3.html)**

Online luxury retailer Yoox Net-A-Porter will delist from the Milan Stock Exchange on June 20. In January this year, Cartier parent Richemont launched a bid for YNAP, planning to buy up all of its remaining shares; once the transaction is completed, Richemont's stake in YNAP will rise to 95%.

**[Global consumer demand for diamond jewelry hit a record $82 billion in 2017](http://uk.fashionnetwork.com/news/Global-diamond-jewellery-demand-reaches-record-82-billion-in-2017,978851.html#.Wv5UKFOFOgQ)**

According to a research report released last Thursday by De Beers, the diamond company under the Anglo American Group, global consumer demand for diamond jewelry reached $82 billion in 2017, up 2% from the previous year, setting a record high. The US market led growth for the fourth consecutive year: the data shows that strong consumer confidence and a positive macroeconomic environment drove US jewelry market demand up 4%, with total demand reaching $43 billion — more than half of global demand. The survey also found that self-purchases accounted for 30% of total diamond jewelry purchases in the US.

**[Global Industry Analysts says the global bridal wear market will exceed $80 billion](http://uk.fashionnetwork.com/news/Bridal-fashion-an-80-billion-business,978737.html#.Wv4_n1OFOgQ)**

According to estimates by Global Industry Analysts, by 2020 the global bridal wear market will exceed $80 billion. It is a highly profitable industry in which consumers are willing to pay more, but at the same time it is also highly complex, due to market fragmentation, the entry of fast fashion, declining marriage rates, and more. In addition, in order to attract more consumers, this tradition-rooted industry has also begun to wade into social media, making digital transformation one of its challenges.

**[Burberry reports fiscal-year results, sales up 2%](https://www.burberryplc.com/en/investors.html)**

Burberry released its results for fiscal year 2017–2018, showing steady momentum for the brand after a year of internal transformation. In the 12 months to March 31, Burberry's total revenue fell 1.2% to £2.73 billion. Excluding beauty products, its sales rose 2%, driven by retail revenue from existing stores, and its operating profit grew 4% to £410 million; adjusted operating profit rose 2% to £467 million. Burberry said the latter was helped by strong retail performance, nearly £44 million in incremental cost savings, and improved beauty profitability due to lower marketing and inventory costs. After announcing its results on Wednesday this week, the company's shares rose 4% to £18.75 per share.

**[Nike, H&M, Gap and Burberry join a sustainable fashion initiative](http://uk.fashionnetwork.com/news/Nike-H-M-and-Burberry-join-forces-for-sustainable-fashion,978370.html#.WvzlnFOFOgQ)**

Nike, H&M, Gap and Burberry signed on to an initiative called "Make Fashion Circular." Francois Souchet, the project's lead, said the aim is to make an economic system in which clothing is never treated as waste "an unstoppable force." Stella McCartney, a longtime champion of eco-friendly sustainability, joined the initiative last year.

**[Richemont launches an all-new watch brand](http://wwd.com/accessories-news/watches/richemont-launches-baume-its-first-new-watch-brand-1202673326/)**

Richemont has launched an all-new brand, Baume — the first watch brand this luxury group has ever introduced — whose predecessor, Baume & Mercier, was acquired by Richemont. The brand targets a new consumer group: customers seeking products that are luxurious yet environmentally friendly, not mass-produced, yet priced below the luxury point. Combining Swiss watchmaking with sustainable, eco-friendly materials, Baume will launch two watch models, The Iconic and The Custom; the former starts at $1,100, while the latter is priced between $560 and $630. In addition, Baume has partnered with the NGO Waste Free Oceans, which will use ocean-recovered plastic to make watch components and parts.

**[L'Oréal Group to launch its first employee share ownership plan](http://wwd.com/business-news/financial/loreal-launches-first-employee-share-ownership-plan-1202673478/)**

On Tuesday this week, L'Oréal Group announced it will launch its first employee share ownership plan in 52 countries. Under the plan, employees can purchase 500,000 shares at a certain discount; the subscription period runs from June 4 to June 18, with settlement on July 24. The shares will be traded on Euronext Paris. In addition, employees must hold the shares for at least five years, except in special circumstances.

[**Pandora reports first-quarter results, sales down 1%**](http://investor.pandoragroup.com/releasedetail.cfm?ReleaseID=1067417)

Danish jewelry and accessories brand Pandora released its first-quarter results. The data shows that total sales for the first three months reached $819 million, and that Pandora's performance declined 1%, hit by slowing sales growth in China. By regional market, Pandora's sales revenue in Asia-Pacific fell 9% (up 1% in local currencies), US sales fell 16% (down 4% in local currencies), while Europe rose 15% (up 16% in local currencies). To improve performance in China, the group said it is implementing a commercial plan to strengthen its China business. It also said the group will increase jewelry and accessory product types beyond bracelets, such as necklaces and rings, striving to reach an equivalent sales level by 2022.

**[Yoox Net-A-Porter's first-quarter revenue rises](http://cdn3.yoox.biz/cloud/ynap/uploads/doc/2018/PR_YNAP_1Q2017_Trading_Statement_14_May_2018_ENG_vFinal.pdf)**

Luxury fashion e-commerce group Yoox Net-A-Porter released its first-quarter results. In the three months to March 31, revenue rose 0.5% to €518 million; at constant exchange rates, sales grew 7.9%. Within that, the in-season brand line — the combined net revenue of the Net-A-Porter and Mr Porter e-commerce platforms — grew 5.9% to €282 million, while the off-season brand line, comprising Yoox and The Outnet, fell 4.6%. By region, YNAP's sales in the UK grew 3.2% to €68.8 million (revenue up 5.9%); North America revenue grew 5.1% to €153 million (sales up 8.5%); and Asia-Pacific, pulled along by the Hong Kong and Japan markets, posted positive results, with revenue up 2.5% to €93.4 million. The group plans to invest €170 million to €180 million this year, mainly in technology, as well as to improve free cash flow compared with 2017 and expand the business.

**[Vetements to return to fashion week after a year's absence](http://wwd.com/fashion-news/fashion-scoops/vetements-reverts-to-couture-week-showing-1202671813/)**

Vetements, which announced last June that it would no longer hold traditional runway shows, has now decided to return to fashion week. According to reports, Vetements will present its Spring 2019 men's and women's collections on the afternoon of July 1, while this year's Fall 2018 Paris fashion week will run from July 1 to July 5.

**[Burberry acquires Italian leather supplier CF&P](http://uk.fashionnetwork.com/news/Burberry-buys-key-Italian-leathergoods-supplier-from-CF-P,977157.html#.Wvme19OFP-Y)**

British luxury brand Burberry announced it is acquiring a luxury leather business from CF&P, its longtime partner and an Italian leather supplier. Marco Gobbetti, Burberry's CEO, said, "This acquisition will create a center of excellence for Burberry, covering all activities from creativity and product innovation to engineering and coordinated production. It will give us greater control over the quality, cost, logistics and sustainability of our leather products." Notably, in recent years more and more luxury brands, such as Chanel and Hermès, have adopted a model of directly controlling their key suppliers, so some consider Burberry's move unsurprising.

**[LVMH Group leads a new funding round for fashion site Lyst](https://news.sky.com/story/christian-dior-owner-lvmh-backs-online-fashion-giant-lyst-11370456)**

According to Sky News, French luxury giant LVMH Group has become the largest investor in a new funding round for fashion site Lyst. Founded in 2010, Lyst is a fashion aggregation site that is both an e-commerce platform competing with Farfetch and Net-a-Porter and a fashion information platform, with nearly 70 million users each year. At present, the specific deal details remain unclear, but sources say Lyst's funding amount is between £50 million and £100 million — a large premium over its valuation in the previous round. Reports also say the deal was led by Groupe Arnault, one of LVMH's shareholders. On this, some believe the LVMH Group is once again taking action to advance its digital innovation program.

### Some opinions:

**[A report on luxury counterfeiting says online sales of fakes have cost luxury brands "dearly"](http://uk.fashionnetwork.com/news/Luxury-brands-lose-30-3-billion-due-to-online-counterfeiting-in-2017,977978.html#.WvzruFOFOgQ)**

![Photo: fashionnetwork](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180521112138xR3PADtYnuUKz1cQ.jpg-w600)

According to a survey report on luxury counterfeiting, total online sales of counterfeit goods reached $30.3 billion, and counterfeiting caused the high-end consumer goods industry $98 billion in losses. Across all industries, total counterfeiting losses in 2017 came to about $1.2 trillion, and are expected to rise to $1.82 trillion by 2020; more specifically, losses from online counterfeiting sales last year came to $323 billion.

The survey focused in particular on economies with high consumer purchasing power, such as France, Germany, China, Japan, the UAE, the UK and the US.

The report also examined the relationship between counterfeit goods and social media, and found that 3D printing poses a potential threat.

"Global trade growth and technological development have had a positive impact on markets around the world, but at the same time they have also brought downsides," the report writes. "Companies spend large amounts of money, time and resources protecting their brands and trademarks. But even with these measures in place, the counterfeit goods market is still growing rapidly."

**[When companies worldwide risk boycotts over political missteps, why does "Kaiser Karl" Lagerfeld seem immune — what makes him so special?](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/05/17/fashion/karl-lagerfeld-renounce-german-citizenship.html)**

Karl Lagerfeld has always spoken bluntly in front of the media and the public, holding nothing back even on sensitive topics. He has publicly expressed his distaste for the #MeToo movement, snapped that models who don't want their clothes pulled off should become nuns, and just recently boldly criticized German Chancellor Angela Merkel's immigration policy, quipping "was it really necessary?"

What's interesting is that no matter how indiscreetly Lagerfeld speaks, it never seems to affect the brands he leads — Chanel and Fendi — much, and any effect is barely noticeable. By contrast, Kanye West, who made his "slavery" remarks, faced a strong boycott from netizens, and the Yeezy shoes he has long made with Adidas faced a serious PR crisis; Ivanka Trump's brand was also boycotted over Donald Trump's earlier sexist remarks, and netizens even launched the hashtag campaign #GrabYourWallet.

On this question, an article published in The New York Times — by author Vanessa Friedman — points out that Karl Lagerfeld unquestionably occupies a unique space in the fashion world. One could say he shaped fashion and is a legend, and this tolerance comes with tolerance for a certain quality: people fear criticizing an authoritative figure, especially the man in charge of Chanel — a brand enshrined at the pinnacle of fashion power.

The article notes that even those who expressed anger did not dare to say so publicly. "Please don't quote me — I don't want to lose my fifth-row seat at Chanel" — such a response illustrates why.

The author argues, "Lagerfeld has been saying outrageous things for so long that people have grown numb to it. It's almost expected. He has positioned himself as a provocateur, and that is part of his brand." She also points out that before we can even begin to digest one of a person's statements, he is already starting to say something else, and the sheer volume of it is enough to sweep away the previous chatter — in effect, a distraction.

"We seem to live in a strange double reality. Just as we have become more sensitive to the experiences of different social groups, we have also grown more accustomed to the rise of uncivil discourse, wherever it comes from."
