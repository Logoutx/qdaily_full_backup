Three years and more than 5 billion yuan invested — how did Evergrande Spring end up where it is today?

"We will spare no expense to build Evergrande Spring mineral water into a great FMCG brand. Success is the only option; failure is not."

Xu Jiayin, chairman of Evergrande Group, was once full of ambition. He did indeed deliver on "sparing no expense" — but the upshot is that Evergrande will no longer have an FMCG brand at all.

On September 28, Evergrande Group, listed in Hong Kong, announced that in order to better develop its core real estate business, it would divest three units — mineral water, dairy, and grain and oil — for a combined consideration of 2.7 billion yuan. Three Shenzhen-based companies will take over these businesses, along with the debt they carry.

If the deal is ultimately completed, Evergrande expects to book a gain of 5.7 billion yuan and revert to being a property company whose performance growth depends mainly on selling homes.

Over the past few years, Evergrande put forward a strategy of "diversified development" and successively spun off quite a few side businesses, including sports, culture, and FMCG. Within the Evergrande Group today there are at least 16 publicly listed companies and 2 companies listed on the NEEQ (China's over-the-counter market). According to Xu Jiayin's earlier thinking, these "side businesses" — mineral water, dairy, and grain and oil — should each go public in Hong Kong as well.

![Xu Jiayin at Evergrande Spring's national distributors' conference](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160929230907ViDjWmd26ap5S0Fn.jpg-w600)

And now? Cutting losses as quickly as possible is probably what matters most. Take the mineral water business: since the second half of 2015, its results have no longer been disclosed separately in the group's financial reports, but the disclosed figures from the first three years alone add up to losses approaching 4 billion yuan. That does not even count the 5.54 billion yuan spent earlier on building factories, setting up sales channels, and advertising.

Xu Jiayin may also have realized that focusing single-mindedly on selling homes is now the best way for Evergrande's market value to rise quickly. In the first half of this year, the group's property business sold 140 billion yuan worth of homes, and it has since raised its full-year sales target to 300 billion yuan — putting it on par with industry leader Vanke.

This property tycoon, so adept at capital operations — what made him, in a moment of impulse, charge into the FMCG arena he knew nothing about? And why did it take him so long to finally confirm that he should pull out of this market?

Let's start with that bottle of "Evergrande Spring."

### Shouldn't all those Evergrande football fans and homeowners be consumers of "Evergrande Spring"?

The debut of "Evergrande Spring" back then was, to put it mildly, nothing short of spectacular.

At the AFC Champions League final on November 9, 2013, the prime advertising space on the chest of the Evergrande football team's jerseys changed overnight from Dongfeng Nissan to "Evergrande Spring"; during breaks in the televised match, 5-second "Evergrande Spring" ads aired repeatedly; and when Guangzhou Evergrande ultimately lifted the trophy, the backdrop of the victory celebration still bore the "Evergrande Spring" logo.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160929231115oKNvstglMz80Jkyn.jpg-w600)

The next day, the "Evergrande Spring" product line was officially unveiled to the public: a water source located in the Changbai Mountains, positioned for "affluent urban consumers," with the 350-milliliter bottle priced at 3.8 yuan.

According to [a report in *Time Weekly*](http://news.ifeng.com/shendu/sdzb/detail_2013_11/28/31630381_0.shtml), Xu Jiayin first proposed the idea of developing a mineral water business in April 2013, when he happened to meet Bayin Chaolu, governor of Jilin province. From the two sides' first meeting to the product's official launch, Evergrande took only about half a year to complete a series of preliminary steps — securing the water source, acquiring a local water plant, assembling an operations team, and designing the brand — and ultimately caught the perfect exposure opportunity of the AFC Champions League final.

It wasn't just mineral water. From dairy and grain and oil to later forays into finance, the internet, and healthcare, Evergrande's diversification proceeded at almost exactly this pace over the past three years. As expected, all these businesses were supposed to become new revenue sources for Evergrande, supporting a real estate business with an enormous appetite for capital.

"Diversified development is absolutely not a stopgap measure for us, but a great strategy with long-term, holistic, and fundamental significance. The new industries and new projects we enter must eventually reach annual sales of tens of billions, even hundreds of billions of yuan. Only in this way does our diversification have meaning and achieve the true purpose of strategic development," [Xu Jiayin once said.](http://www.huaxia.com/tslj/rdrw/2015/05/4418382.html)

This meant that every emerging business Evergrande selected had to be backed by a sufficiently large market capacity, and had to possess sales capability close to — or even greater than — Evergrande's real estate business. That sounded like a fairy tale.

For example, the sales targets Xu Jiayin first set for Evergrande Spring were 10 billion yuan in sales in 2014 and 30 billion yuan in 2016 — equivalent to building, alongside China's bottled mineral water market at the time, a brand-new market of the same size.

In a previous series of special reports on the FMCG industry, we discussed just how turbulent a period China's FMCG market is currently going through: the internet [destroyed sales systems that brands had built over decades](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/29402.html) practically overnight; big brands lost control over their distributors, who in turn [no longer bothered to push products hard](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/29590.html), and so on. For FMCG products, channels — that is, getting products onto shelves — are key.

But Evergrande's public position was this: the Evergrande brand itself could create a "third channel" beyond traditional supermarkets and e-commerce.

Of course, Xu Jiayin never used the phrase "third channel" himself. But in his vision, they could use Evergrande's sales offices, already spread across the country, as direct-sales outlets; the mineral water business could also be combined with Evergrande's own real estate business, as part of community support services, with water delivered straight to residents' doors.

![A scene commonly seen at Evergrande sales offices around the country](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160929232443otX4J8FOYmLPe2SA.jpg-w600)

In other words, Evergrande's property developments should have been an ideal channel for reaching end consumers, and those Evergrande "homeowners" should, in theory, have been consumers of "Evergrande Spring."

They set their sights even more on Evergrande football fans. Evergrande Football should be the most compelling part of the Evergrande brand right now. Thanks to the team's strong results in recent years, a cohort of loyal fans has become willing to spend money on all kinds of peripheral merchandise.

If the team's jerseys could be sold, then why not bundle or directly sell mineral water as well?

Actually, Evergrande could have tried some simpler and faster ways to make money, such as brand licensing or contract manufacturing. But Evergrande insisted instead on building the entire system from the ground up, and — using its original real estate sales team, plus staff poached from other premium water companies such as Evian, 5100, and Kunlun Mountain — it rapidly built a nationwide operations team of more than 1,000 people across 31 regions.

A more plausible explanation, one might guess, is that Xu Jiayin was quite confident in what he regarded as Evergrande Spring's "channels" and "consumers."

### The legendary 5-plus billion yuan — how exactly was it spent?

Like nearly all premium waters on the market today, Evergrande Spring's advertising emphasized the quality of the water itself: "What we transport is not surface water, but 30-million-year-old volcanic mineral spring water from the deep forests of the Changbai Mountains."

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160929231452DGORdcTNejComJvB.jpg-w600)

But in reality, the Changbai Mountains are a recognized premium water source, and the factory-building and extraction costs there had long been driven down to fairly low levels by the major brands that had already set up shop. *Time Weekly* once [quoted industry insiders](http://news.ifeng.com/shendu/sdzb/detail_2013_11/28/31630381_0.shtml) as saying that building a water plant there with an annual output of 100,000 tons would require total investment of around 100 million yuan, factoring in land, equipment, and working capital. Evergrande operated by acquiring water plants and then upgrading their production lines; total investment in its two larger plants came to only 200 to 300 million yuan.

Much more money went into all kinds of advertising. According to Xu Jiayin himself at the 2013 "Evergrande Spring" national distributors' conference, in order to achieve the goal of "a household name," Evergrande poured 1.3 billion yuan into the brand's promotional blitz during the launch period alone, from November 9 to November 30 that year. "When Evergrande advertises, money is the last thing we think about."

In terms of promotion strategy, Evergrande Spring also followed the conventional FMCG playbook, with TV ads accounting for 70% as the absolute mainstay. And within the first half of 2014, it successively signed four brand ambassadors: Jackie Chan, Fan Bingbing, Kim Soo-hyun, and Jun Ji-hyun.

A massive advertising blitz, coupled with big-name celebrity endorsements, was one of Evergrande's go-to marketing tactics in its real estate business. Combined with the lure of low-price promotions, it had rapidly lifted sales at many property projects around the country and triggered buying frenzies.

![When Evergrande's Venice hotel project opened, Fan Bingbing — an Evergrande Spring ambassador — appeared on site](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160929232738chCP7XDkEjIawZbN.jpg-w600)

But an FMCG product like mineral water ultimately has to reach consumers through all kinds of channels before a purchase happens. And the "direct-sales outlets" and "consumers" Xu Jiayin imagined were, in the end, only a small part of that system. This also meant Evergrande ultimately had to take the traditional FMCG path — massive stocking of shelves, building out channels.

Although Evergrande had never touched this industry before, it was clearly willing to spend on channel building in its early days:

On one hand, it poached people from other FMCG companies and built teams with salaries two to three times industry norms. When the project launched, many in the company were former employees of mineral water companies such as Wahaha, C'estbon, and Master Kong. Zhang Hua, Evergrande Spring's marketing director, came from Nongfu Spring.

On the other hand, soon after the brand advertising went out, Evergrande quickly convened a national distributors' conference and subcontracted the sales tasks. According to figures Evergrande previously disclosed, within 30 days Evergrande Spring achieved average daily orders of 200 million yuan, and the total value of sales signed with distributors nationwide exceeded 5.7 billion yuan — a solid opening.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160929232822ASf5ZBCipMsVbQRo.jpg-w600)

And at the distributor-stocking stage, Evergrande also left generous margins. A distributor who once handled the Evergrande Spring market in Harbin [wrote on Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/question/24115233): for a bottle of Evergrande Spring retailing at 3.5 yuan, the distributor's purchase price was 2.68 yuan; the company could offer employees handling terminal stocking a base salary of more than 4,000 yuan after tax, visiting 30 stores a day; it paid for free display stacks in supermarkets, made free signage for small shops, and still had a sizable budget for getting into stores — "it's hard not to get products onto shelves."

But in reality, as of May 2015, Evergrande Spring's total sales revenue was only 1.287 billion yuan. Compared with the 5.54 billion yuan of upfront investment, that meant for every 3 yuan spent, only 1 yuan came back.

### "Evergrande Spring" actually entered a market with real potential — it even got something of a head start

In fact, the timing of Evergrande Spring's entry was a good one.

Around 2013, China's drinking water market was indeed in an upward phase: AC Nielsen survey data showed that the average growth rate of China's drinking water market reached 32% in 2014, with the low-end water market growing at 11%–12%, while premium water grew at 46%–50%. And under the health-conscious consumption wave of those two years, bottled water was also a category growing faster than carbonated soft drinks.

In the long run, Evergrande Spring entered a market with growth potential. More crucially, Evergrande Spring entered a relatively blank market segment.

In the bottled water market at the time, there were either purified or spring water brands such as China Resources C'estbon, Nongfu Spring, and Wahaha, mostly priced below 2 yuan; or, starting with Kunlun Mountain at around 5 yuan, 5100 Tibet Glacier Mineral Water, which retailed at 8 yuan, while imported water brands such as Evian and VOSS sat at the very tip of the price pyramid, affordable to only a tiny minority.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201609292334038hOEmqrAf7SwdxMu.jpg-w600)

In the 2–5 yuan price band where Evergrande Spring sat, the main competitors at the time were only two brands: Ganten, retailing at 2.5 yuan, and Kunlun Mountain, at 4.8 yuan. In today's popular parlance, this was demand that satisfied "consumption upgrade."

And although Baisuishan and Kunlun Mountain (the two of them entered at roughly the same time) both entered the market a bit earlier than Evergrande Spring, before Evergrande Spring started pouring money into advertising, ads for Baisuishan and Kunlun Mountain were rarely seen. The exact timing is now hard to verify, but what can be confirmed is that Evergrande not only bought Evergrande Spring a loud bang with its money, but also brought greater attention to the market as a whole.

A year later, this price band suddenly gained products such as 5100, Arxan, Water Cube, and Wahaha oxygenated water, all equally unsparing in advertising. So much so that when Nongfu Spring later developed three premium drinking water products, it had to put real effort into design.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160929233136tl31XM0AvrmDPoNW.jpeg-w600)

As competition intensified, price wars became the most direct way to fight for market share. In 2015, Evergrande Spring announced price cuts nationwide, uniformly reducing both the 350-milliliter and 500-milliliter bottles to 2.5 yuan.

This meant Evergrande Spring also had to compete with the very brands it had originally avoided — Wahaha, Nestlé, Nongfu Spring — each of which had deeper brand equity and more loyal consumers than Evergrande Spring. And consumers of "Evergrande Spring" now had a reason to defect.

On top of that, to this day Evergrande Spring has only one water source, in the Changbai Mountains, with limited capacity. Shipping water bottled there to cities across the country carries very high logistics costs. Industry insiders say that assuming sales in the Shanghai area, the ideal price point would need to be above 4 yuan. But Evergrande Spring's brand premium clearly hasn't been established yet.

### The market positioning wasn't wrong, the timing wasn't wrong — but selling water and selling homes are ultimately two different things

Bulky and heavy — if you talk to people in the FMCG industry, most of them will probably tell you that water may be the most exhausting category to build. In fact, the entire FMCG industry is a thin-margin business. The two most critical things — marketing and channels — one continuously creates impressions for consumers' impulse-purchase decisions, while the other makes sure the product is "within arm's reach."

Evergrande Spring looked capable of both. But the problem is that, with thin margins, scale is ultimately the key to profitability for FMCG. The standard practice is to have a strong product portfolio — to sell as many products as possible through a single channel, like industry leader P&G. And if you just look at beverages, Coca-Cola has more than 20 products worldwide with annual sales exceeding US$100 million each; Wahaha and Nongfu Spring are both adding more products as well. Behind all of this sits a well-developed system as support — and you can see they have all been cultivating it for many years.

Now back to Evergrande's core — and most profitable — business: real estate. The essence of this business lies in acquiring land; the so-called competition mechanism and market don't actually happen at the end consumer. Every wave of rising home prices is also intimately tied to policy.

In the first half of this year, Evergrande spent nearly 80 billion yuan to acquire 80 land projects around the country — the most in the industry. Of these, 84% of the land was located in first- and second-tier cities.

After shifting its focus back to first- and second-tier cities, Evergrande's sales performance in the first half also rose to a high level: of its 141.78 billion yuan in sales, more than half came from second-tier cities; the company's total assets are now close to 1 trillion yuan, the largest in the country.

Against this backdrop, an FMCG business worth less than 3 billion yuan in total looks all the more dispensable. And shedding this burden amid such a booming housing market this year makes it all the more logical.

Three years in, Evergrande Spring can be summed up as a "phenomenal" event — to some extent, it was a "breakout hit." If Xu Jiayin's 5-plus billion yuan of wasted money brought any lesson to business, one of them is probably this: don't assume that just because you have "users," you can sell them anything. Whatever the barriers to entry, every industry has its own moats.
