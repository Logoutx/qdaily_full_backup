Taco Bell Has Gone Trendy in China — What Is This "Fast Casual" Dining Trend?

@@QD_BODY@@
It's a subtle balance struck between people's demands for efficiency and health, between wanting a quick bite and wanting a place to sit for a little while.

Purple neon lights, graffiti covering whole walls, a dim interior even in daylight, a cocktail bar and high stools, Taylor Swift's new album playing on loop, plus the theme song "Remember Me" from *Coco*... All of this makes the Taco Bell at Feng Sheng Li in Shanghai look more like an American gastropub than a fast-food joint.

The restaurant has been open for less than two months, yet Huang Hongqiang is already eating here for the fourth time. "When I just want to fill my stomach these days, I look for somewhere relaxed, where I can listen to music and sit for a while," he said. On the evening of March 8, he arrived with two colleagues before eight and stayed until nearly ten, ordering two rounds of food for a total spend of 190 yuan.

![Taco Bell at Feng Sheng Li (Image source: Yum China)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/2018031316081863px9XMvJmQzDjW2.GIF-w600Gif)

Few people probably know that Taco Bell, known for its burritos, belongs to Yum China — the same company as the KFC diagonally across the Wujiang Road intersection and Pizza Hut. Yum! Brands completed the spinoff of its China business in 2016, and Yum China went public on the New York Stock Exchange on November 1, 2016.

According to the 2018 edition of the Top 500 Chain Restaurants report, just released by research firm Technomic, [Taco Bell overtook Burger King to become the No. 4 fast-food brand in the US](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/50505.html), behind McDonald's, Starbucks and Subway. With more than 6,200 stores in the US, Taco Bell is, after all, a fast-food brand.

![New items at Taco Bell (Image source: Yum China)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180313161324Dovfr1ZiOTdI6j0B.png-w600)

[Taco Bell first entered China back in 2003 (when it was still called Ta Ke Zhong)](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/35649.html), opening three stores in Shenzhen and Shanghai positioned as casual Western restaurants in the vein of Pizza Hut; it later pulled out in 2008 due to poor management.

Now returning to the Chinese market a decade later, Taco Bell's brand image and positioning look completely different from both the US market and its former Chinese incarnation. Of course, it is also facing a completely different market.

The consumption upgrade of the fast-food industry has become an increasingly visible trend, in both the US and China — though the US went down this road earlier and is further along. Beyond the self-renewal of traditional fast-food chains like McDonald's, KFC, Burger King and Taco Bell in their stores and ingredients, the brands that better represent this wave are the so-called "fast casual" restaurant brands.

In the US, the Mexican-style Chipotle, burger brand Shake Shack and salad brand Sweetgreen all fall into this category. These restaurants sit between fast food and casual dining: they lead with "health" or "quality," use fewer heavily processed semi-finished products or additives than traditional fast food — and, of course, charge higher prices. Serving speed is often on par with fast food (sometimes slightly slower, depending on the preparation process), and, importantly, they want customers to sit in the store a little longer.

In other words, the so-called fast casual restaurant strikes a subtle balance between people's demands for efficiency and health, between wanting a quick bite and wanting a place to sit for a little while.

McDonald's had been losing market share in the US, and the customers it lost, McDonald's said, were mostly poached by fast casual restaurants. And the moves McDonald's is rolling out globally — burgers made with fresh beef instead of frozen, table service, and so on — are in fact converging on the fast casual model. Seen in that light, Taco Bell's current positioning in China fits this concept perfectly, though the term "fast casual" never caught on in China and gets lumped instead into the broader discussion of fast-food consumption upgrades.

According to Meituan's 2016 *China Restaurant Report*, average spending per table in the fast-food and snack category rose from 42 yuan in 2012 to 63 yuan last year, a cumulative increase of 49%. We have covered in detail the consumption upgrades of [milk tea](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/37616.html) and [street snacks](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/43804.html); now it seems the fast-food consumption upgrade has taken another form.

![Taco Bell at Wujiaochang](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180313160917mfuBPTnOrHdqRM1e.png-w600)

![Taco Bell at Wujiaochang](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180313160920BhFZV3LHg9tAjoK1.jpg-w600)

In a little over a year, Taco Bell has opened three stores in Shanghai, and a look at the locations tells you exactly which consumer demographic it is targeting.

The first store opened in Lujiazui, an area with hordes of tourists and close to the headquarters of major banks, financial institutions and corporations. The second sits in the Wanda Plaza at Wujiaochang, which besides the Knowledge and Innovation Community (KIC) — home to many tech companies and startups — also covers the students of Fudan, Tongji and Shanghai University of Finance and Economics. The Wanda Plaza there had also just been through a brand refresh, with sports brands replacing gold-jewelry shops along the street, making it younger. Taco Bell was one of the newly unveiled stores, opposite a Line store, as well as Taoyuan Village and Blue Frog.

The third store is in Feng Sheng Li, which opened in 2017 and is a textbook case of gentrification: the former shikumen residential buildings were reappraised and sold off, and the lower-middle-income neighborhood was remade to suit an emerging affluent crowd. Besides Taco Bell, the restaurants that moved in include the craft-beer bar Goose Island and boutique burger White Castle — mostly Western food — with the stated ambition of becoming "the next Xintiandi."

In Yum's official formulation, the "international community" is the single most important site-selection criterion; these people have solid spending power and willingness, and will pay for better food and a better experience.

Households and children — the demographic traditional fast-food brands prize — are shut out: Taco Bell offers no kids' meals. [It dropped kids' meals and toys in the US in 2013](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/06/06/business/exception-to-taco-bell-shift-on-artificial-ingredients.html), and targets only young people.

Huang Hongqiang's experience confirms this. "You almost never see anyone over forty here," he said. "I'm 35, and I'm on the older side in this store. At its peak, from seven to eight in the evening, all you see are good-looking young men and women." Huang works on Nanjing West Road, in the finance industry.

"The food here looks like 'the next level,'" said Lindsay, a regular at Taco Bell in the US before coming to China a year ago. "Taco Bell in the US doesn't have an open kitchen — it's usually a drive-through, you get your food from the window." But that is changing: in November 2016, Taco Bell [opened a flagship Taco Bell Cantina in Las Vegas](http://www.businessinsider.com/taco-bell-is-now-offering-full-service-weddings-in-vegas-2017-2), with trendy decor, an open kitchen, and even alcohol and a resident DJ — it looks more like a bar.

Yum China has brought Taco Bell's most fashionable format to the Chinese market. In the US, Taco Bell pushes the $1 value menu and its average check is not much different from fast food like KFC or McDonald's; in China it looks more modern and more upscale.

The store design also caters to the current "photo-friendly" ([Instagrammable](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/39394.html)) trend. The Lujiazui store has surfboards with California motifs hanging from the ceiling; both stores feature large graffiti walls; and the sprawling neon decorations are interior elements rarely used in traditional fast-food outlets — all in all, it creates a hip, of-the-moment vibe.

Right inside the door you can see the open kitchen. At KFC you can also glimpse part of the back kitchen behind the ordering counter (in fast food, the kitchen is usually placed behind the counter to speed up service), but Taco Bell is clearly different in its layout: all three stores lay the kitchen out flat on view to customers, rather than stacking it vertically behind the front counter.

![The open kitchen (Photo: Zhifei Dong)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180313161158zifOu7lB8qQ4ehPc.JPG-w600)

![Cocktail slushies (Image source: Yum China)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180313160634PyQNHRdusUJDFtxi.png-w600)

If you order one of its newly launched grilled-to-order dinner items, the staff will warn you that "it may take fifteen minutes." More than one consumer we spoke to mentioned that Taco Bell is slow to serve.

That is deliberate — it wants you to sit down. This also shows in the drinks menu: Taco Bell offers 28 beverage options in total. By comparison, KFC offers 19, even after recently adding many coffee options. Taco Bell serves beer and five cocktail slushies, and the Feng Sheng Li store even sells Goose Island craft beer. Carbonated drinks (Pepsi, 7-Up and Mirinda) come with free refills. All of this says the restaurant wants you to linger rather than chase table turnover the way KFC does.

And only once you sit down is it possible to sell you more. Like Huang Hongqiang and his colleagues, over two hours you keep ordering snacks or drinks. "Look around — the people nearby aren't eating, they're chatting. If you just want to eat, you go to KFC or McDonald's and don't stay long, but young people feel there's no reason to hang around there."

Both the Wujiaochang and Feng Sheng Li stores have carved out a small area for live performances, with resident singers on weekend slots.

![Weekend performance at the Taco Bell Feng Sheng Li store (Photo: Zhifei Dong)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180313160041W5MBqHybpFXATceE.JPG-w600)

Pizza Hut's appeal to young people is fading. "Consumers feel that if you saw the Pizza Hut brand as a person, he'd be a 'warm guy' or a 'backup,'" said Yum China CEO Joey Wat. Yum China urgently needs a new restaurant brand that can represent a fashionable, casual lifestyle.

Pizza Hut has been underperforming in major markets like the US and China for some time. [Its same-store sales in China have been falling since 2011](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/22617.html) (with signs of improvement last year, when same-store sales rose 1%), and [analysts once suggested Yum sell Pizza Hut](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/02/09/yum-brands-posts-weak-sales-as-pizza-hut-continues-to-struggle.html). If it cannot reinvigorate Pizza Hut, Yum needs to ready its next strong brand. Taco Bell, which has done a good job of courting young people in its home US market over the past six years, might be a good candidate.

Compared with KFC and Pizza Hut, Taco Bell has long done little internationally. Now Yum plans to grow its 280 overseas stores to 1,000 by 2020, with sales reaching $15 billion.

Yum says the reason it didn't expand before was that fried chicken and pizza are far better known globally than Mexican food, but "now, outside the US, people in other countries are getting more and more familiar with Mexican food."

As an emerging brand with only three stores in Shanghai, the reason Taco Bell caught our attention is that under Yum China's capital and group-level operations it could well scale up and change the landscape of the fast-food industry. Taco Bell is in an emerging market — but it is also an increasingly competitive one.

Beyond the likes of White Castle and [boutique burger brands like Shake Shack](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/44350.html), which is about to enter the Chinese market, self-renewing and upgrading traditional fast-food chains are its competitors too.

Ting Hsin, the parent company of Dicos — which has focused on second- and third-tier cities — is also eyeing the fast casual market. It [launched the pizza brand Nabulesi in 2016](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/29967.html) and has so far opened four stores in Shanghai. Customers can pick their own pizza toppings, the kitchen is semi-transparent, and the oven is prominently on display. The average check is around 60 yuan. Wang Yiwen, a spokesperson for Ting Hsin's restaurant business, once mentioned that they had studied restaurant brands such as Chipotle and Shake Shack.

![Nabulesi's build-your-own pizza (Image source: Nabulesi)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180313160703YSJu71HQCm6kVXfG.jpg-w600)

Burger King is another rival not to be underestimated. Compared with KFC and McDonald's, its positioning is very close to Taco Bell's. After changing operators around 2012 and sharpening its positioning, Burger King has aimed at the more individualistic young people of big cities, with a higher average check than KFC or McDonald's.

As early as 2015, Burger King launched its Premium line of burgers, priced at 34–41 yuan each, with combo meals reaching 48–55 yuan. When McDonald's average check was 23.5 yuan, Burger King's had already reached 33 yuan.

Possibly thanks to its upgraded positioning, Burger King has done well in China, [growing at double digits for the past three years.](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2017-11-13/turkish-burger-king-operator-tfi-files-for-u-s-initial-offering) That is a fairly strong pace for a brand that has been in China for more than a decade. By comparison, KFC in China over the same period was declining or posting same-store growth of 3–4%.

![The newly launched Angus beef line belongs to Burger King's premium range (Image source: Burger King Weibo)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180313155458pm0odwakXQWMiCgy.jpg-w600)

Yum's arch-rival McDonald's has also been running fast-food-upgrade experiments over the past two years.

In the second half of 2015, McDonald's build-your-own-burger outlet Create Your Taste [caused a stir in both Shanghai and Guangzhou](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/11460.html). These made-to-order burgers started at 42 yuan a la carte, with combos starting at 49 yuan. At the time, many consumers jokingly called it "the Subway of burgers."

Although the project was quietly killed a little over a year later, McDonald's did not give up on moving upmarket. It soon launched the Chef series of burgers developed in collaboration with Michelin-starred chefs. In price and positioning, the Chef line was a compromise between Create Your Taste and ordinary low-priced burgers: 28 yuan per burger (one tier above the 20-yuan Big Mac), with combos at 33 yuan. Also made to order.

![McDonald's latest Chef series, launched last September (Image source: McDonald's)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20180313155438W18hRiq62cSXHyGj.jpg-w600)

Recently, McDonald's "restaurant of the future" in Guangzhou rolled out DIY desserts — sundaes, waffle cones and affogato can all be customized. Beyond that, there are hardware upgrades: McDonald's announced it will add more "2.0 restaurants," which offer table service, self-order touch screens and mobile payment, among other things.

Nor can we say that low-priced fast food and fast casual don't compete. [Nick Setya, an analyst at Wedbush Securities, said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2017-09-26/shake-shack-suffers-as-upscale-burger-industry-gets-more-crowded) fast casual restaurants are caught in a pincer between fast food and casual dining, "pressured from both sides." In the US market, after a stretch of decline, fast food has begun fighting back against fast casual.

Because of the enthusiastic market response, McDonald's rolled out $1, $2 and $3 menus across the US. Its rivals, Wendy's and Burger King, also launched various low-priced combos to win back price-sensitive consumers.

All of these are problems Taco Bell may run into as it expands later. There's also flavor localization: Yum has already adapted the Mexican flatbread for the Chinese market — swapping the cold cheese inside for melted hot cheese — but some consumers still find it a cold dish and "don't like it." And the problems fast casual faces in the US market — how to balance store count against oversaturating the market and losing freshness and appeal — are ones that restaurants including Taco Bell will all have to solve.

"Consumption upgrade is the most important trend," Yum China CEO Joey Wat said at the 2017 investor conference. "Consumers want better and more."

Whatever becomes of this brand in China going forward, fast casual — this emerging segment of demand — has already begun.
