If you don’t have the time or the inclination to brave this “lively and crowded” design fair, take a look at its highlights instead.

On the second day of Design Shanghai, a long queue had already formed at the entrance of the Shanghai Exhibition Center; opening-day attendance reached 10,237. The first two days of the fair were trade visitor days, open exclusively to industry professionals and media, with the general public and students admitted only on the 16th and 17th.

Yet even with this crowd-control strategy, the dense foot traffic badly undercut the experience of visiting. Many small booths were ringed layer upon layer by visitors stopping to examine the exhibits, while in the larger booths there was barely room to turn around once you stepped inside.

The fair’s steadily surging popularity over the past two years has led many design brands to schedule their new-product launches during Design Shanghai. From among the thousand-plus debuts, we picked the 24 most worth seeing. If you don’t have the time or the inclination to brave this excessively “lively” design fair, take a look at its highlights instead.

Most of these are brand-new launches, with a few highly popular picks mixed in. Bentu, for instance, continues its exploration of technique in cement materials, while Ziinlife keeps breaking the fixed image it has left with users; young brand WUU ventures into furniture design for the first time, and Japanese star studio Nendo has brought its designs for Zens Living to the show.

### Xi / 2018, designed by Bentu

When it comes to independent design brands that can work endless magic with concrete, Bentu is one.

For this fair Bentu brought new outdoor pieces and wall-decoration materials. The Xi / 2018 series comprises wall-mounted planters and free-standing planters of various sizes, made of concrete mixed with recycled construction-waste aggregate.

The raw cement color is clean and, at first glance, a bit cold and hard — but given its purpose — holding fresh flowers and greenery and pairing with home decor — this look turns out to be ideal: grounded, goes with everything, the kind of product that grows on you.

### Frame / 2018, designed by Bentu

This set of floor lamps named “Frame” is also part of Bentu’s new 2018 outdoor series. The lamp body’s frame, in raw cement made of concrete and recycled construction-waste aggregate, looks firm and crisp in line, with a strong geometric feel.

However, team members told us the Frame floor lamp makes demands on its setting: it works best in public outdoor spaces where wiring has already been laid underground; for home use, you need to bury lines in advance in a private garden or outdoor area. The collision of cold cement and warm light makes for a striking presence in an outdoor space.

### Impromptu Sofa & Impromptu Chair, designed by Ziinlife

Judging from its past two seasons, homegrown design brand Ziinlife has been pushing to break through, shedding its former warm, cute, fresh-and-clean image for a richer, more varied design language. The designers told us that “Wonderful Balance,” the new 2018 spring collection, seems to play visual games but is in fact built around strong practicality.

Take the Impromptu Sofa: it looks like an improvised burst of inspiration, assembled by simply interlocking two clean flat planes, giving the whole structure a light feel. The Impromptu Chair from the same series turns two-dimensional planes into a three-dimensional solid form — nimble and ingenious. The backrest extends into an armrest on one side, the seat cushion extends into an armrest on the other; the three interlock and echo each other in interesting ways.

### Xianting Lamp, designed by Ziinlife

This is another new piece from Ziinlife’s spring collection this year; it has a refined mechanical feel while remaining thoroughly modern. The Xianting Lamp mimics a pendulum, swaying between balance and imbalance: the slender lamp stem swings around the axis of the base bracket. The base is stable and elegant, designed with a crescent-moon-like arc, and a powerful hidden magnet inside keeps the simply structured lamp forever poised.

### AXIS Side Table, designed by WUU

This is independent home-design brand WUU’s fourth year at Design Shanghai. Under the theme “A SENSE OF TIMELESSNESS,” it presented products spanning three categories: lighting, daily-use objects, and furniture. This also marks WUU’s first foray into furniture design.

AXIS is the first piece in WUU’s furniture line, available in two models with two optional tabletop materials; every part of the body is machined from a single block of aluminum — solid and grounded. The anodized surface treatment gives AXIS a fine tactile and visual quality. The marble version combines the warmth of stone with the hardness of aluminum.

### QUARK Quark Pendant Lamp, designed by WUU

True to its name, QUARK is a basic unit of spatial lighting: it can be used as a single lamp or grouped in multiples. Tap the touch switch on the body to adjust the brightness. Its form is simple and clear, yet the surface texture is round and delicate.

### Angle Ruler, designed by Yiwai Design

This is the star piece of the “Designer’s Gifts” series from independent design brand Yiwai Design — not a new product this season, but immensely popular. Notably, the Angle Ruler won a product design award at the 2018 Red Dot Awards.

It is highly innovative, overturning many people’s fixed idea of a ruler by combining the functions of a straightedge, a parallel ruler, and a protractor. Two rulers attach to each other via magnets and iron strips; a slide track controls the direction, and the whole can easily split in two or join into one.

The front carries conventional 20-centimeter straightedge markings; the back offers a very practical graphic-design tool — a reference chart of type sizes and line weights.

### Combo Sofa, designed by FRANK CHOU DESIGN STUDIO

The new pieces FRANK CHOU DESIGN STUDIO (Zhou Chenchen Design Studio) showed this time take “space, time, and light” as their point of reflection, expressing the theme “Timeless Sculpture in the Home.”

The sofa named Combo distills several of the most representative techniques from the history of handmade upholstery, using playful modular combinations to remake the “classic” with modern design methods. In the designer’s words, this lively, bold composite has the dramatic color of a “game combo” and the whimsical rhythm of a “small band.”

### Stack Side Table, designed by FRANK CHOU DESIGN STUDIO

The staggered tabletops and rectangular support structure, worked through a “three-dimensional composition” approach, display a distinctive sense of volume and artistic color.

### Kirstie van Noort × 2016/, designed by Rongji

Design brand Rongji invited Dutch designer Kirstie van Noort to dig deep into the raw materials used to make Arita porcelain. The ceramic series she created in collaboration with 2016/ presents a warm palette of earth tones and white-based gradient dinner plates — and you might never guess that these gentle works are made with discarded black ore as both inspiration and ingredient.

She ground the black ore into powder, mixed it with white kaolin, and developed a new type of clay, from which she made a series of tableware. After firing, it takes on a warm earth tone; she then developed new natural glazes — mixing sand and iron in different proportions produces different colors.

So the final works fall into two series. One is in earth tones, made from the clay developed by mixing black ore with base ore; the uniform earth color is entirely the clay’s own, with no other dyes added. The other series keeps the traditional white as the base color but uses the newly developed natural glazes, applied with airbrush coloring, to create gradients.

### Linglong Porcelain Series, designed by Yiyang Yisheng

The new Linglong porcelain series Ran Xiangfei brought this time comprises a set of containers of various sizes. Its signature feature: on the porcelain body, openwork carving creates many regular “linglong eyes,” and after glazing and firing these holes become translucent bright apertures. The new pieces’ apertures come in several patterns, and when a light is lit inside, the interplay of light and shadow makes the porcelain look even more crystalline.

### Moon Shadow Lamp, designed by Camino

Camino means “road” or “path” in Spanish; the design language of this American home-design brand is a fusion of styles — above all, Nordic minimalism blended with American vintage design.

The Moon Shadow Lamp uses a Western minimalist design language to tell an Eastern longing for home. Created by up-and-coming Taiwanese designer Vii CHEN and inspired by homesickness while studying abroad, it uses metal wire to create a diffused light source — like seeing the moon through a window, a hazy effect that stirs the imagination. As a lamp, it both illuminates and serves as ambient decor; as a screen, it cuts and partitions the view.

### Iceland Series, Sérénité Holiday Series, Great Wave Series, and Tarsila Series, designed by Minimaïst

Designer Qian Qingtong, winner of the 2017 “China’s Best Design Newcomer” award, founded the design brand Minimaïst. What she brought to the fair this time are the Iceland series, the Sérénité holiday series, the Great Wave series, and the 2018 newcomer, the Tarsila series.

The firm that helped her design the booth was Buschmeyer + Cai Architects. Responding to Minimaïst’s brand spirit of bringing nature indoors, the architects wanted these beautiful objects to be presented within the nature that gives them their posture — even if only within a tiny space. The entire booth is a model of the rolling terrain along Iceland’s southern coast, abstracted through a series of treatments into a frozen fragment cut from nature, in dialogue with the Iceland and Tarsila series.

### Aoyama Series, designed by ZAOZUO

Shuhei Aoyama, a Japanese designer hugely popular in China, ventures into furniture design for the first time, bringing the Aoyama series to home-design brand ZAOZUO.

Based on the 7-2-1 principle of usage frequency, he sets open spaces for the most frequently used items and accounts for storage needs in every scenario, delivering a whole-house solution: wardrobes, sideboards, bookshelves, entry cabinets, TV cabinets, shoe cabinets, and nightstands, paired with flexible, expandable modules that offer 57 combinations to meet a household’s storage needs.

### YUAN Series Coat Rack, designed by URBANCRAFT

URBANCRAFT is an independent home-furnishings brand founded in 2016 by designer Li Ximi, who previously served as chief designer at Neri&Hu.

The “YUAN” series covers coat racks, side tables, mirrors, and lamps. This coat rack combines three materials — brass, walnut, and cowhide: a brass frame carries a wooden hanging rod and a bottom tray, the cowhide connects the rod to the brass scarf ring, and the wooden tray at the bottom can be removed for separate use.

### by 3 cabinet/coffee table system, designed by URBANCRAFT

### Magnet & Paperweight, designed by Jiannanhua

Besides patterned tiles, handmade cement-tile brand Jiannanhua has also launched derivative products — including bookends we previously covered, and the “Magnet” and “Paperweight” brought to the fair this time — all attempts by the designers to recycle cement tiles.

Their raw material is mostly discarded corner fragments from the making of handmade cement tiles, later wrapped in brass. The “Paperweight” can hold down light objects or serve for self-defense; the “Magnet” can be stuck to a refrigerator or iron pipe, adding a nice touch.

### Silo Clock, designed by Beyond Object

British design brand Beyond Object, which focuses on desktop home objects, home accessories, and personal accessories, brought its signature Silo clock to the fair. The clock has no dial of its own: users can freely arrange the positions of the 12 markers and modify it at will.

Silo’s hour and minute hands are in fact two incomplete triangles; as time passes, the shapes they form keep changing — a beauty you can sink into.

### ALIGN Off-Axis Rotating Pen, designed by Beyond Object

The visual focus of the ALIGN off-axis writing pen is a displaced section of barrel. The designers noticed people’s habitual tendency to “align” things, and used this to nudge users into rotating the misaligned barrel section into alignment — which slides the refill out and deploys the writing function. But users soon discover that once the refill is out and the second barrel section is aligned, the third section is misaligned again. The unique offset design keeps it resting steadily on a desk without rolling, whether open or closed.

### Birdsong Vessels, designed by Zens Living

Home brand Zens Living brought the booth “Village of Wonders” that Japanese design studio Nendo had designed for it at the Paris home-decor fair, transplanting it to Design Shanghai. The already-small booth space was cut up further: display shelves became irregular little white houses forming a small village, and the exhibits became its “villagers.”

This series of Birdsong vessels is also by Nendo. Inspired by small birds perched on power lines, stands themed on “trees” and “power lines” carry containers of various sizes, so that the whole suggests a flock of different-sized birds roosting on them.

### Character-Riddle Series, designed by Zens Living

This series is likewise Nendo’s work. It incorporates geometric points and lines — elements of structural and stable beauty — and takes shape from the basic units of pictographic script: radicals and components. By weaving traditional Chinese-character elements into everyday small furniture, the designers echo Zens Living’s theme of Eastern living.
