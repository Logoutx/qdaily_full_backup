At 9 a.m. on November 13, earlier than the scheduled time, Mingjue (a pseudonym) arrived alone at the labor and personnel dispute arbitration tribunal in Laoshan District, Qingdao. He waited at the entrance on the first floor for the lawyers, ready to have them hand the arbitrator five LGBT educational pamphlets along with some bank statements. Mingjue believed these pamphlets would help the arbitrator understand him better, and would help the case move forward.

A little over a month earlier, kindergarten teacher Mingjue had filed a complaint with the arbitration tribunal, saying he had been unlawfully dismissed by Wei Wenquan, the head of his former employer, "Mengbao Kingdom Daycare" (hereafter Mengbao Kingdom), because of his sexual orientation.

Before the hearing, Mingjue went through every procedure a labor case is supposed to have: filing the case, communication, mediation. But in the end no settlement could be reached, leaving only the final stage: the hearing itself.

Mingjue had made many preparations for the hearing day, but when the day came, he did not plan to go in with the lawyers. "I was worried I'd get too emotional," he said, recalling how, during an earlier session at the mediation committee, he had burst into tears the moment he started talking about his past experiences of discrimination.

The five educational pamphlets were materials Mingjue had picked up at gay community events: *Understanding "Tongzhi"*, *The Seven Deadly Sins of "Homophobic Textbooks"*, *The Anti-Extortion Handbook*, *Duoduo's Quirky Misadventures*, and *Campus Bullying: STOP*.

![The LGBT educational pamphlets Mingjue prepared (Photo / Chen Liya)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20181209205124THdIYaO5woS80EJz.jpg-w600)

The hearing had originally been set for 9:20 a.m., but due to "procedural issues" — the tribunal had made a last-minute change to hold the hearing in closed session — it was delayed by more than an hour, finally beginning at 10:30 a.m.

While waiting for the hearing to begin, Mingjue rode his bike to Mengbao Kingdom on his own.

It had been quite a while since he had last been here since leaving the job. He noticed the daycare had put up a new sign, someone was cooking in the kitchen, and the little sandpit in the courtyard still bore traces of children playing.

Whether he could return to a workplace like this had now become an open question — even if he won the lawsuit.

### 1.

Mingjue is from Tai'an, Shandong. He is 31 this year, not tall, wears glasses, and speaks in a gentle, soft voice. He has just completed 10 years working as a kindergarten teacher.

He had never thought about what it would be like not to work in education. Until this past August, he was still planning to save enough money to open a kindergarten of his own in Qingdao.

He came to Qingdao in 2014. By then Mingjue already counted as an experienced early-childhood teacher, and he found a job at a kindergarten in Laoshan District that practiced the Montessori method he identified with. He stayed for three years.

While teaching at this kindergarten, two key events changed the course of Mingjue's career.

One was the incident of parents collectively storming the kindergarten. In 2015, over problems with the facilities and environment, the kindergarten was collectively reported by a group of parents, triggering a wave of students withdrawing. A kindergarten that originally had nearly 30 students suddenly lost more than half of them.

At the time the parents demanded the kindergarten relocate. To keep the kindergarten from closing, Mingjue, as a mere employee, could simply have ignored these demands. But after work every day, he still rode his bike around Qingdao looking for a more suitable location.

He even joined a direct-selling company to solve the enrollment problems caused by the withdrawal wave, only to find the results less than impressive.

The reasoning was simple: "When I was studying to become a psychological counselor, I got to know a sister who worked in direct selling. She invited me to give an educational talk at one of the direct-selling branch offices, and the response was excellent. I realized their teams were spread all over Qingdao, and since our kindergarten was facing pretty big enrollment difficulties at the time — the facilities weren't that good and the location was poor — I thought I could use the talks for publicity."

In June 2017, the kindergarten closed.

Perhaps the closure of the kindergarten triggered the second key event: partnering with the father of one of his students, Wei Wenquan, to open a kindergarten together.

### 2.

With the kindergarten closed, Mingjue suddenly lost his job. At the same time, Wei Wenquan came to him and asked whether he wanted to open a kindergarten together, with Wei providing the capital. This was Wei Wenquan's first investment in an early-childhood institution.

"He was originally my older son's teacher, and I thought his classes were decent — after all, that kindergarten was pretty small. And it happened that my younger son was about to start school. After getting to know him, I felt this teacher was pretty good," Wei Wenquan said.

For Mingjue, who had long harbored the idea of opening his own kindergarten, this proposal was a great chance to realize his dream, and he quickly agreed.

On October 23, 2017, Mengbao Kingdom was formally registered and established. Besides Mingjue, Wei Wenquan also recruited another teacher in charge of enrollment, and the three became partners of Mengbao Kingdom together. In March 2018 it formally opened its doors to students.

Mingjue went from an ordinary employee to the daycare's teaching program director. In a sense, this was the first collaboration between two "novices."

Everything started from zero, and what lies ahead is usually the unknown and difficulty. Mingjue and Wei Wenquan would frequently differ in their ideas about the direction of teaching and operations. "The first year or two was a phase of adjustment and exploration. We were both experimenting, and neither of us felt that one person's ideas had to be the right ones," Mingjue added. "This was all within the normal scope of cooperation."

![The small playground outside Mengbao Kingdom Daycare (Photo / Chen Liya)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20181209205525oVbGC3450j6A8vRz.jpg-w600)

Worth noting is that Mengbao Kingdom's location was exactly where the previously closed kindergarten had been, inside a residential compound in Laoshan District.

The compound has five buildings, and Mengbao Kingdom occupied the first floor of one of them, spanning two units. Outside there was a courtyard converted into a small playground; besides the colorful graffiti on the walls, it had a small sandpit, a slide, toy cars and other facilities.

Within a two-kilometer radius of Mengbao Kingdom there were at least nearly 10 kindergartens, but most were relatively large ones, with 200 to 300 students, mainly children aged 3 to 6.

This was one of the reasons Mengbao Kingdom ultimately decided to admit only children aged 0 to 3 and to position itself as a "daycare." In addition, their fees were in the mid-to-high range: the monthly management fee plus meal fees came to about 5,000 yuan.

Because tuition was priced high and the space was limited, nearly four months after formally opening, Mengbao Kingdom had at most eight students — and one of them was Wei Wenquan's younger son.

Enrollment difficulties soon surfaced. "Later, seeing that enrollment was not particularly ideal, we started doing publicity — we made an official account and a mini program, and even screened movies in the compound. I was originally the teaching director, and enrollment was handled by another operations director, but later on a lot of things still ended up being my responsibility," Mingjue said.

![The mini program Mengbao Kingdom Daycare made for enrollment](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20181209205654tle76Avb9oSH5h2c.jpg-w600)

### 3.

On July 28, 2018, Mingjue took his mother along to the second-anniversary celebration of PFLAG's Linyi, Shandong branch (a gathering for families and friends of gay people), where he shared his experience of living as a gay man.

On August 6, he shared the event write-up, "[Linyi Branch Second Anniversary: A Chicken Feast — I'm Gay and Proud!](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/sqoLZl7_mF49gTLBnr7qIQ)," on his Moments.

Mingjue had not come out to everyone yet, so he first set the post's privacy controls so that the parents of Mengbao Kingdom students could not see it. But parents of former students saw it, and they sent messages to Wei Wenquan.

"At the time some parents reacted very strongly. They messaged me on WeChat asking whether they should take their kids to get blood tests and so on — and quite a few of them. I felt the parents really minded, and I worried that if this started spreading around our daycare, the daycare would collapse. We only have a handful of kids here to begin with; if all the parents objected, they might all withdraw," Wei Wenquan said.

The next day, Wei Wenquan called Mingjue into the office, told him about the parents' "feedback," and gave him two choices: either never again publicly reveal himself as gay in the identity of a teacher, or resign.

"His professional ability was still quite strong; nobody doubted that. But if you don't go public with your identity and just do your own job well, I think that's still acceptable," Wei Wenquan said.

Recalling that conversation afterward, Mingjue was still angry. He said something like this would never happen to an "ordinary person."

As a gay man, Mingjue believed that working in education had nothing to do with sexual orientation. But he also understood that the issue had nothing to do with whether his partner minded his orientation: "Maybe he (Wei) felt the institution was still small and it didn't matter if one person was fired. But it also shows he was very afraid of pressure from the parents' side. After all, he was thinking more from an operator's perspective — it might affect enrollment."

Mingjue was torn. He was, after all, one of the daycare's partners; yet once it came to the rights of gay people, it was a matter of personal mission. So on the night of his conversation with Wei Wenquan, he first posted a photo of a "resignation statement" in Mengbao Kingdom's internal WeChat group. It read: due to personal factors, he was resigning.

Afterward, Mingjue shared the matter with friends, and through the introduction of the gay-rights organization "LGBT Rights Advocacy Association," he came into contact with lawyer Tang Xiangqian and lawyer Yu Liying, both of whom had previously handled cases and fought for the rights of sexual minorities.

"From a legal point of view, the grounds for termination were insufficient. Sharing a gay article did not violate any rules," said lawyer Tang Xiangqian.

With the lawyers' endorsement and support, Mingjue's determination to fight for his rights only grew stronger, and he decided to file a lawsuit.

### 4.

On September 19, Mingjue went to the labor and personnel dispute arbitration tribunal in Qingdao to submit an application, deciding to sue Wei Wenquan for discrimination under the rubric of a labor dispute.

That day, outside the entrance of the tribunal building, he held up a sign that read: "I teach children to be honest, so I cannot lie. I am gay."

![Mingjue holding the sign outside the labor arbitration tribunal on the day he submitted the application (Photo / provided by Mingjue)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20181209210100QR3G0yenWHgiCarj.jpg-w600)

On September 27, the Qingdao Labor and Personnel Dispute Arbitration Commission formally accepted the case. Mingjue received the tribunal's notice, which stated the hearing date, location, and the cause: "compensation."

Because Wei Wenquan had not signed a formal labor contract with Mingjue, under the law, if the case involved unlawful dismissal, the employer would have to pay the employee double wages on a monthly basis starting from the second month of employment.

News of the case filing [was first exposed through the official account of the "LGBT Rights Advocacy Association"](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/4FgRon5e9PcjTw8cj7GCcw). Many media outlets immediately followed up with reports, and before long the matter became a trending topic on social networks.

When Mingjue speaks, there is a "teaching" kind of enthusiasm about him; he does not hesitate to share his views and values on a subject. Even while mired in the lawsuit, he turned away no media outlet that came knocking.

More than 20 media organizations contacted him. For this he created a special category label and group for them. During this period of "losing his teaching job," besides continuing his direct-selling work, a large part of his time went to responding to media interviews.

Raising broad public opinion was one of Mingjue's motives for suing. "After this case was exposed, it stirred up a wave of online discussion about whether gay people can be teachers. I'd say that initially achieved my goal," Mingjue said.

Mingjue and the lawyers tended to see the case as a form of advocacy — in other words, they believed how the case unfolded would affect the development of other people's efforts to fight for similar rights.

"This is discrimination against gay people, but as reflected in this case it is a labor dispute. Without his identity, this would just be an ordinary labor dispute case," said lawyer Tang Xiangqian.

Mingjue said Wei Wenquan had once warned him that if the lawsuit continued, no one in Shandong's early-childhood education circle might ever hire him again. But Mingjue believed the lawsuit was quite likely to get a broader range of people to correct a mistaken impression, and was therefore worth fighting.

As the case developed, Wei Wenquan and Mingjue diverged in their views of where the case should go and what to expect from it. The former only wanted the labor arbitration to be reasonable; the latter wanted the inequality faced by gay people to be redressed — even though, strictly speaking, it was against those parents that he should have sought redress.

"I had absolutely no intention of toppling him (Wei) or smearing his name. I mostly felt the whole thing was very unfair — that over an incident like this, I was fired," Mingjue said.

As for Wei Wenquan, as an investor, what he cared about most was the compensation issue. "I did not mistreat him, including over the later resignation — he wrote a resignation letter too. He left, and I gave him compensation; I did everything that decency required. I feel I did everything I should have," Wei Wenquan added. "Although he is suing me, I don't consider him my enemy. Since you chose to do it, you must have your reasons... I count as a firsthand witness and participant in this gay incident too."

Regarding Mingjue's claim that he had come out to some people at the "previous kindergarten," Wei Wenquan said he had not known about this. He had only sensed before that Mingjue had gay tendencies, but it had never been made explicit, until he saw the Moments article on August 5. As for inviting Mingjue to run the school together, he had never taken that into consideration.

Wei Wenquan said that from the formal opening until now, Mengbao Kingdom had been continuously losing money. Although he had long expected losses in the early phase, he had not imagined that after only five months of operation, a student's parent would report to him that Mingjue was gay and unfit to be a teacher.

![The formal case-acceptance notice (Photo / provided by Mingjue)](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20181209210549lPorT85aqGbDXxvj.jpg-w600)

### 5.

Mingjue said he is now a "controversial figure" in Shandong Province. Even if he were to apply to any kindergarten at random, "it might bring them pressure, and I don't want to bring too much trouble to others" — which meant he had to give up the teaching job for the time being.

Before the dismissal incident, the people who knew about his gay identity were all his closer friends and relatives, colleagues, and some students' parents.

Mingjue said he had not previously been treated unfairly in the workplace because of being gay. "This is the only time in all these years that a boss has fired me. In the past I was always the one who fired the boss — when I was unhappy with the work, I just said goodbye." But he also mentioned that during his school years, he had indeed been bullied by classmates because of his effeminate traits.

Unlike some gay people who only focus on their personal lives, Mingjue cares deeply about gay rights.

"When I had just graduated from university, I once thought about going into social work. At the time I even wanted to write to national-level people's congress deputies across the country, petitioning for the legalization of same-sex marriage. Later I came to feel that anti-discrimination — the law is of course one path, but the most fundamental thing is changing people's hearts, influencing how people understand others," Mingjue said.

### 6.

From the filing of the case through the mediation period, Mingjue said more than once that after the case concluded, he hoped to have a chance to return to Mengbao Kingdom and continue teaching. He said he missed the children very much.

But deep down he knew clearly that once he had made up his mind to sue, this idea became a fantasy.

Mengbao Kingdom's student count has already gone from eight to six.

Wei Wenquan said that the day after the case was filed, people from the Qingdao Municipal Education Bureau came to Mengbao Kingdom and demanded that he shut the daycare down, the reason being "the public-opinion fallout was too big — media across the country and even overseas had reported on it, and the government was under pressure."

Wei Wenquan pressed the education bureau to let the daycare stay open until after Spring Festival, since some students would graduate by then and a whole cohort could be sent off. "Later the education bureau didn't say yes or no — just said to keep it open for now, and that we had to wait until this matter was handled. After all, if the daycare closed, there would be nothing left for the arbitration to deal with. What would I still be handling if the daycare closed?"

Up to the day of the hearing, Mengbao Kingdom was still operating, but how much longer it could stay open, Wei Wenquan had no idea.

![A screenshot from Mengbao Kingdom Daycare's promotional video](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20181209210915H8LkI2QaN0z51qVR.jpg-w600)

There was more than one uncertain thing about the whole lawsuit. That the two sides ultimately headed toward a formal hearing was itself because the mediation committee had failed to reach a consensus.

During the process, Mingjue actually did not take a hard line on the compensation issue. He conceded the compensation he might originally have received — 40,000 yuan — down to 9,000 yuan. But he hoped that a line could be added to the mediation agreement as a note: "Both parties recognize that gay people enjoy equal rights to employment."

If the mediation succeeded, this line might constitute a form of legal support — for Mingjue, and for the broader gay community. But in a sense, this sentence went beyond the scope of the labor arbitration dispute between Mingjue and Wei Wenquan — because in fact neither party denied that gay people enjoy equal employment rights; rather, Wei Wenquan, under pressure from others and out of consideration for his interests, had terminated the employment relationship with Mingjue.

Wei Wenquan also agreed to add the note and complete the mediation.

Lawyer Tang Xiangqian put it this way: "We hoped to get an official document stamped by the formal authorities, which would carry more authority. This was a bottom-line issue for our client. Without that sentence, this case would be an ordinary labor dispute case, unrelated to being gay."

In the end, however, the arbitration side did not agree to sensitive wording appearing on the mediation document of a labor dispute case, and the mediation was declared a failure.

### 7.

On November 22, the labor arbitration ruling came out: Mingjue won. The ruling showed that Wei Wenquan had to pay Mingjue the wage differential — a total of six months' wages — and dismissed all other arbitration claims.

The moment the ruling came out, Mingjue passed the news to the media, and publicly expressed mixed feelings of relief and disappointment: "The arbitration delivered a relatively just result, and this case also let all of society see the longing of sexual minorities for legitimate rights. But at the same time, there is some disappointment, because for now, society as a whole still harbors discrimination and prejudice, and many people still refuse to try to understand groups different from themselves — they even cause harm and negative impact to others, and choose to look the other way. So this case is not an endpoint but a starting point. I hope more and more comrades who suffer discrimination because of their sexual orientation and gender expression will stand up and say NO!"

Under Article 83 of the Labor Law of the People's Republic of China, if a party to the case disagrees with the ruling, they may file a lawsuit with the local court within 15 days.

Mingjue told QDaily that he was not sure whether Wei Wenquan would appeal. Wei Wenquan, for his part, had not yet formally responded.
