What audiences got instead was not what they wanted — a better-polished film.

On March 25, Lao Lang appeared on *I Am a Singer* and sang *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* (《睡在我上铺的兄弟》). Twenty-two years earlier, Lao Lang had been the original singer of this campus folk song. That same night, the song's lyricist and composer Gao Xiaosong also appeared on stage, joining in to rap a verse. The young audience of *I Am a Singer* found it fun and fresh.

Perhaps the happiest person at that moment was Huang Ziyan, vice president of marketing at Le Vision Pictures. In fact, the two of them singing *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* together on that hit TV show had been orchestrated by Le Vision Pictures. A week later, the film of the same name, backed by Le Vision Pictures, would hit theaters.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702101753RusrLxNcHwmvjBtq.jpg-w600)

What delighted Huang Ziyan was that the name of this classic campus song, cultivated over the past two years, seemed to have gained new influence just as they had hoped. And Gao Xiaosong, the key figure who had granted the film adaptation rights to *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*, was extremely cooperative with the publicity. When the film adaptation of the song was announced last May, Gao Xiaosong said: "When we recorded *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*, the budget was tight and there was no money to shoot an MV. I always kept thinking that I had to make up for that regret."

While Gao Xiaosong and Lao Lang were on *I Am a Singer*, director Zhang Qi and the film's lead actors, Chen Xiao and Li Xian among others, were taking *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* on a roadshow to 41 universities across the country. That number far exceeds the 31 stops of the comparable youth film *Fleet of Time* and the 16 stops of *The Left Ear*. Huang Ziyan said the money they spent marketing *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* was no less than what went into *Tiny Times*.

Director Zhang Qi found that he had deliberately planted fewer than ten jokes in the film, but by the staff's on-site count, the audience burst into collective laughter 30 to 40 times. After the final brawl scene ended, the whole theater even broke into applause.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201607021017538LfXSt9jNzsl4TpU.jpg-w600)

Before the film's release, Le Vision Pictures put out a long infographic titled "The Birth of a Super IP: Milestones of *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*." Lending a classic song's name to a film was something most film companies, Le Vision Pictures included, had never done — not to mention that they had also specially produced a web series of the same name and layered on large-scale marketing.

LeEco believed that this comprehensive innovation of the model gave them a higher chance of winning. Of course, they hoped for big box office.

## "Gao Xiaosong Is Enough"

In early 2015, Le Vision Pictures got hold of a screenplay called *Crazy for Graduation* (《我为毕业狂》), the story of four boys during their college graduation. They then approached Gao Xiaosong, the writer of *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*, and reached a deal about a month later, though the cost of the rights transaction was not disclosed. The screenplay was retitled *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*.

Both the producers and the investors believed the opinion of the original IP creator mattered greatly. Gao Xiaosong served as the film's chief producer and music director. He was directly involved in discussing and deciding many of the creative choices in the film's production and promotion, and even made a cameo. His two students, Yin Yue and Qian Lei, wrote the lyrics and music for several of the film's songs.

For audiences, he was also arguably the most influential person on the crew, the lead actors included. In Huang Ziyan's view, "Gao Xiaosong is enough."

When the film had just completed its rough cut, there was an internal screening. A third of the way in, Gao Xiaosong suddenly called for a stop. Director Zhang Qi and the Le Vision Pictures executives at the screening were stunned, worried that Gao Xiaosong disliked the film *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*. Then Gao Xiaosong said, "I'm going to the bathroom. Wait for me — don't keep playing it before I get back. Just a few minutes." Only then did they breathe a sigh of relief.

Before LeEco brought in Gao Xiaosong, the screenwriter of the original *Crazy for Graduation* screenplay was Zhang Qi, who now directs the film. He had previously made three theatrical features: *Lethal Heartbeat* (《夺命心跳》), *Save Me* (《救我》), and *Holding Love* (《HOLD 住爱》). Zhang Qi's idea began in June 2013. He wanted to make a different kind of youth film — one that was not nostalgic but of the moment, and very realistic. It would focus on the stretch before college graduation and tell a story about the hot-blooded recklessness of young men.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702101753Etw9i0Q5nplhsczJ.jpg-w600)

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702101754hoVCiBySkFluvqP1.jpg-w600)

The box-office appeal of this kind of story had precedent. That same April, Vicki Zhao's directorial debut *So Young* was released, made its mark as a youth film, and ultimately grossed over 700 million yuan, ranking third in that year's China box office. Wanting to make a different kind of youth film at that moment was an unmistakably commercial ambition.

![Still from *So Young*](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201607021017545SRNqgrP8TK3XlZI.jpg-w600)

In May 2014, Zhang Qi's first screenplay draft came out; at the time it was called *Adult Guide* (《成人指南》). The name seemed to allow for a bit of flirtation with budding sexuality, but the censorship verdict Zhang Qi received said it showed a tendency toward vulgarity, and it failed to pass. The screenplay took the name *Crazy for Graduation* and circulated from one film company to another in search of a money backer, until it finally landed at Le Vision Pictures.

Zhang Zhao, CEO of Le Vision Pictures, took a liking to the screenplay and felt its core story was strong. He was around 50 and remembered a campus folk song popular in the 1990s, *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*. He asked Zhang Qi whether he was interested in combining *Crazy for Graduation* with *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*. According to Zhang Qi, Zhang Zhao made the suggestion partly because Le Vision Pictures happened to be in talks with Gao Xiaosong about some collaborations, creating an opportunity to combine the film with music.

Such a change was less a suggestion than a demand from the money side. Huang Ziyan put it bluntly: she believed that on the strength of the screenplay alone, LeEco would not "win." But once it was combined with *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*, "this film could be different." The song had once been on everyone's lips, and its lyrics point straight at campus, youth, and growing up.

It could be positioned as a film about hot-blooded brotherhood. With differentiation and a famous name, it had a chance of winning — and therefore, for Le Vision Pictures, investment value.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702101754QKWovXgyEjZLNeH7.jpg-w600)

But Zhang Qi had some reservations.

When he first wrote the screenplay, Zhang Qi had not wanted to write a nostalgic story. But *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* was plainly too nostalgic. For the teenage viewers who make up the majority of the film market, slapping the name of an end-of-the-century song on the adaptation did not seem like a particularly clever move either. A web series viewer who asked to remain anonymous told QDaily that the title did create a sense of familiarity, but after watching, they were left with more of an impression of the show's content and did not develop any deeper resonance with the song.

Zhang Zhao of Le Vision Pictures told Zhang Qi that the "nostalgia" problem could be solved through the marketing themes in the film's promotion. But the ratio of friendship to romance in the story needed adjusting. Following the differentiated playbook for youth films, the film should give more weight to the friendship storyline. In the end, in the film's final cut, Le Vision Pictures trimmed another 20 minutes of the male and female leads' romance, which left some viewers feeling that the romance was underdeveloped and moved far too fast.

Director Zhang Qi could hardly refuse the demand: as long as the film carried the name *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*, it would draw attention the moment cameras started rolling.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702101755QYdSGc0eWZ6NB2rl.jpg-w600)

## Shooting the Web Series and the Film Together

In May 2015, Le Vision Pictures brought Gao Xiaosong to Tsinghua University to announce that *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* would be adapted into a film. The news was reported by major media outlets that very day, and whether people thought it absurd or worth looking forward to, it drew attention the moment it was announced.

At the same press conference, Le Vision Pictures also announced the cast of *Brothers Sleeping Above Me*. The best-known names were Chen Xiao and Qin Lan; the rest were young newcomers. To soften the nostalgic feel of *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* and win identification from young viewers, LeEco said it would also hold campus singing contests at universities across the country, launching a round of "old songs, new renditions" events, and take the film's actors onto the Le Sports channel for live event coverage.

Unfortunately, participation in these activities was not high. Take the *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* campus music PK contest: after two months and 300 events, total video views for the songs just passed 10 million — meaning an average of around 30,000 views per event.

The online video company also announced that while shooting the film, it would simultaneously shoot a web series with the same name and the same characters.

This is a common IP-incubation tactic: a series can warm audiences up for the film and, with luck, generate hefty advertising and licensing revenue itself. A series can also use extra storylines to make the character setups and the story world look richer. The film *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* tells the story of the three months before college graduation, while the 13-episode web series chose to tell the story of this group of people from freshman to senior year.

Zhang Qi and his team had to rush out the scripts. Shooting was set to start in June, and the scripts had to be done before that. At their usual pace, writing 13 episodes' worth of scripts would take half a year; Zhang Qi and his team shortened that to four months. At the same time, Zhang Qi felt that having the film script come first constrained his creativity to some extent.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702101755bEe5O7W1ocsf0hvk.jpg-w600)

Out of IP-incubation ambition, Zhang Qi gradually came to understand the compromises demanded by reality. Zhang Qi wanted the same group of actors to appear in both the film and the web series. That made the actors uneasy, since it required nearly twice as much of their time. Before shooting formally began, Zhang Qi had wanted to shoot the film and the web series separately, but neither the locations nor the actors' schedules allowed it. In the end, the web series and the film were shot together over four months.

But the two had entirely different budgets and shooting methods. Take one simple example: the crew borrowed a school auditorium during the holidays, so all the auditorium scenes had to be shot in that window, one after another. That is the usual way a series is shot, and now it was copied straight into the film. To ensure image quality, director Zhang Qi required that the cameras always use cinema cameras with prime lenses rather than zoom lenses. Prime lenses are more stable but have to be swapped and remounted constantly. As a result, more than ten days before shooting wrapped, the assistant director clashed with Zhang Qi and walked off with the directing staff. Zhang Qi had no choice but to switch to zoom lenses.

Director Zhang Qi and the crew were not sure how much of a boost the web series could give the film. Still, it was plain to see they were indeed going for a "blockbuster."

The English title of *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* was rendered as *Who Sleeps My Bro?* ("Who Slept With My Brother?"). The series touches on romance, budding sexuality, fights, and homoerotic bromance among the guys, and aired on Le.com right after another of the company's boldly styled self-produced dramas, *Go Princess Go*. Some viewers who liked the web series said, "It's a bit dirty, but pure and passionate." The gags interspersed through the plot are not hackneyed either, good for laughs at a gathering of friends.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702101756ULZRjbI35zBPt40i.jpg-w600)

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702101756wum6k3GXl7DKHjov.jpg-w600)

According to LeEco's plan, to get closer to the target audience — that is, the students currently on campus facing graduation and work — the web series was scheduled to air during the winter break. But since the film would not open until April 1, the gap left for audiences after the web series ended could not be too long, lest their enthusiasm cool. To stretch out the broadcast, LeEco decided to split the originally planned 13-episode web series into 26 episodes, and by changing the viewing pattern, extended the run further.

One web series viewer who asked to remain anonymous said that after the first 13 episodes went online, the remaining episodes were updated only after a long gap, and LeEco did not promptly announce the release schedule, which ruined the viewing experience. In the end, the web series *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* closed its run with 400 million total views. By comparison, *Go Princess Go* surpassed 2.6 billion views at the end of its run.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201607021017575G76qFED0prwhfKL.jpg-w600)

In director Zhang Qi's view, this was all because no one had done a project like this before and they were still in an exploratory phase. Even so, Zhang Qi felt shooting the web series was worth it. Because of the web series, not only Chen Xiao and Qin Lan but also newcomers like Li Xian, Du Tianhao, and Liu Ruilin gained a following during the campus roadshows. Huang Ziyan estimated that 75% of the audiences at roadshow events had watched the web series.

Another direct sign: before the web series aired, the Weibo posts of Li Xian, who plays Xie Xun in the show, usually got only dozens — sometimes even just a handful — of reposts; after it aired, his reposts could reach hundreds or thousands.

## Audiences Didn't Get What They Wanted

On March 25, the duet of *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* by Lao Lang and Gao Xiaosong placed seventh in the elimination round of *I Am a Singer*, and Lao Lang, the lowest-ranked singer, was eliminated. The classic 1990s campus folk song seemed not to have resonated enough with the live audience.

A week later, the film finally opened on April 1.

One moviegoer who asked to remain anonymous said she felt somewhat disappointed after watching the film. The film was less fun than the web series. Perhaps because of length constraints, the middle section felt rushed while the ending dragged. Many of her friends went to see the film because of Gao Xiaosong, but his two scenes "gave viewers full-blown secondhand embarrassment."

Film critic "Taotao Loves Movies," after watching the film, offered this verdict: "A very formulaic and boring film, and the characters have no typicality either. What's more, this film about sleeping brothers doesn't actually put brotherhood at its center (it's only expressed through symbols) — instead it falls back into the dull clichés of a romance. And those lines you shout from the top of the building at the end — do you yourselves even believe them?" As of 3 p.m. on April 7, the film's Douban rating was 5.3.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201607021017571hW68fUcC2ldM5gO.jpg-w600)

Le Vision Pictures is probably more concerned with the box-office effect of this super IP than with such reviews.

In its first three days, *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* held at roughly 20 million yuan in daily box office, with about 20 people per screening on average. Compared with the youth film *The Left Ear*, released in April 2015, both the box office and the per-screening attendance were only about half of *The Left Ear*'s. It also ran into tough comedy competition — *Chongqing Hot Pot* and *My Beloved Bodyguard* opened the same period — and three days in, the box office of *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* began to slide.

![Still from *Chongqing Hot Pot*](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702101758tFh5ZCn4uMTqGPw3.jpg-w600)

At present, the film *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* has yet to pass the 100-million-yuan mark. And since the film's release, neither Le Vision Pictures, the film's official Weibo account, nor the director and several lead actors have mentioned the film's box office on their Weibo pages.

This super IP ultimately failed to add a milestone of great weight. According to Le Vision Pictures' plan, the idea was this: through marketing, *Brothers Sleeping Above Me* would go from a warm, nostalgic song into a hot-blooded, of-the-moment film and TV product, with the most important audience being the students now on campus. Casting, behind-the-scenes team, content design, release channels, and marketing channels — none of it deviated from this plan.

But what audiences got instead was not what they wanted — a better-polished film.
