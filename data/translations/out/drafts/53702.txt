### Agenda Setters

**[The Washington Post: Europe is rejecting thousands of Afghan asylum seekers every year — but what awaits them back home?](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/europe-is-rejecting-thousands-of-afghan-asylum-seekers-a-year-but-what-awaits-them-back-home/2018/05/28/62494144-1593-11e8-930c-45838ad0d77a_story.html?utm_term=.fa1c1f6094c6)**

Three years of living in Brussels had, for the 25-year-old Afghan Hewad Sobhani, amounted to an initiation into Western civilization: a bright apartment, a lively social life, a job at a café. Paying taxes, learning French, making friends. The turmoil of his homeland seemed far away.

But in May he set out on the journey home, becoming one of the thousands of asylum seekers turned away by Western Europe this month.

In 2001, the Taliban ended their 30-year rule over Afghanistan, and millions of Afghan refugees were taken in by other countries. Most crossed the border into Iran or Pakistan. Hundreds of thousands headed to Western Europe in search of a new life.

In fact, many of the young people who left were not fleeing war, unrest, or religious repression. On the contrary, they left at a time when Afghanistan was under the "democratic trusteeship" of the United States and NATO. Most were single young people like Hewad Sobhani; only a few lived in rural areas long overshadowed by the Taliban — the majority came from cities.

In 2014, Afghanistan's wartime economy collapsed as Western troops withdrew, and word spread that many Syrian refugees who had made it to Europe had successfully stayed. Only then did young Afghans like Hewad Sobhani join the largest population movement since World War II.

In 2015, a total of 200,000 Afghans entered Western Europe, 80% of them applying for asylum, and received temporary asylum permits in wealthy countries such as Germany. Friendly locals, free accommodation, and language courses made these refugees feel that legal status was no longer a distant prospect.

But over the past two years, Western European countries have been tightening their refugee policies: more asylum applications are being rejected and deportation orders issued at an accelerating pace. That does not mean Afghanistan is ready to absorb this newly returned population of its citizens. Taking them in will be all the more difficult: these are people who grew up in the post-Taliban era, spent their childhoods and adolescence amid the Western world's promises of democracy and a free life, and fled under threat of war — yet who now face, upon deportation, a country where everything remains to be rebuilt.

Afghanistan's unemployment rate currently stands at 40%, and refugees are being deported back from Iran and Pakistan in a steady stream. Some 400,000 new workers enter the labor market each year looking for jobs. Families often regard these suddenly returned relatives as a burden, and some refugees must repay relatives the thousands of dollars they borrowed to scrape together travel money for the journey west.

Afghanistan also remains far from safe. Forty percent of its territory experiences persistent unrest or violent attacks.

"We have security problems. We have economic problems. We also have 1.6 million people being deported from Pakistan and Iran. We signed an agreement with Europe last year to take back all deportees, and we are cooperating with every effort, but we will keep asking Europe to review its Afghanistan policy," Hafiz Ahmad Miakhel, a spokesman for the Afghan refugee agency, told The Washington Post.

### 24-Hour News Makers

**[Italy's government formation fails, prime minister replaced, fresh general election possible, sending investment markets into turmoil](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/may/28/italy-president-sergio-mattarella-names-interim-prime-minister-carlo-cottarelli)**.

Two years after the UK's Brexit referendum, Italy's continuing political turmoil has once again cast a shadow over the future of the eurozone.

On May 27, after two months of negotiations, the two parties trying to form a government — the "anti-establishment" populist Five Star Movement and the far-right League — reached an agreement, proposing to Italian President Sergio Mattarella that law professor Giuseppe Conte, who has no political experience, serve as prime minister, and putting forward Paolo Savona, an 81-year-old economist with euroskeptic views, as finance minister.

Mattarella approved the choice of prime minister, but in order to "defend the eurozone" and "avoid panic among investors overseas and in Italy," he refused to approve Savona as finance minister. That evening, Conte announced he was giving up the post of prime minister.

On May 28, Mattarella then appointed Carlo Cottarelli, the International Monetary Fund's former fiscal affairs chief, to head Italy's caretaker government.

Luigi Di Maio, leader of the Five Star Movement, and Matteo Salvini, leader of the League, reacted angrily to the decision, calling on supporters to hold peaceful demonstrations on June 2 and to hang Italian flags from their windows in protest against the president. They also said they would not support a technocratic cabinet formed by Cottarelli. Di Maio and others even proposed impeaching Mattarella — unheard of in Italian history — though they later withdrew the proposal.

French President Emmanuel Macron praised Mattarella, saying he "showed an extremely high sense of responsibility in protecting Italy's institutions and democratic stability." German Chancellor Angela Merkel said she was willing to work with any government Italy forms, but that the country's economic policy must comply with the requirements of eurozone rules.

According to Bloomberg, Cottarelli has accepted the appointment as prime minister and will call a general election. If the new government fails to win a confidence vote, the election will be held this coming August; if the new government wins votes in both the lower house and the senate, the election will take place in early 2019.

Italy's last general election was in March of this year.

[According to Wallstreetcn](https://wallstreetcn.com/articles/3323006), Moody's warned in a statement on the 28th that because the new "accord" of Italy's governing coalition lacks fiscal constraints and could delay or overturn important previous reforms, such as pension reform, Italy could be downgraded from its current Baa2 rating (the second-lowest investment-grade rating at Moody's) to Baa3 (still investment grade), or possibly even to junk.

**[China and the US clash over whether China forces technology transfer](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-usa-trade-wto/u-s-and-china-clash-over-technology-transfer-at-wto-idUSKCN1IT214)**. The US and Chinese ambassadors to the World Trade Organization clashed on the 28th at the WTO over the issue of "technology transfer." US Ambassador to the WTO Dennis Shea pointed out that China's administrative regulations force foreign companies that want to do business with China to share technology, and that Chinese government officials can exploit vague investment rules to require foreign companies to carry out technology transfers. China's ambassador to the WTO, Zhang Xiangchen, denied that China engages in "forced technology transfer," calling the US claim a "presumption of guilt" with "no evidence."

### Some People Say

> **"The Spider-Man of the 18th arrondissement!"**

Malian migrant Mamoudou Gassama is about to receive French citizenship — for a rather special reason.

Last year, the 22-year-old Mamoudou Gassama set out from Mali by boat, crossed the Mediterranean to Italy, and then entered France. Last Saturday (May 26), while walking in Paris's 18th arrondissement, he saw a crowd gathered in front of an apartment building, a four-year-old child hanging from a balcony outside, so he climbed up the exterior of the building to the fourth floor and rescued the boy.

Videos filmed by onlookers and uploaded to the internet sparked wide discussion. Paris Mayor Anne Hidalgo called Gassama "the Spider-Man of the 18th arrondissement." On Monday of this week (May 28), French President Emmanuel Macron also received Gassama at the Élysée Palace, thanking him and awarding him a medal for bravery, while promising to grant him French citizenship and arrange a position for him in the fire service.

> **"This no longer has anything to do with truck drivers. What people are protesting is corruption."**

To protest soaring fuel prices, Brazilian truck drivers have been on strike for a week, paralyzing the country, with estimated economic losses reaching $2.8 billion.

In 2016, state-owned Petrobras decided to peg domestic fuel prices to the international market, causing fuel prices to soar 50% within a year.

On the 24th, representatives of the Brazilian government, Petrobras, and the truck drivers' unions met and reached an agreement to cut diesel prices by 10%, switch from daily adjustments to holding prices unchanged for 30 consecutive days, split the costs equally between the government and the state oil company, and require the truck drivers to end the strike immediately.

But the strike continued, with food and fuel shortages appearing across the country, factories halting production, transportation disrupted, and schools forced to suspend classes.

On the 27th, President Michel Temer finally relented, announcing in a televised address that diesel prices would be cut to 0.46 reais per liter (about $0.13), with the reduction maintained for 60 days.

The above was the reply of a strike supporter [in an interview with The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/05/28/world/americas/brazil-truckers-strike-economy.html).

### You Might Also Be Interested In

**[EU plans to put 10 kinds of single-use plastic products on a "blacklist"](http://www.chinanews.com/gj/2018/05-29/8524860.shtml).**

According to the China News Service, the EU published a draft proposal on May 28 to ban 10 kinds of single-use plastic products, including plastic tableware (straws, knives and forks, etc.), cotton swabs, balloons and their holders, plastic food containers, plastic cups, plastic bottles, cigarette butts, plastic bags, chip bags (including candy wrappers), and wet wipes.

The draft says these 10 single-use plastic products currently account for 43% of marine litter in Europe, while fishing gear containing plastic components accounts for 27% of Europe's marine litter — 70% combined, making them the most common plastic waste on European beaches.

The plan proposes "categorized management" of this plastic waste, with the strictest treatment proposed for plastic tableware, cotton swabs, and balloon holders: an outright ban.

For plastic food containers and plastic cups, the plan proposes sharply reducing market consumption; for plastic bottles, it requires EU member states to reach a 90% recycling target by 2025. For balloons, cigarette butts, plastic bags, chip bags, wet wipes, and even fishing gear, the plan pressures manufacturers to fund cleanup, recycling, waste disposal, environmental awareness campaigns, and other work, and also requires changes to the outer packaging of balloons and wet wipes to clearly indicate their environmental impact and proper methods of waste disposal.

Currently, half of the plastic collected and sorted within the EU is shipped abroad, 85% of it exported to China.

**[Japan's pet insurance market is growing at nearly 20% a year](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/realtime/world/story20180528-862743)**.

Japan's nationwide pet insurance penetration rate is only around 7%, lagging behind countries such as the UK and Norway. But according to Lianhe Zaobao, the size of Japan's pet insurance market had reached 49 billion yen by the end of March last year, an increase of nearly 20%.

In 2017, of all pets kept across Japan, there were 8.92 million dogs and 9.52 million cats. Because pets have no public insurance system, medical costs that owners must bear in full have become a worry for pet owners. Beyond blood transfusions and blood tests, diagnosis using MRI machines is also spreading, driving medical costs ever higher.

Competition among insurance companies has grown increasingly fierce. Japan's Anicom Insurance launched a product covering 70% of medical costs, partnering with more than 6,000 hospitals where insurance payment can be used at the counter. ipet Insurance has also launched a similar flagship product. Japan's Rakuten has also entered this market by acquiring a small-amount short-term insurance company.
