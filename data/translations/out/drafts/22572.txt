The 2016 Stockholm Furniture Fair has just wrapped up, and pieces that fully embody the essence of Nordic design — along with people's pursuit of higher-quality home living — made frequent appearances. We previously dedicated a feature to [the story of Danish furniture design brand HAY](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19432.html); riding on the buzz of the Stockholm fair, let's take a look at the most active design brands on the Scandinavian peninsula.

## Artek

Legend has it that in Finland, nearly every household owns a piece by Artek. The brand was founded in 1935 by Finnish architect and interior designer Alvar Aalto, and what it manufactures is mostly furniture of his own design. The style established from the very beginning — an emphasis on naturalness fused with Alvar Aalto's architectural thinking — comes through as resolutely simple. He excelled at creating the widest range of furniture choices from the fewest materials and the cleanest forms; Aalto believed the soul of design comes from all of its basic components. Some even say that Artek's furniture, made from traditional Finnish birch, is practically synonymous with Nordic design — it simply feels like furniture.

In September 2013, the Swiss furniture giant Vitra acquired the Finnish brand, though it continues to operate as an independent entity.

## Menu

This Danish brand was founded in 1979, and we often feature its individual pieces in our *Great Design* column. Look closely and you'll find that Menu has the coolness and composure typical of Scandinavian design — pieces that combine utility, quality, and beauty. The crisp yet elegant forms make it a textured home accessory even when it just sits there, unused.

Menu takes an open attitude toward design, giving designers ample room for imagination. Whether in material or form, designers are left free to experiment and explore, and many of the works reflect an innovation that challenges the existing shapes of everyday objects.

Take The Yeh side table, for instance — it solves the eternal spatial problem of round tables being unable to sit flush against a wall.

![The Yeh Wall Table by Kenyon Yeh, 2014](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702132834fdXerOAEh1RFKUjs.jpg-w600)

Or this wine set called "WW Carafe": its shape went through more than 20 design iterations before being finalized, all in pursuit of the perfect trajectory for pouring wine.

## ferm LIVING

The young Danish brand ferm LIVING was founded in 2005. Its founder, Trine Anderson, was once a graphic designer who, while renovating her own home, couldn't find wallpaper that was both good-looking and affordable — so she designed a series of wallpapers herself, and in the process, founded ferm LIVING.

It's a very typical Nordic design brand: clean, crisp lines and shapes with a bright, warm palette. Compared with furniture brands active in the Nordic scene such as HAY, Menu, Normann Copenhagen, and Muuto, ferm LIVING prefers to weave patterned effects into its furniture and accessory designs — hardly unrelated to the founder's graphic design background.

## Iittala

The Finnish brand Iittala, founded in 1881, has a history of more than 135 years. Its name comes from a small town 50 kilometers north of Helsinki, Finland's capital. In Finland, Iittala glassware is a double guarantee of aesthetics and quality.

Unlike many Nordic home brands, Iittala focuses on the design of blown and pressed glassware, using polishing, lacquering, and engraving techniques to leave behind quite a few enduring masterpieces.

One example is the Lantern series, created in 1999 by Iittala's current global design director Harri Koskinen. The candle holder's lines are crisp and clean, and candlelight shining through its exquisitely designed mouth-blown glass seems to float in mid-air. It became a permanent collection of MoMA, New York's Museum of Modern Art. In 2013, the Lantern series was redesigned as a plug-in table lamp — Harri wanted to bring more light into a space.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702132839iDyLXQ7ROeUfr592.jpg-w600)

The Kartio geometric series, created by Kaj Franck in 1958, is a study in pure, minimalist pitcher and tumbler design, fully demonstrating Kaj Franck's perfect balance in commanding material and geometric form. Kartio's rich colors also highlight Iittala's leading technique in colored glassware.

## Fredericia

The 1950s were the golden age of Nordic modern furniture, and Andreas Graversen caught the wave: he acquired Fredericia, founded in the 1910s and on the verge of collapse, persuaded the renowned Danish furniture designer Børge Mogensen to join the company, and in short order turned it into one of Denmark's most beloved furniture manufacturers.

From 1955 to 1972, Børge Mogensen designed a series of new furniture that recast certain Danish classical furniture styles as appropriate modern design elements. The Spanish Chair, developed in 1958, remains one of Fredericia's best-known products to this day.

![The Spanish Chair by Børge Mogensen](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702132841AVxcTybOPa1g6ZdC.jpg-w600)

In the 1990s, Nanna Ditzel became the company's chief designer; her Trinidad Chair won an award in the United States and sales soared.

## offecct

This is a Swedish design company, founded in 1990 by Kurt Tingdal and Anders Englund. [offecct](http://www.offecct.se/en) mainly produces furniture, with an emphasis on combining local craft traditions with product longevity. This idea of durability also extends to environmental sustainability — for example, using renewable materials to make furniture.

In 2015, they created the OFFECCT LifeCircle® concept, whose core is extending the lifespan of products. That extension goes beyond producing durable furniture to include the reuse of old furniture. That said, durable, long-lasting furniture has always been a hallmark of Nordic design.

## Normann Copenhagen

The Copenhagen design brand [Normann Copenhagen](http://www.normann-copenhagen.com/) was founded in 1999 by designers Jan Andersen and Poul Madsen. Its first product, however, was Norm 69 — the "pinecone lamp" — released in 2002. It requires self-assembly: you slot 69 foil pieces together like building blocks, no adhesive tools needed — quite ingenious. Light radiates from the lamp through the foil pieces into the room, and the lamp itself forms layered patterns of light and shadow. Many of their lamps also look pieced together like building blocks, full of childlike whimsy.

![Norm 69 by Simon Karkov](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160702132846k9IEdm6v0HG8wAfj.jpg-w600)

Normann Copenhagen's product range is now quite rich — furniture, lighting, and accessories — with interesting ideas constantly on view, simple yet not simplistic. Lively colors give it extra vitality, making it well suited to young people.

## Muuto

Also from Copenhagen, the brand [Muuto](http://www.muuto.com) is more devoted to carrying on the Nordic design tradition. Founded in 2006, it is one of the brands that have been active internationally in recent years. "Muuto" derives from the Finnish word "muutos," meaning change. Although the designers they collaborate with come mostly from Sweden, Norway, Finland, and Denmark, the brand pursues looking at Nordic design from a new perspective.

Muuto's products fall into furniture, lighting, and accessories, and it works with more than 30 designers. They inherit the traditional Nordic emphasis on utility: extremely minimal forms, a focus on practical function, and most pieces available in a range of colors — very flexible.

## Wästberg

The Swedish lighting brand [Wästberg](http://www.wastberg.com) has been appearing frequently at trade shows in recent years and racking up awards, building partnerships with internationally renowned designers such as David Chipperfield, Inga Sempé, and Nendo. But it is actually a young brand, founded in 2008. Founder Magnus Wästberg spent a long career in lighting, and on the website he wrote an extraordinarily romantic manifesto.

Wästberg's lamps clearly inherit the Nordic style: minimalist, geometric forms with the occasional lively color that looks unremarkable at first glance. As with their lamp names — a W followed by a number — it's a very traditional, unpretentious approach. Still, Wästberg always has small detail improvements that make its lamps more practical: a clip on the lamp base, for instance, or plenty of USB ports, and nendo's lampshade can be used on its own as a container for odds and ends.

## &tradition

The Danish brand [&tradition](http://www.andtradition.com), lately active at all the major design fairs, is actually a newcomer less than 6 years old. Its name expresses the brand's reverence for Nordic tradition — and it values innovation just as much. We previously covered the "palette table" that Spanish designer Jaime Hayon and Italian designer Luca Nichetto created together: three pieces made of different materials, each distinct in material, shape, and color, arranged at varying heights to achieve a clever balance.

![“Palette Table” by Jaime Hayon & Luca Nichetto](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201607021328536V30Yunp1xKWthLg.jpg-w600)

There's also a pendant lamp that references the old-fashioned lamps once used at Copenhagen's docks. Its shade, made of arched steel, tapers downward and inward, blending a flavor of the past with modern design sensibility. This pendant lamp comes from the Danish design studio Space Copenhagen. Although &tradition's positioning is not limited to whether a designer is Nordic or a style is Nordic, you can still see simplicity, elegance, and exquisite craft in its work.
