### **What agenda-setters are talking about**

[**The New Yorker: Will the voyeuristic screen-scrolling game on social media be the future of television?**](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2018/06/18/skam-the-radical-teen-drama-that-unfolds-one-post-at-a-time)

At 3:40 p.m. on April 24, *SKAM Austin* released its first episode on Facebook. No ads, no promotional campaign from the production team, no credits. It dropped like an ordinary update in the feed. The story follows a group of high school students in Texas, and episodes vary in length—some as short as two minutes, "long" ones up to eight minutes. The release schedule syncs with the plot—and with real high school life: you might watch the on-screen couple argue at school at the very same time, 12:40 p.m. on a Monday, or see a student pick out party outfits before a Saturday-night party.

At its core, *SKAM Austin* is no different from other TV dramas about the emotional troubles of high schoolers, but the producers layered in hidden plotlines that turn it into a massive voyeuristic detective game. Every character has an Instagram account, and by tracking those accounts you can dig into their carefully curated—and occasionally incriminating—pasts to analyze the subtle dynamics between characters. If you follow these accounts, they sometimes follow you back; Facebook and Instagram keep pushing you real-time alerts: "abby_taffy just posted a photo." All of this creates an immersive experience that blurs the boundary between fictional and real social networks.

Traditional television is a one-way street. The actors and audiences on either side of the screen are static, never interfering with each other, while producers strain to guess viewers' tastes and viewing habits. This model held for more than 50 years, but in the age of YouTube, Facebook, and Twitter it is falling behind. Young people hardly watch TV anymore, instead spending nine hours a day on online media. *SKAM* emerged in response: it caters to ever-shrinking attention spans while using suspense and multiple entry points to deliver a thrilling sense of participation. Of course, it also caters to the platform's marketing needs—for Facebook, what better ad is there than a TV drama in which everyone uses Facebook?

The cultural significance of *SKAM* lies in the fact that viewers are not just watchers but also creators—the production team adjusts plot details based on fan comments. Viewers feel it is less an alternate reality than an extension of their own lives.

Beyond the plot and the presentation, every step of *SKAM*'s planning and distribution is tailor-made for young people. The Norwegian original's production team knew young people had flocked to social networks, so they made social networks the setting of the drama; they knew young people didn't want their parents to see what they were posting, so they deliberately skipped public advertising and relied on word-of-mouth marketing. The strategy worked—in Norway, more young people know *SKAM* than know *Game of Thrones*.

The New Yorker writer notes that like every other "killer app," *SKAM*'s ultimate purpose is still to make money. Creator Julie Andem admits they did try to maximize audience engagement, but she insists the point was not to make money—it was to help young Americans feel less alone.

Although the phone seems a more democratic, less didactic medium than television, *SKAM* has not entirely escaped its public-TV lineage (the Norwegian producer NRK is known as Norway's BBC). It still tries to educate its audience. Its message remains: keep working at it and things will eventually turn around; shame is temporary, growth is permanent; young people should build confidence and become independent individuals.

### **News-makers of the last 24 hours**

[**Mexico, Canada, and the United States win hosting rights for the 2026 World Cup—and the World Cup is expanding.**](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-soccer-worldcup-fifa-2026/mexico-canada-welcome-hosting-2026-world-cup-with-u-s-despite-frayed-ties-idUSKBN1J91CG)

On Wednesday, FIFA voted to make the decision in Moscow, the capital of this year's World Cup host, Russia. It is the first time in World Cup history that three countries will co-host. With the U.S., Canada, and Mexico locked in trade and diplomatic disputes, winning the joint hosting rights gives the three countries an occasion to stand together.

The U.S. is the main venue. Under the arrangements, of the 80 matches in total, the U.S. will host 60, the other two countries 10 each, and the final is set for MetLife Stadium in New Jersey. The U.S. and Mexico respectively hosted the men's World Cup in 1994 and 1986, while Canada has hosted the women's World Cup.

The three countries' leaders each expressed their welcome of the vote. Canadian Prime Minister Trudeau told reporters: "The whole world will have the chance to come together and witness the close cooperation among Canada, Mexico, and the United States."

U.S. President Trump, meanwhile, promised that strict visa restrictions will not apply to fans coming to attend the matches. Reuters reminds readers that even if he wins a second term, he will leave office in early 2025. During the bidding for the World Cup, Trump had threatened that any country voting against the North American bid would face consequences in trade negotiations.

The three countries promise the tournament can bring FIFA $11 billion in revenue.

News closer to home: the 2026 World Cup will expand from the current 32 teams to 48. The teams will be divided into 16 groups, with the top two in each group advancing to the knockout stage. [It is believed](http://www.skysports.com/football/news/12040/11403653/world-cup-2026-three-hosts-48-teams-and-80-games-how-will-it-work) that the regional quotas for the 48 teams are: Europe 16, Africa 9, Asia 8.5, South America 6, Central America and the Caribbean 6.5, Oceania 1, and 1 among the hosts.

### **What else happened in the world**

[**France's National Assembly on Wednesday passed the controversial rail reform bill for the state railway SNCF with a large majority**](https://www.afp.com/en/news/205/french-mps-adopt-signature-macron-rail-reform-doc-15w9f91), marking a key victory for President Macron in his showdown with the unions. Analysts have compared this contest to Margaret Thatcher's battle with British coal miners in the 1980s. The bill goes to the Senate for a vote on Thursday.

[**The U.S. Justice Department plans to bring more civil rights cases to court to counter discriminatory treatment of houses of worship by some municipal bodies.**](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-usa-justice-sessions-religion/u-s-justice-dept-targets-discrimination-against-houses-of-worship-idUSKBN1J92OM) This is seen as the Trump administration's latest move in support of religious freedom.

[**84 scientists jointly published an article in Nature stating that Antarctic glaciers are melting three times faster than before.**](https://www.afp.com/en/news/205/antarctic-ice-loss-triples-boosting-sea-levels-doc-15w83r5) Two-fifths of the ice loss has occurred in the past five years. The scientists believe global warming already poses a real threat to coastal regions.

[**A proposal by venture capitalist Tim Draper to split California into three states will go to a vote alongside this year's elections.**](https://www.apnews.com/231f0536806c496c9c6bacc0bbce4929/Bold-California-initiative-to-split-state-in-3-faces-hurdles) California is the most populous U.S. state, with nearly 40 million people. The initiative requires dividing California into northern, central, and southern parts to make it easier to govern. Democratic gubernatorial candidates have announced their opposition to the initiative.

### **Someone said**

> [**"It's like returning to the world of the living. At this moment, I feel completely free."**](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/jun/13/italian-mp-piera-aiello-mafia-threat-reveals-her-face-for-first-time)

Piera Aiello, 51, is an Italian politician. At 14, she was forced to marry the son of a Sicilian mafia boss. In 1991, her husband was killed by a rival mafia clan. She then entered the witness protection program, changed her name, left Sicily, and never appeared in public again.

Last year, Aiello decided to take back control of her life, running for parliament as a candidate of the anti-establishment Five Star Movement. During the campaign, she had to cover her real face with a veil, earning the nickname "the faceless candidate." After winning, she began working in Rome, where conditions could no longer guarantee the protection of her real identity. So she decided to remove the veil and face the public.

"I decided to stand up, so that people who resist the mafia would no longer be marginalized," Aiello said.

Fighting the mafia is Aiello's main political cause. In recent years, efforts to curb the mafia have achieved some results, but organized crime remains a serious problem both in Italy and abroad. It is believed that cocaine smuggling into Europe is dominated by a mafia. Some informants, after fleeing overseas, are still hunted by the mafia. Aiello's victory has made them start dreaming of the future again.

> [**"Until the truth comes out, my heart will not be at peace."**](https://edition.cnn.com/2018/06/13/uk/grenfell-tower-fire-no-justice-intl/index.html)

In the Grenfell Tower fire in London a year ago, Ethiopian immigrant Paulos Tekle lost his son. June 14 marks the first anniversary of the fire. The criminal investigation is still ongoing, and Londoners are calling for the truth to be established and for those responsible to be held accountable.

The party most directly responsible is the building's contractor. Experts found that the cladding material on the building's exterior did not meet construction standards; when the building caught fire, these materials released toxic smoke, preventing firefighters from getting close to residents to carry out rescues.

This raised questions about the fire-response system. Britain uses a passive fire-response approach: during the Grenfell Tower fire, residents were first told to stay in their flats, and only two hours later did they finally receive the order to evacuate. The fire service argued that the faulty decisions stemmed from firefighters not knowing about the defects in the building materials and underestimating the difficulty of the rescue—not from flaws in the fire-response mechanism itself.

More criticism points to the Kensington and Chelsea borough council and the tenant management organization (TMO). The building's former residents believe these two official bodies had long ignored safety hazards inside the building and must be held responsible for this man-made disaster.

### **You might also be interested in**

[**Mexicans will now be able to write in candidates' nicknames in the presidential election.**](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/jun/13/mexico-presidential-election-write-in-candidate-nicknames)

Less than three weeks before Mexico's general election, the country's electoral commission changed the rules: in addition to checking the box next to a printed candidate's name, nicknames, abbreviations, and campaign slogans written in by voters will also count as valid. Opponents argue this could cause chaos in the vote-counting process.

Many Mexican politicians are known by their nicknames: current president Andrés Manuel López Obrador is usually called "Amlo" and, because of the literal meaning of his name, is also named after a local fish. He is also keen on giving opponents nicknames, and these nicknames have gone viral on social networks. A political science professor points out that Amlo's packaging and marketing skills are on par with Trump's.

That said, the election result seems hardly in doubt. Although many people hope the country will change in its fight against drug violence and political corruption, the current president Amlo still leads in all polls by a wide margin.

[**On the eve of the World Cup opening, the Spanish football federation sacked the national team's head coach.**](http://www.espn.com/soccer/spain/story/3524002/julen-lopetegui-sacked-by-spain-before-world-cup-after-taking-real-madrid-job)

As Spain's national team head coach, Lopetegui (Julen Lopetegui) had never lost a match; his work was exemplary. Not long before, he had just renewed his contract with the Spanish federation. But just two days before the World Cup kicked off, Real Madrid suddenly announced that Lopetegui would take over the club after the World Cup, signing for three seasons. This move enraged the federation.

"We were forced to fire the national team's coach," said Rubiales, president of the Spanish federation, at a Wednesday press conference. "We wish him all the best; he did an excellent job in the qualifiers. But an employee can't leave the federation out of the loop while looking for his next job, keeping us in the dark until five minutes before the announcement."

The federation then announced that Fernando Hierro, a former national team and Real Madrid defender, would take over as head coach. His first match in charge will be Friday night's group game against Portugal.
