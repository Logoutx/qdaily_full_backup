We compared brand marketing during China's Spring Festival Gala and the American Super Bowl, and came away with some interesting findings.

In recent years viewers have spent CCTV's Spring Festival Gala mocking it as they watch, but this year's show hit a new low in word of mouth — many people didn't even bother coming up with jokes anymore, simply summing up their feelings with the one word "awkward."

In the QDaily Research Lab's survey, "What do you want to gripe about at the Spring Festival Gala?" we received nearly 10,000 responses. The top three: "It's just background music — everyone is grabbing red envelopes," "Feng Gong's sketch, as expected, unloaded a pile of catchphrases," and "political correctness." On Weibo's trending topics chart, #SpringFestivalGalaGripeCenter# racked up 1.53 million interactions — but that's modest next to the 350 million for *The Mermaid*, the film released today, or the 100-million-plus attention figures for the Jiangsu Satellite TV and Beijing TV Spring Festival galas that aired one after another.

Young people stopped expecting anything from the Gala long ago, but this is the first time there wasn't even a single surprise.

Seven and a half hours after the Spring Festival Gala ended, the Super Bowl — America's "Spring Festival Gala" — kicked off its championship game. The showdown was between the Carolina Panthers and the Denver Broncos, and the halftime show sandwiched between the two halves featured the British band Coldplay, Beyoncé, and Bruno Mars, the man known as "the Mars guy." The scene was on fire.

The halftime show is one of the Super Bowl's highlights, as we discussed in yesterday's piece on the Super Bowl's 50-year history.

Our [detailed report on the Super Bowl yesterday](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/22146.html) already explained that it can't simply be summed up as America's "Spring Festival Gala." Still, in terms of sheer attention, the words "Spring Festival Gala" convey its status in America more intuitively.

Put the CCTV Spring Festival Gala and the Super Bowl side by side and the stylistic differences are plain to see. But the two are very similar in one respect — both are the year's marquee brand event in their home country, and simply making an appearance is often taken as a sign of a company's or brand's strength. That is exactly the point of this article.

The reason advertisers are willing to splash out is, of course, the ratings. According to data released by CCTV.com, 690 million people still watched the Year of the Monkey Gala in front of their TV sets — though how much that figure is really worth is open to question. This year's Super Bowl ratings haven't been released yet, but given that it has set a new record every year, they presumably won't be far off. In 2015, 114 million people in the U.S. watched the Super Bowl — meaning that 74 out of every 100 Americans watching TV at that hour were tuned to the game.

In absolute terms the Super Bowl can't beat the Spring Festival Gala (that's a matter of population), but at a time when television as a medium is being written off in both the U.S. and China, the Gala's ratings have been declining for years while the Super Bowl keeps setting records. Either way, neither is short of advertisers — and when we put the two advertiser lists side by side, we found some interesting things.

Right after the 7:30 p.m. *Xinwen Lianbo* ended, ads began running nonstop. But the real big spenders were the ones that squeezed into the final ten minutes of the countdown. Auctioned off last November, these slots were topped by LeEco's 30-second ad at 70 million yuan. Midea, Meizu, and Gujing Gong (liquor) took the three countdown slots; although their final prices weren't disclosed, rumor has it they exceeded 100 million yuan. Beyond those, Jomoo, BAIC Huansu, Alipay, China Pacific Insurance, 360 Search, Bank of Beijing, Feihe milk powder, Telunsu, WiFi Master Key, Gome, Suning.com, Yadea electric scooters, Xiang Piao Piao, and Bybo Dental all made an appearance.

The Super Bowl, meanwhile, featured 43 brands. Besides regulars like Budweiser, Coca-Cola, and Pepsi, first-time entrants included Amazon, Taco Bell under Yum! Brands, the Pokémon games, and the health juice drink Bai — all stepping into this premium arena where a 30-second slot costs US$5 million (which makes the Super Bowl look a lot cheaper than the Spring Festival Gala). According to data from Prosper Insights and Analytics, 17.7% of people consider these ads more important than the championship game itself.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/2016060711544990cH7QKDohg5AEmI.gif-w600Gif)

## **The internet and autos: the main forces on these two 'battlefields'**

When half the audience tunes in to the Super Bowl for the ads, the only thing people seem to remember from our Spring Festival Gala's opening ads is the danmu (bullet comments). That feeling only deepens after sitting through five hours of the "Spring (xin) Festival (wen) Gala (lian) (bo)" — the parenthetical syllables spelling out "news broadcast."

Setting ad creativity aside, what message does the advertiser roster of the "world's most-watched, longest-running, most-staffed gala" send?

Meizu, LeEco, 360, and Shanda's "WiFi Master Key" all clustered in the golden slots of the Gala countdown. Internet companies once again led this year's ad war — much like last year — from which you can see how hot the internet has been these past few years.

360 appeared twice before the Gala began, the first internet company to show up — and what it pushed was neither Qiku phones nor 360 Safeguard, but 360 Search, which became a standalone brand last January.

360 hopes to use the CCTV platform to boost brand awareness as quickly as possible — still the Spring Festival Gala's greatest value for advertisers. According to iiMedia Research's *2015 Q3 China Mobile Search Market Report*, beyond Baidu, which holds the largest share, Shenma Search commands 17.4% while 360 Search holds just 4.2%, with competitors like QQ and UC also eyeing the mobile market hungrily.

Huang Zhang had previously declared on Weibo, "If Meizu fans really want Meizu to advertise at the Spring Festival Gala, I'll do it if this gets more than 100,000 reposts." He kept his word: Meizu's ad ran three times before the Gala began.

As we all know, the closer an opening ad runs to airtime, the more precious it is. Meizu landed the platinum slot this year, second only to the two Gala-ad veterans Midea and Gujing Gong liquor — not even counting Meizu's placement in the bottom-right corner of the 10:30 p.m. public service ad. Alipay and Mengdian also appeared in the pre-show ads.

On the Super Bowl side, the auto industry — a prolific source of ad dollars — came flocking back this year. Acura, GM, and Honda all returned after sitting out the 2015 (or even earlier) Super Bowl, each bringing its key models.

Acura, for instance, would show off its new NSX supercar in an ad for the first time; GM brought the Buick brand to the Super Bowl for the first time; and Honda bought a 60-second spot to push the 2017 Ridgeline pickup.

Automakers have always been Super Bowl regulars, but after the 2008 financial crisis Detroit's car companies were badly hurt, while Korean and Japanese brands spent on ads with more confidence. As the NFL's new official sponsor, Hyundai went big, snapping up two 30-second ads in the first half, a 60-second ad before kickoff, and another 60-second ad in the pre-game show. Toyota and Kia are also familiar Super Bowl faces in recent years.

Besides cars, food and beverage brands are also Super Bowl regulars — Coca-Cola, Doritos, Pepsi, Snickers, and the like appear almost every year.

Correspondingly, another category of Spring Festival Gala ad spender is home appliances and milk — Midea and Mengniu, for example.

## Internet companies at the Spring Festival Gala are busy one-upping each other and defining themselves; those at the Super Bowl are already facing the future

Xiaomi, once rumored to appear at the Gala, didn't show up as expected; of the internet companies that did appear, Meizu and LeEco had already traded barbs beforehand.

Last November, after CCTV's annual ad auction, LeEco snapped up the only 30-second slot for 70 million yuan. Meizu CEO Huang Zhang then voiced mild displeasure on his personal Weibo, claiming that if more than 100,000 Meizu fans wanted the company on the Gala, it could be done. LeEco Superphone's official Weibo replied: **"Even with 100,000 reposts, gold slot or platinum, the first Spring Festival Gala ad of the year belongs to LeEco. Whether making products or appearing at the Gala, LeEco settles for nothing but first place. Welcome to try and keep up."**

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160726151103drKWcFDw4hBeLRy0.jpg-w600)

The spat ended with Meizu taking the second-to-last platinum ad slot (which looked like a win). And the ad that finally aired pulled out plenty of gimmicks: it billed itself as the Gala's first danmu-style shot, developed the creative together with its own users, highlighted that the company has been around for 12 years, that its 2015 sales topped 20 million units, and threw in its once-popular catchphrase — "craftsman spirit."

2015 went quite smoothly for Meizu: sales surged on the back of the Meilan Note 2 launched in 2014, and in February it took a 3.6 billion yuan investment from Alibaba. Riding high, it also faced old rival Xiaomi and new rival LeEco, so Meizu was eager to tell consumers one thing: it's the stronger one.

Rival LeEco's ad, meanwhile, emphasized self-definition, which had to do with its January 12 logo change and the reshuffling of its organizational chart. Facing longstanding criticism of its exaggerated, over-the-top marketing, LeEco summed itself up as "just a dream laughed at by 99% of people." It brought in Zhang Yimou and Liu Jianhong, already signed to LeEco, plus the closely allied Guo Jingming and Jia Yueting himself, to shoot the ad. In the official version aired on CCTV, however, Liu Jianhong's shots were cut — one wonders whether that had anything to do with his former identity as a CCTV football commentator. Liu left CCTV in 2014 to become chief content officer of LeEco Sports.

At the Super Bowl, internet companies have hardly been absent in recent years, but most of their ads express a judgment about the future.

Take Amazon, a first-timer this year: its ad was mainly about its Echo smart speaker — new hardware containing Alexa, a voice assistant in the vein of Siri and Cortana. Then there was PayPal, which believes it has created an emerging class culture of the future — no longer nouveau riche, but a new consumer group enjoying more comprehensive financial services: the millennials.

## On paper LeEco bid the highest at the Spring Festival Gala, and Budweiser spent the most at the Super Bowl

LeEco's 70 million yuan won it the top-bidder crown at the 2016 CCTV Spring Festival Gala, taking the only 30-second slot. The feat itself became a talking point.

Apart from LeEco, every opening ad at the Spring Festival Gala ran 15 seconds. Super Bowl ads, which love to tell stories, typically use the full 60 seconds. With demand outstripping supply, CBS this year had to turn down many 30-second orders and mainly sold 60-second slots.

Budweiser has always been one of the Super Bowl's biggest ad spenders. This year it bought up 180 seconds of airtime, running one ad each for Budweiser, Bud Light, and Shock Top. Budweiser's Super Bowl ad spending this year is estimated at US$30 million — though that's slightly down from last year's 210 seconds (US$4.5 million per 30 seconds) and the 240 seconds of the year before.

![Bud Light's special Super Bowl can](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201606071154507TQkqIbXw6JKodu1.jpg-w600)

A survey of nearly 40,000 U.S. consumers showed that NFL Super Bowl ads lifted audiences' fondness for the advertised products by 6.6% — the lift was even higher in prime time — and viewers' fondness for Budweiser rose 37.8% after last year's Super Bowl.

As the Super Bowl's national brand, Budweiser also signed a strategic partnership agreement with NFL China (the National Football League) on the eve of Super Bowl 50, running through 2022, to bring this American national sport to more Chinese viewers.

According to CVSC-Sofres Media (CSM) statistics, by the end of 2014 the Chinese audience interested in the NFL and American football had reached 17.7 million — a figure that had grown tenfold over the previous five years. These viewers are mainly active in China's first- and second-tier cities.

More than 17 domestic media platforms livestreamed the Super Bowl this year — did you watch?

## **Spring Festival Gala advertisers are increasingly local — where have the international big names gone?**

Jomoo, BAIC Huansu, Alipay, China Pacific Insurance, 360 Search, Bank of Beijing, Feihe milk powder, Telunsu, WiFi Master Key, Gome, Suning.com, Yadea electric scooters, Xiang Piao Piao, Bybo Dental, LeEco, Midea, Meizu, Gujing Gong liquor...

Budweiser and McDonald's, which appeared at both the Super Bowl and China's Spring Festival Gala last year, were nowhere to be seen; Samsung Galaxy and Jeep, which had squeezed into the last dozen minutes before last year's Gala opening, were gone too. Compared with previous years, this year's pre-show ad time was entirely taken over by domestic brands.

The Spring Festival Gala keeps getting less watchable, and its pull on young people declines year by year. It's also true that [multinationals are tightening their ad budgets](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19998.html) and paying more attention to segmented marketing for different brands. The new top bidder in CCTV's 2016 ad auction was a dedicated P2P lending platform, "Jilongdai," while P&G — which had taken the top-bid crown three years running a decade earlier — was nowhere to be found near the top of this list.

Midea, meanwhile, is the undisputed national brand of the CCTV Spring Festival Gala, having held the midnight countdown ad slot for 15 consecutive years. That slot — like the Meizu and Gujing Gong ads ahead of the countdown — is a non-bidded position obtained through a strategic partnership with CCTV, meaning other companies couldn't necessarily land it no matter how much money they threw in.

According to available data, the midnight time-announcement ad cost 6.8 million yuan in 2005; in 2007 it rose 5.4-fold to 37.09 million yuan; in 2011 it went for 57.2 million yuan.

Looked at another way, most Super Bowl advertisers are domestic companies too — except they've long since become multinationals.

## **Some subtle coincidences among the new faces that squeezed into the Super Bowl and the Spring Festival Gala**

Like the Spring Festival Gala, the Super Bowl has unexpected new faces every year. Po from *Kung Fu Panda* became the "big winner" who scored at both the Super Bowl and the Gala. Wix.com, the Israeli personal website-building service, chose this Chinese panda with American genes for its second Super Bowl appearance.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160607115451T9qQ3nkcZ5WdYjuO.gif-w600Gif)

Riding the domestic box-office heat of *Kung Fu Panda 3*, FAW-Volkswagen's all-new Bora — a strategic partner of the film — also ran a custom ad at the Spring Festival Gala.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/2016060711545275YltGOp6ICFz01i.gif-w600Gif)

In the full-length versions of both ads, extensive animation was used to insert the product into the original footage. Apart from the slightly jarring Chinese dubbing, the pairing of "Bora" and "Po" — "only those with kung fu deserve a car with kung fu" — looked perfectly natural.

Pokémon made its Super Bowl debut to celebrate its 20th birthday, with production quality, pacing, and music all suitably rousing. Bank of Beijing also made a well-timed move at the Spring Festival Gala — this year marks its 20th anniversary as well.

You know, lesser-known brands always have one reason or another for getting onto the Spring Festival Gala or the Super Bowl (flush companies, by contrast, need no reason).

At this year's Spring Festival Gala, Bybo Dental took the ad slot second only to LeEco's, backed by Lenovo's 1 billion yuan investment. A few weeks earlier, 71-year-old Liu Chuanzhi made a special trip to attend the private dental group's forum-slash-annual-meeting, stating, "Investing in Bybo Dental is Legend Holdings' entry point into healthcare." With Bybo Dental set to accelerate its expansion in 2016, choosing this moment for a Gala appearance was only natural.

And Avocados from Mexico, the trade association that debuted at last year's Super Bowl to great effect, came back this year with another creative ad, which we included in this year's [Super Bowl ad creativity roundup](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/22108.html).

This has to do with avocados becoming trendy in the U.S. in recent years. In Mexico, avocados are a kind of "universal currency"; Mexico is the world's largest avocado producer, and its annual avocado trade with the U.S. is worth US$1 billion — five times more than the second- and third-largest producers, Indonesia and the U.S., combined.

## Super Bowl ads bet on creativity; Spring Festival Gala ads still mostly stop at "we're here"

Honda's ad featured flocks of sheep, Doritos brought three dogs, the drink brand Mountain Dew featured a monkey and a pug, Hyundai had a brown bear, and Heinz ketchup's ad starred dachshunds — animals were a high-frequency element in this year's Super Bowl ads.

The most obvious effect of using animals is that they make it easy to pull off a story that's unconventional and a bit fun. So Honda and Mountain Dew went the deadpan-humor route, while the animals in the Doritos, Hyundai, and Heinz ads all had an adorably dumb quality. Either way, they neatly hit people's delight buttons.

Another element Super Bowl ads love is movies. This year, Wix.com's ad and Coca-Cola's ad enlisted *Kung Fu Panda* and *Ant-Man* respectively — the former a sequel, the latter already a box-office success — and audiences' long familiarity with the characters naturally meant no sense of estrangement. In addition, every year's Super Bowl airs trailers for Hollywood studios' upcoming heavyweight films — this year there were *X-Men: Apocalypse*, Disney's live-action film *The Jungle Book*, *Deadpool*, *Jason Bourne 5*, and others.

None of this is unique to the 2016 Super Bowl — in fact it's always been this way. But the ads find fresh angles every year; more than half of YouTube's most popular ads of the year come from the "Super Bowl." In its 50-year history the Super Bowl has also produced plenty of classic ads — Apple's "1984" being the one you know best.

The Spring Festival Gala, by contrast, mostly deploys celebrity endorsers in ads like Xiang Piao Piao milk tea and LeEco's, or resorts to flat narration in voiceovers — merely telling viewers "we're here!"

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201606071154543flHbLdquV1AkoCp.jpg-w600)

To be fair, there's a huge gap between Spring Festival Gala ad creativity and Super Bowl ad creativity, and that gap will be hard to close anytime soon — many factors are behind it. Still, we hope to see something different in this most eye-catching dozen minutes or so. One small comfort: brands' New Year ads are already improving, as we covered in our [2016 New Year ad roundup](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/21788.html).

## We snark and shake for red envelopes while watching; in the U.S. they scroll Facebook and Twitter while watching

This year's official red envelope provider for the Spring Festival Gala switched from WeChat to Alipay, and those scheduled red-envelope pushes became what kept many people watching.

In its post-midnight statistics, Alipay announced that the "Xiuhongbao, Chuan Fuqi" ("snatch red envelopes, pass on good fortune") campaign it ran during the Gala generated 324.5 billion interactions, and 790,000 people who collected all five blessings split the 215 million yuan prize pool, averaging 271.66 yuan each. Still, the rule that you had to collect the complete set to redeem anything drew plenty of resentment; some complained that the rules alone filled 13 phone screens.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160607115455h9yT2PifJ4HlUDQm.jpg-w600)

WeChat red envelopes, which didn't make it into the Gala this time, randomly rolled out two pre-show Easter eggs to drum up enthusiasm: "New Year greeting red envelopes" with photos inserted, and "red envelope photos" that appeared in Moments. As of 3 a.m. on the first day of the lunar new year, it released its own numbers too: a total of 8.08 billion red envelopes were sent on New Year's Eve, and 420 million people worldwide joined the "red envelope battle."

In the lulls between red-envelope grabs, people didn't forget to check Weibo and WeChat official accounts. Big accounts worked overtime — inventorying past Spring Festival Galas that produced classic after classic, dissecting celebrities' outfits one by one, and so on. But as we said at the start, there were just too many disappointed viewers. One Zhihu user put it this way: "(This year) is a milestone, a watershed in Spring Festival Gala history. From now on, the Gala is no longer part of Chinese New Year."

On the Super Bowl side, setting aside the Panthers-Broncos game itself, from Lady Gaga singing the national anthem at the opening to the halftime performances by Coldplay, Beyoncé, and Bruno Mars, the Super Bowl set audiences ablaze. Whether in the choice of camera shots or the stage design, everything created a colorful, kaleidoscopic effect — one pointing directly at the diversity and equality American society currently emphasizes. So on both Twitter and Facebook, plenty of LGBT accounts joined the conversation. In addition, the sight of Stephen Curry, this year's NBA Most Valuable Player, drumming for the Panthers before kickoff became a hot post on social media.

On the ad front, Doritos' ad stirred up a measure of controversy. The ad, which used Doritos to play with an unborn baby in the womb, got plenty of complaints, and Twitter users went so far as to say they had to rethink whether they'd keep eating the chips.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160607115455LXbjkrWUnxT4u890.jpg-w600)

## "Chinese characteristics" vs. "American characteristics"

This year's Spring Festival Gala featured three public service ads. 《有你在，便是家》 ("With You, It's Home"), 《梦想照进现实》 ("When Dreams Shine Into Reality"), and 《门》 ("The Door") were created by Dentsu Aegis Network's Veraworm, McCann, and Leo Group's Amber Communications respectively. Veraworm also wrote the lyrics for the song 《发光时代》 ("Shining Era") in its corporate capacity — the song is actually a form of product placement for "Guirenniao" apparel, and the same song will later appear in the brand's ad film.

This genre, which could almost never appear in a high-priced Super Bowl ad, also reflects the special nature of the Spring Festival Gala. Let's provisionally call it "Chinese characteristics."

While deep-pocketed car brands took over the Super Bowl, a Yadea electric scooter ad appeared at the Spring Festival Gala. If the Netherlands is a nation of bicycles and Italy a nation of Vespa scooters, China counts as a nation of electric bicycles: they account for roughly 90% of global sales, having grown from a few tens of thousands of units in the late 1990s to 120 million units in 2013. Between the bicycle and the car, Chinese consumers have a transitional option — the electric bicycle.

360 Search's 15-second Spring Festival Gala ad probably wanted to say just one thing: it's trustworthy. But the backdrop to that message was "the internet is a mess — don't believe everything, watch out for scams..." That inevitably recalls the recent Baidu Tieba incident and the earlier "Putian clan" paid-search ranking scandal. When Google tries to abide by its "don't be evil" principle, this kind of posture — tangled up in search scandals, with the tagline written plainly as "find one you can trust" — is quite the "Chinese characteristic."

![The New York Times called this year's Super Bowl halftime show "A Most Colorful Halftime Show"](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/2016060711545697dTZN3D8CtIXryU.jpg-w600)

The Super Bowl, of course, can't escape American characteristics either. The locally adored avocado bought ad slots for the second consecutive year; Bud Light used the ongoing U.S. presidential election as its creative hook; Audi's ad paid tribute to David Bowie — the man who set out from London, shaped a generation of European and American music and aesthetics, and had just passed away; and MINI's ad featured the openly LGBT American soccer player Abby Wambach, aiming to cater to local attention around this "political correctness."

So you see, America's "Spring Festival Gala" also goes in for political correctness — which is perfectly understandable in itself. But could our Spring Festival Gala be a little more watchable?
