"We loved the proposal on paper, but it was simply too hard to realize. For various reasons, we later worried it might really never get made." Looking back at the product packaging her team designed for Nongfu Spring, British designer Sarah Pidgeon still marvels at how long and complex the process was. From taking on the project to producing a prototype, they spent roughly two and a half years — rare in packaging design.

Sarah Pidgeon is a partner at the British design studio Horse. She had worked on Nongfu Spring's packaging design several times before the studio was founded, but the collaboration that truly made her studio's name was their most recent one: the packaging design for Nongfu Spring's glass-bottled water.

In early 2015, Nongfu Spring launched its glass-bottled water. The teardrop-shaped glass bottle carries images of typical flora and fauna from the Changbai Mountains, its water source, along with related numbers and captions — each figure carrying a special meaning.

The mineral-water packaging design, two years in the making, swept nearly every major award in the packaging design field in 2015, including the International Food and Beverage Excellence Award (FAB Awards), the UK D&AD award (Design and Art Direction), the international packaging design Pentawards, and the annual awards of packaging media outlet The Dieline.

[The UK Design Week Awards also gave this product packaging its only packaging design award of 2015](https://www.designweek.co.uk/design-week-awards-2015-the-winners/), calling it "elegant and beautiful," "a collector's piece, very restrained, with printing so fine it looks almost three-dimensional."

Meanwhile, the product began appearing in high-end dining venues, forums, and conferences, and was also sold through e-commerce sites. The 750ml size was priced at 45 yuan — higher than any domestic Chinese bottled water product.

For now, such an exquisitely packaged, high-priced mineral water is an anomaly among domestic Chinese bottled drinking-water products. How exactly was its design made? We explored the story behind it.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160703122631XQupvmJKyLbSWT0l.jpg-w600)

![Nongfu Spring glass-bottled water](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160703122631FcxtNoHOQh3q5bKC.jpg-w600)

When Nongfu Spring first approached the Horse studio, the project had already been running for a year. Another design studio was then busy producing designs for the product, but Nongfu Spring hoped to find fresher ideas and commissioned Horse.

Their requirements were clear. "They wanted a beautiful, entirely distinctive bottle that could express the charm of the water source's landscape," Sarah Pidgeon recalls. "It had to have a specially customized shape, and to reflect the purity of the water, it had to appear transparent and clear." And according to Zhou Zhenhua, assistant to Nongfu Spring's general manager, the glass bottle should let consumers know at a glance that it was water, not wine; second, it had to reflect the human character of the Changbai Mountains and guide consumers to pay attention to these things.

To understand these requirements, Sarah Pidgeon and her team did quite a lot. Simply thinking through the design concept took a long time — raising a series of questions to be solved upfront, and then, through market research, company visits, and studying Nongfu Spring's past products and its design materials accumulated over the years, gradually working through those questions.

Nongfu Spring said it wanted Changbai Mountain flora and fauna on the bottle, and quite directly invited them to the Changbai Mountains to experience the natural environment of the Moya Spring water source firsthand. "It's unimaginable — if we hadn't gone to the Changbai Mountains, whether we could have realized how wonderful it is. It's full of inspiration, it stirred our creativity, and when we got back to the studio we were completely energized." The water source lies within a local nature reserve, where the designers studied the local plants and animals.

We can't know whether Sarah Pidgeon had that moment of jumping from her seat shouting "Eureka!", but she did read some of the material Nongfu Spring gave her — mostly data and facts about the water source. Reportedly, these gave Sarah her inspiration.

"I remember one item said the area is covered in snow for 270 days a year. We immediately understood that the design should try to express all these interesting facts," Sarah told QDaily.

So, in the final design, the glass bottle carries 8 species of plants and animals typical of the Changbai Mountain region, with animal and plant motifs distinguishing whether the product is sparkling water. The illustrations for the packaging were created by British illustrator

Natasha Searston. From her hand came the slowly padding Siberian tiger, the antlered red deer, the soaring osprey, the wary Chinese merganser; while on the bottles of still mineral water are motifs of the Komarov crabapple, Korean pine, wild ferns, and snowflakes.

These bottle motifs are rendered in great detail. To keep the look from becoming busy, the bottle uses only two color schemes — red-and-white or red-and-green. Besides the large brand name, red is used to highlight a particular number — the number that represents what Sarah called an "interesting fact": for instance, the 48 known species of wild mammals in the Changbai Mountain nature reserve, the 94 known families of wild angiosperms, and the fewer than 1,000 Chinese mergansers remaining worldwide from the Tertiary glacial period.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201607031226312cIoDuaLhpSm146N.jpg-w600)

![Nongfu Spring glass-bottled water](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160703122632b8Qvmr1d0GA9XKqp.jpg-w600)

From preparation to the final design, the process was anything but smooth — it stalled completely for a full year at one point, leaving Sarah Pidgeon doubtful and worried about whether it could ever be completed. By June 2012, Horse's design had already won Nongfu Spring's approval, but because bottle-making and printing techniques could not fully realize it (the bottle body is curved, while the illustrations are extremely fine and cover a large area, requiring machinery and technique of a very high level), and Nongfu Spring could not give a clear direction for improvement, the project had to be suspended. Nongfu Spring also spent a period looking for other design proposals.

As the project dragged on, design costs mounted — spent both on hiring designers and on repeated rounds of adjustment. During the product's entire R&D period, the international studios invited to design [included, besides Horse, the UK's Pearlfisher studio, Italy's Rossetti Design, and Russia's StudioIn](http://www.dinghu.com/article/content/201511/59/1.html). Because of cultural differences between the international designers and the company, and differing understandings of bottled-water products, Nongfu Spring remained unsatisfied with details of the design drafts.

For example, some bottle shapes resembled wine bottles or trophies and didn't feel like a water product; some caps were too large and didn't feel good to grip when opening. In other cases, the hydrological traits expressed through bottle shape or pattern were too generic to capture what makes the Changbai Mountains distinctive. The finer points even came down to the animals' expressions — a tiger in the pattern that looked unfriendly or unapproachable could also be grounds for rejecting the design.

"Look at this tiger's eyes — they've actually been through many revisions, revised until you feel it's a natural tiger. We didn't want to turn it into a cartoon tiger, nor a fierce one. It has to have a balance. And whether to draw it from the side or head-on, plus the layout constraints... there's a lot of finesse here," Zhou Zhenhua told QDaily.

After nearly two years of comparison, in 2014 Nongfu Spring felt it would be a pity to abandon Horse's proposal, so it went back to the designers, and the two sides discussed how to redesign the bottle shape, resolve the detail problems, and push production-process improvements. At final sign-off, the design teams — Horse included — had submitted 58 rounds and more than 300 proposals in total, and only then did the Chinese beverage company make up its mind.

![As water served with meals, its packaging must not look too much like wine packaging](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160703122633du9eDHTUZqbyKL3S.jpg-w600)

If you know a little about packaging design, you may know that Nongfu Spring's emphasis on packaging counts as ahead of the curve in China's industry. Previously, the packaging of its tea drink *Oriental Leaves* (东方树叶) and its milk-tea drink *Da Nai Cha* (打奶茶) both won silver awards in the beverage category of the international packaging design Pentawards.

"Packaging is the most direct channel for communicating with consumers," Zhou Zhenhua says. "The product sits there, consumers can see it — whatever message you want your product to convey, this is the best vehicle. So we believe that what we want to convey to consumers should be embodied in the design, so that consumers know at first glance what we're trying to do."

This is especially true in the high-end water market.

Nongfu Spring took longer than people expected to launch this product. [In 2010, its market share surpassed Wahaha's, and with 17.5% of the market it ranked second in China's bottled drinking-water market](http://money.163.com/13/0618/14/91LISGQG002526O3.html), behind Tingyi, and it has long held one of the top two spots ever since. But before Nongfu Spring launched its glass-bottled water, brands with smaller market shares — such as 5100 Tibetan Glacier Mineral Water, Evergrande Spring, Kunlun Mountain mineral water, and Alkaqua — had all touted the "premium water" label for their products.

In this competitive environment, Nongfu Spring's [general manager Zhong Shanshan explained the commercial considerations behind the glass-bottled water in an interview with *Packaging & Design* magazine](http://www.dinghu.com/article/content/201511/59/1.html): first, to fill the gap for a domestic premium drinking water at high-end Chinese conferences and dining venues; second, in recent years market demand has been segmenting steadily, premium drinking water is sought after, and the entry of foreign brands has heated up competition in the domestic market — against this backdrop, a brand's product categories should segment accordingly. But because it took nearly three years to find a suitable water source, and the packaging design also went through twists and turns, Nongfu Spring only launched the glass-bottled water in 2015.

Judging by price and packaging-design specs, this product's benchmarks are actually not Evergrande Spring or Kunlun Mountain mineral water, but foreign glass-bottled waters such as Perrier, San Pellegrino, and VOSS — imported waters that have appeared increasingly often at domestic high-end dining venues and events. Just this past January, [Norway's VOSS had 50% of its equity acquired by the Chinese company Reignwood Group](http://finance.ifeng.com/a/20160112/14162321_0.shtml), which holds the exclusive China sales rights to Red Bull and a vast distribution network; VOSS hoped to use it to expand its Chinese market.

![Uni-President's Alkaqua mineral water is another "thoughtful" entry among domestic mineral-water packaging: you can see more product information hidden on the back of the label through the bottle, and the bottle itself works like a magnifying glass. Exquisite.](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160703122633f2N4biuarzFgelyv.jpg-w600)

![Perrier](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160703122633XdIF8t0T3hDuWeyl.gif-w600Gif)

![VOSS, the Norwegian water brand, sought after by many for its stunning packaging](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160703122634rTg3sX61U4jVRvkp.jpg-w600)

In early 2015, launched alongside the glass-bottled water were Nongfu Spring's water for infants and young children and its natural mineral water for students — also distinctively packaged. The baby water considers the grip gesture for holding it, while the student water uses cartoon illustrations by a foreign illustrator, along with a cap designed not to let water spill easily.

This is regarded as a brand upgrade for Nongfu Spring — precisely segmenting the market to launch three products aimed at different consumer groups, with the move into the premium water market as its chance to break through the ceiling.

In response, Zhou Zhenhua says: "Everyone wants to make a good product, but what counts as good — as a company grows and matures, and as its horizons broaden, its definition of good changes too."

Judging from the product packaging alone, drinking-water products used to be only ordinary red-bottled mineral water, doable with the most basic packaging and labels, whereas the packaging design of this glass-bottled water is extremely complex and requires coordinating many more departments.

Nongfu Spring has a packaging-design coordination department internally. It distills the product's story from the brand (the specific content it wants to tell consumers), uses that to find the designers whose style best fits the product, and communicates with the designers. Once the designers' varied creative work is done, the coordination department contacts the industrial-design team to consider printing and bottle-making processes, so that the design draft is ultimately realized and smoothly put into production.

This series of processes was later turned into ad-film material. At the February 2015 product launch, Nongfu Spring released a series of ad films. Shot to a high standard, they covered Nongfu Spring's Changbai Mountain water source and factory, and included interviews with the designers, who explained the design philosophy to consumers.

Zhou Zhenhua says the original intention was simply to leave a record for the company's history — they took photographers with them into the Changbai Mountains to film the plant construction, water-source surveys, the design process, and so on, with everyone in the film in their natural working state. Once the material accumulated, the company decided to cut it into an ad to tell consumers about the product's birth.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160703122634FKfJYr02oWTP7aMl.jpg-w600)

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160703122635w6CUTj1v5yELk8dO.jpg-w600)

![Nongfu Spring's ad shows the birth of the product; the footage came from a documentary the company originally shot for its own use](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201607031226356QXIShCunGw9e07W.jpg-w600)

It is indeed a serious attempt. Nongfu Spring has not disclosed more sales information, but judging from consumer feedback scattered across social-network channels, many people have remembered details such as the water source, and have sensed from the packaging that this company is trying to do something different.
