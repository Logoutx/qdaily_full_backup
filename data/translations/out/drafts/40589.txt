We will use three stories to show you the group of people standing behind a wanghong who sells hit products, and what industry changes they have been through. This is the second story.

Scouting wanghong — internet celebrities who move product — is Wang Yang's daily job.

Specifically, it goes like this: every day, Wang Yang opens Weibo and searches for red-hot personalities who have recently shot to fame. When she spots one whose posts are engaging and whose looks photograph well, she quickly sums up her traits with a few adjectives like "artsy" or "campus style." If someone seems promising, she sends a private message, saying that her company, Ruhnn, is quite interested and would like to explore a collaboration.

Ruhnn is a women's wear e-commerce company, and the first employer Wang Yang joined after graduating from college. In the more than a year since, she has spent every day scrolling social media and negotiating partnerships. Recently, though, she also took over the operations of a wanghong store. It is a young store still in its growth phase, "with the potential to explode into a big one," and it has become the focus of her work lately.

Arriving at the office at 10 a.m., Wang Yang first scrolls through the store's traffic from the previous day.

She says she has to be "like a bean counter," calculator in hand, computing the daily traffic growth rate, and checking Taobao's latest promotions so she can sign the wanghong store up for them.

The day before her interview with QDaily, the store she runs had just put up new items. After finishing the traffic math, she quickly checked the refund volume, then adjusted the number of orders.

This is a problem many Taobao stores face, and it is especially acute for wanghong stores — because of their longer pre-sale windows, and because wanghong stores mostly have to honor no-questions-asked returns within 7 days. To keep inventory down, factories produce the first batch of garments, and wanghong stores typically leave a 10-to-20-day pre-sale window, during which a flood of refunds inevitably occurs.

Wang Yang's other front is the wanghong's Weibo account. Sometimes fans don't communicate through Taobao's Wangwang chat tool directly; instead they leave comments under the wanghong's Weibo posts — things like "couldn't grab it" or "shipping takes forever." Some fans ask the wanghong to model a new piece on camera, showing the effect in video rather than still photos. Wang Yang needs all this feedback, and then coordinates with the store owner and staff across the various departments.

She is the connective tissue between the wanghong and every department — once a wanghong signs on, everything from the positioning of her style and the profile of her fan base to data feedback from the store requires Wang Yang to coordinate up and down the chain.

That has quickly familiarized Wang Yang with the workflow between every link — and acquainted her with the building's more than 800 employees.

### What does the "New Talent Development Department" do?

At Ruhnn, there are more than 30 marketing-department employees like Wang Yang, but the department's name has now been changed to the "New Talent Development Department." The change of name signals that Ruhnn has transformed from an apparel manufacturer into a "wanghong incubator." In a word, the New Talent Development Department holds the power over which wanghong get into Ruhnn.

Its employees are like talent scouts in the traditional sense: they dig up new faces, set assessment criteria, and also hunt down collaboration opportunities for their wanghong.

Ruhnn's three criteria for a wanghong are: aesthetic sense and an eye for picking styles, content-creation ability, and fashion sense. It doesn't care whether a wanghong is unmarried, and it doesn't even put much weight on her existing follower count.

Worshipping follower counts too slavishly once burned Wang Yang. Even when a wanghong starts with a sizable fan base, poor positioning means follower growth stays too slow later on, driving up the cost of building her up.

Before deciding to sign a wanghong, Wang Yang runs several assessment tests to estimate the cost of developing her. This rests on a wanghong's core competitive strength: her ability to produce content. The wanghong have to interact with fans, putting out photos, text, short videos and livestreams to keep their attention.

And even that is only a small part of Ruhnn's wanghong business.

What the New Talent Development Department does is bring wanghong who already have some influence into Ruhnn and "cultivate them into big V's." As for turning ordinary people into wanghong, that task goes to the New Talent Training Department. They liaise with a wanghong talent agency called Xingyou Entertainment, which simultaneously provides incubation services for Xueli's e-commerce company Chenfan. Most recently, it also raised an 80-million-yuan Pre-A round from Chenfan.

In the summer of 2016, Ruhnn and Xingyou partnered to set up "red-hot academy" campuses in Daxing, Beijing, and Yuhang, Hangzhou, to incubate wanghong. Ruhnn has so far signed more than 40 trainees from these academies. From trainee to officially opening a store takes at minimum two to three months, "depending on how quick they are on the uptake."

The academy's two most successful cases to date are Da Mianjing and Song Dai. Da Mianjing's Weibo candidly reveals that she weighs 135 jin — about 150 pounds — encouraging plus-size girls to boldly try different styles, and occasionally sharing "slimming" outfit tips. Although she has only 120,000 followers, a few months ago her base was just 2,600, and the growth momentum is solid. More importantly, she squarely targets the "plus-size girl" demographic, "so conversion is high," Wang Yang says.

Their profiles sit on Xingyou Entertainment's website, each tagged with a very different style: Da Mianjing's personality tags are "plus-size woman" and "makeover whiz," while Song Dai gets the label "soft anime girl."

Tagging is a crucial step in setting a target fan base for a wanghong — a method borrowed from the star-making machinery of Korean agencies. These completely different personas, transplanted into the apparel industry, each represent a distinct style, paid for by a distinct audience.

Ruhnn's roster, for instance, is segmented into Zhang Dayi with her street-casual route, Song Dai with her sweet style, and Da Mianjing aimed squarely at plus-size girls.

![Zhang Dayi's style is relatively casual.](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170510000156KoCecQ5DuGSFr0nh.jpg-w600)

This kind of wanghong assembly line looks a lot like the old star-making model — and indeed they do use star-making methods to mass-produce wanghong. But in practice, every role on that assembly line has shifted position.

In the past, talent agencies and brands had a supplier-and-client relationship, with celebrities signed under an agency. But in the context of the wanghong economy, e-commerce companies sign the wanghong themselves, choosing either to cultivate them in-house or to take them to a more professional agency to boost their popularity.

### What kind of company is Ruhnn?

Jiubao, Hangzhou, is a famous hub for Taobao women's wear production, and Ruhnn is the most renowned company in it — but it hides among the factory buildings and is remarkably hard to find. The company occupies an entire eight-story building yet doesn't even have a nameplate bearing its name and logo.

This grayish-yellow industrial zone conceals thousands of small and midsize apparel manufacturers and private garment workshops. Most of the roller-shutter doors on the factory floors stand open, trucks pass through from time to time, and loaders carry down bundles of fabric.

In Hangzhou, the vast majority of Taobao e-commerce firms still operate the traditional way, but in recent years three companies — Ruhnn, Tisu and Chenfan — have changed roles. The first two both came out of garment manufacturing; only Chenfan, founded by "Wang Sicong's ex-girlfriend" Xueli, has no such background.

Ruhnn's CEO, Feng Min, comes from a science-and-engineering background. In 2007 he had already founded a mail-order platform similar to Mecox Lane, but it failed. At the end of 2011, he founded the women's wear brand "Libailin" and within two years lifted the store to double-gold-crown status.

Up until two years ago, Ruhnn was still ranking in Taobao's top ten sellers under the "Libailin" name; now it has become a wanghong-incubation company listed on the NEEQ, China's "New Third Board." Wanghong with millions of Weibo followers — Zhang Dayi, Da Jin, Zuo Jiaojiao — are all on Ruhnn's roster.

Inside Ruhnn's eight-story building are nearly 20 departments large and small. From time to time, employees ride the company's enormous freight elevator up and down, shuttling between departments.

The New Talent Development Department controls the gateway for wanghong; on the 8th floor, more than 80 designers pick and design styles for the wanghong, and the drawings are sent down to the pattern-making department on the 6th floor; from fabric to finished sample garment, the process passes through fabric sourcing, inspection, sewing, and factory ordering — a pipeline that alone involves over 100 employees. And apparel is only part of what wanghong stores sell — cosmetics are also part of the day-to-day business.

Ruhnn's roster comprises more than 40 wanghong stores, with dedicated content teams handling the text content for the wanghong — Weitao posts, product detail pages, official WeChat accounts. Customer service alone occupies nearly an entire floor. Between the neatly arranged desks of the second-floor service team stand a few clothing racks laden with garments — the latest items just put up by the wanghong stores. The service reps need to see and touch the fabric themselves before they can answer the Taobao customers on the other end of Wangwang. Beyond that, there are a hundred-odd employees scattered across branding, planning and control, administration, human resources, legal......

More than 800 employees sit at every link of this long chain, yet all of them revolve around a single center: the wanghong.

In 2015, Ruhnn still covered everything from factory floor to operations, with 1,700 employees. In early 2016, after the factories were spun off, headcount dropped by more than half.

In Jiubao's traditional business model, workers are an extremely important role. These dense ranks of labor occupy the upstream end of the garment trade, sewing clothes that ship off across the country. Their goal is to sell hit apparel.

But now Ruhnn has become much lighter — and a more attractive target for capital. After raising a B round of tens of millions of yuan from Legend Capital, Alibaba took a 300-million-yuan stake in Ruhnn in November 2016, becoming its fourth-largest shareholder with nearly 10% of the company.

It now exists in the form of "wanghong + incubator + supply chain." Whether the people doing the producing or the things being produced, everything has changed.

### The wanghong Zhang Dayi is Ruhnn's most successful "product"

By 2013, Zhang Dayi — a regular in *Rayli* and *Mina* magazines — was already famous on Weibo, and after being repeatedly asked about the clothing brands she wore, she wanted to start her own label.

At the time Zhang Dayi was still an online-store model for Ruhnn's predecessor "Libailin," so she began negotiating with Libailin, hoping it would become her supplier. The collaboration revealed possibilities to both sides, and in 2014 she became the first wanghong signed by Ruhnn.

![From Zhang Dayi's Taobao store](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170510000628mFcQ05dvSgrskz8G.jpg-w600)

Within a year, Zhang Dayi's store had beaten out numerous long-established shops to claim the No. 1 spot among Taobao wanghong stores.

The resources Zhang Dayi enjoys at Ruhnn are all top-tier. She is not just a signed wanghong — she also set up a subsidiary under Ruhnn, "Hangzhou Dayi E-Commerce Co., Ltd." Beyond everyday clothing, she sells lingerie and cosmetics, which means she needs more hands. The designers doing apparel design for her store number seven or eight; other stores get only one or two.

The copywriters, operations staff and customer-service reps who work with her are grouped separately, independent of the other wanghong stores. Customer service alone has more than 50 people, "sitting together like a class," Wang Yang says — nearly equal to the combined customer-service headcount of all the other wanghong stores, "but there are just too many customers, and they keep complaining the reps reply too slowly."

In 2016, Zhang Dayi's subsidiary posted revenue of 228 million yuan — more than half of Ruhnn's total revenue. In other words, the combined earning power of the dozens of wanghong on Ruhnn's roster still couldn't match her alone.

Ruhnn is more like a composite of nanny, agent and supply chain. Zhang Dayi only has to control the styles and "show up looking pretty"; everything before and after is handled by Ruhnn behind the scenes. She spends most of her time in Shanghai and only needs to visit Ruhnn once a month, when her assistant hauls away all the sample garments to be put on sale in the coming quarter.

### How did wanghong and Taobao e-commerce end up together?

Of Ruhnn's 100 signed wanghong, a large share are former magazine models or Taobao models. Facing a bottlenecked modeling industry, they found an exit in becoming wanghong store owners.

The decline of fashion magazines blocked the path of young women who had hoped to model. And modeling is a hard profession to sustain long-term — especially at girlie-styled publications like *ViVi* and *Rayli*, where once a model reaches a certain age, brands discard her. The wanghong's other predecessor, the Taobao model, exists mainly to shoot product photos for Taobao stores, paid by the hour or by the number of garments — essentially no different from a manual laborer, with time as her only cost and more work meaning more pay.

So magazine models and Taobao models need to transition before their careers run out. Given their constant exposure to e-commerce and their camera-readiness, the most common option is to open an online store selling women's wear or cosmetics.

Their built-in traffic on social networks happened to be exactly what Taobao stores were chasing at the same time. Weibo became the ideal petri dish — Alibaba holds a 31.5% stake in Weibo. In 2015, the arrival of Weibo's "Weibo Shop Window" feature compressed the jump between the two platforms to a single step, cutting the loss of would-be buyers along the way.

Opening an online store is a traffic game. The "Taobao brands" once beloved of the investment world — Handu Yishe, Qigeqe, Liebo — were products of the Taobao dividend era. But as traffic costs rose at breakneck speed, small merchants and newcomers could only start hunting for new ways to draw traffic.

So stores seeking traffic and Taobao models seeking supply chains paired up, to good effect. This replicable model led girls with similar résumés or naturally photogenic faces to start chasing the opportunity — and they had long been targets of capital's pursuit. The "Ruhnn-like" companies emerged in response.

Weibo became the wanghong's main battlefield, home to more than 15,000 e-commerce wanghong.

In 2016, self-media accounts on Weibo earned 11.7 billion yuan over the year, most of it flowing to e-commerce wanghong.

Douban was once a boot camp for wanghong, too. But Douban's weakness on social features pushed wanghong hoping to embrace e-commerce toward Weibo in the end. Relatively outstanding Douban personalities like Zhang Xinyuan and Jessie Li, meanwhile, marched into show business — a completely different road.

Beyond providing a platform, Weibo also acts as an adviser to wanghong incubation companies: it not only offers back-end data services but also provides fan profiles. Ruhnn, Chenfan and Tisu are all Weibo "strategic partners" — a designation that requires a wanghong agency to hold more than 20 wanghong accounts, more than 10 million followers, and total annual wanghong-store revenue above 250 million yuan.

### Risk

The wanghong agency business is no small trade.

Tmall store owner Lao Qi recently signed a wanghong named "Tingting," whose store had nearly collapsed because she "had absolutely no idea how to run it." She relied on Taobao livestreaming to drum up business, shot her store's photos on a phone, and had no stable suppliers.

Lao Qi spent 20 years in the garment wholesale trade before starting in e-commerce seven years ago. Like early-stage Ruhnn, he has a supply chain and ample Taobao operations experience.

But Lao Qi cautiously signed only a three-month agreement with Tingting. He knows full well that although the wanghong-building model is replicable, the cost of building one will only climb as competition intensifies: "The main thing is her own ability — whether she can make it work." All Lao Qi can do is swap in a more professional batch of photos, and spend 70,000 yuan on Taobao's Zhitongche ad platform.

To outsiders, there seems to be no pattern to becoming a wanghong. Taobao model He Ningning was one of the eight bell-ringers at Alibaba's New York Stock Exchange IPO. She follows a healthy, sunny fitness route, talks about working out on Weibo, and even appeared on the quiz show *《一站到底》*. But she "just can't catch fire," with only 200,000 Weibo followers.

By comparison, a wanghong named Yang Xia on Ruhnn's roster attracted more than two million followers within half a year. She has no background to speak of, already has a son and a daughter, and often posts family photos with her kids and husband on Weibo. But because she is positioned as a "hot mom" and works in cosmetics, her follower count grew fast.

Compared with apparel, beauty bloggers are the category where results come easiest. Before-and-after makeup videos or photos have the most direct and most compelling effect. But set against a makeup wanghong with face-transforming technique like Michelle Phan, Yang Xia's looks are monotonous — mostly everyday light makeup. Comments from fans mostly marvel at how happy she seems, and they also ask her the secret to "having two kids with no stretch marks."

![@Yang Xia Sunny](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201705090127066Xyt9VWqYlAmknSu.jpg-w600)

Ruhnn is happy to see cases like this: the cost of making her famous is low, the speed is fast, the fan base is clearly positioned, and the earning power is strong.

To date, several wanghong have left Ruhnn after becoming famous — a risk incubation companies have always had to weigh. But Wang Yang says she understands; she stresses that Ruhnn's goal is no longer just the wanghong business. The next step is to "own our own brands."
