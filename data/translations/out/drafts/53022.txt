### What the Agenda-Setters Are Talking About

**[The New Yorker: The future of vape culture and the rise of e-cigarette maker Juul](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2018/05/14/the-promise-of-vaping-and-the-rise-of-juul)**

Vape derives from Vapour ("vapor"), and is commonly known as the vapor e-cigarette. You can use the word Vaping to describe the act of inhaling from an e-cigarette, though the more fashionable term these days is Juuling.

Juul was originally the name of an e-cigarette brand, but like the copier brand Xerox, which became synonymous with "photocopying," it is now a byword for vaping. A Juul set consists of a vaporizer and four pods, retailing at US$34.99 and US$15.99 respectively.

According to Wells Fargo's forecasts, the US e-cigarette market will grow 25% year on year in 2018 to US$5.5 billion — only about 4.6% of the current US cigarette market. But within the e-cigarette market, Juul holds a 60% share.

Attempts to reduce the harm traditional cigarettes do to human health by developing e-cigarettes date back to the 1960s. The cancer-causing part of cigarettes is not the addictive nicotine itself, but the tar and other chemicals mixed in with it. In 2003, Chinese pharmacist Hon Lik invented the prototype of today's mainstream e-cigarette — a device that vaporizes liquid nicotine using an internal heating system. In 2005, two Stanford University product design graduate students, Adam Bowen and James Monsees, founded an e-cigarette R&D company called Ploom, later sold it to a Japanese company and renamed it Pax Labs, and, after a series of experiments, launched the e-cigarette brand Juul in 2012 — a name evocative both of a gem (Jewel) and of the unit of energy, the Joule.

Juul's cleverness lies in its compact design, which breaks from the traditional cigarette-mimicking look and instead has a "geeky" form, like a USB flash drive; it only delivers nicotine, so operating it is simple. It also boosted its recognizability and appeal through flavor: four varieties in all — tobacco, mint, fruit, and dessert. In terms of the underlying principle, the biggest breakthrough was the chemistry behind it: adding benzoic acid to nicotine particles replicates the rapid nicotine hit produced by burning tobacco in conventional cigarettes.

The popularity of traditional cigarettes was shaped by Madison Avenue and Hollywood; people over 40 think of the rugged cowboy lighting up a Marlboro. E-cigarette culture, by contrast, is defined by Instagram and Snapchat. Juul's official Instagram account, @juulvapor, has 44,000 followers, and images eagerly uploaded by devotees of Juul culture, such as @doit4juul, look effortlessly cool.

The pleasure it offers closely resembles scrolling Instagram — a jolt when one feels tired or anxious, while filling exactly those brief scraps of free time that slip away so quickly. With a Juul you can take two puffs and snap several selfies in 10 seconds, whereas finishing a cigarette takes at least 5 minutes.

Jason Jeong, a Cornell University student, is a Juul user. In a new column he started in the campus newspaper, called "The Juul Manifesto," he argues that Juul embodies the spirit of his generation: "fascinated by technology, creative, and therefore good at making bad decisions quickly"; "aspiring to a healthier lifestyle without realizing the pursuit itself harms their health"; "flippant when it comes to serious topics like health and morality." He has a social group of three to four hundred people, dubbed the "Juuluminati" (Juul + alumni), dedicated to trading pods.

But the rapid spread on social media has left the FDA at a loss over how to restrict e-cigarette marketing aimed at teenagers. On April 24, the FDA announced it would mount a large-scale global blitz to crack down on e-cigarette sales targeting minors. Warning letters went out to 40 retailers. On May 1, the FDA sent warning letters to 13 companies selling liquid nicotine in packaging resembling juice boxes.

### 24 Hours of News-Makers

**[Malaysia's opposition wins the general election for the first time in 60 years](http://www.chinanews.com/gj/2018/05-10/8510214.shtml)**.

Vote-count results released by the Malaysian Election Commission on the 10th showed that in the country's 14th general election, the opposition alliance Pakatan Harapan (PH), led by 92-year-old former prime minister Mahathir Mohamad, won 113 parliamentary seats — a majority that secures the election and will allow it to form a new government. The ruling coalition Barisan Nasional (BN), led by incumbent prime minister Najib Razak, won only 79 seats.

BN is made up of 13 parties, including the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO), the Malaysian Chinese Association (MCA), and the Malaysian Indian Congress. Since Malaysia's independence in 1957, a coalition centered on UMNO has held power continuously.

Mahathir's victory means Malaysia changes its ruling party for the first time in 60 years.

[An editorial in the Financial Times](http://www.ftchinese.com/story/001077469) argued that the oddest thing about this election was that "the candidate who offers the greatest hope of change is a 92-year-old former authoritarian."

Born in December 1925, Mahathir is the oldest candidate in this election. He served as prime minister from 1981 to 2003 — 22 years, the longest tenure in Malaysian history. During his time in office, Malaysia's economy developed markedly and the poverty rate fell substantially.

In early 2016, Mahathir quit UMNO for the second time and, several months later, founded a new party, determined to join forces with other opposition parties to defeat Najib's BN in the 2018 election, because "UMNO is no longer the UMNO of old; it has become a party that supports and protects Najib."

In recent years, Najib has been mired in a corruption scandal involving the 1MDB sovereign wealth fund. The US Department of Justice filed a civil lawsuit alleging that roughly US$4.5 billion in assets had been misappropriated from the fund, which Najib established in 2009.

### What Else Happened in the World

**[North Korea released three Korean-American citizens held in labor camps](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-northkorea-missiles/three-americans-freed-by-north-korea-ahead-of-trump-kim-summit-idUSKBN1IA08I?il=0)**. On May 9, US President Donald Trump announced the news on Twitter. He also said that before their release, Secretary of State Mike Pompeo had made an unannounced visit to North Korea to discuss the upcoming US–North Korea leaders' summit.

The venue for the summit will be announced within three days, but it will not be Panmunjom on the military demarcation line between North and South Korea. Some media reports said the meeting will be held in Singapore.

White House spokesperson Sanders later said the White House regards North Korea's release of the American citizens as a gesture of goodwill. The three Americans — Kim Dong-chul, Kim Dong-chul, and Kim Hak-song — were detained in May 2017, April 2017, and 2005 respectively, on charges of "suspected espionage and stealing North Korean state secrets."

**[IAEA Director General Yukiya Amano issued a statement on the 9th saying the agency is certain Iran has been implementing the Iran nuclear deal as required](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-iran-nuclear-iaea/iran-still-implementing-commitments-under-nuclear-deal-iaea-says-idUSKBN1IA1JV)**. He added that the agency will closely follow developments concerning the deal.

**[Chinese Premier Li Keqiang held separate bilateral talks with South Korean President Moon Jae-in and Japanese Prime Minister Shinzo Abe](http://www.chinanews.com/gn/2018/05-10/8510163.shtml)**. The former focused mainly on denuclearization of the peninsula, while the latter centered on how to bring China–Japan relations back onto a normal track. China and Japan agreed to launch a "maritime and air liaison mechanism" on June 8.

**[A magnitude 6.5 earthquake struck Afghanistan](http://www.xinhuanet.com/world/2018-05/09/c_1122808620.htm)**. At 3:11 p.m. local time on the 9th, an earthquake hit the Hindu Kush region of northern Afghanistan, at a depth of about 97 km. No casualties were reported. It was the second earthquake to hit Afghanistan in two days; the previous one, a magnitude 4.9 quake, struck central Afghanistan on the 8th local time.

**[The US imposed new sanctions on defense firms in Russia, China, and several other countries](http://tass.com/world/1003544).** According to Russia's Sputnik news agency, the US sanctioned defense companies from Russia, Iran, North Korea, China, Turkey, and Saudi Arabia for violating laws on the non-proliferation of weapons of mass destruction. A total of 28 entities were placed on the sanctions list. The US government believes these companies could assist Iran, Syria, and North Korea in developing ballistic or cruise missiles.

**[Volcanic activity in Hawaii continues](https://edition.cnn.com/2018/05/09/us/hawaii-kilauea-volcano-acid-rain/index.html)**. On May 8 local time, the Kilauea volcano in the US state of Hawaii erupted again, spewing toxic gas from two new vents, and authorities once more evacuated residents. Since last Thursday, the volcano has produced a series of major eruptions, with lava fountains reaching 90 meters high, while fissures in the ground also released deadly volcanic gases. Lava has covered at least 104 acres of the Big Island and destroyed more than 30 buildings, including at least 26 homes, but there have been no casualties.

**[The Democratic Republic of Congo confirmed that the Ebola virus has killed 17 people](https://qz.com/1273712/drc-congo-ebola-outbreak-kills-17/)**. In a statement on May 8, the DRC health ministry said that near the town of Bikoro, in Ikoko Impenge, 21 patients showed signs of hemorrhagic fever, and laboratory tests confirmed two cases of viral infection. This is the ninth outbreak since the Ebola virus first appeared in the country in 1976. Between 2013 and 2016, the Ebola virus killed more than 11,300 people in West Africa.

Ren Xianliang, vice chairman of the Social Development Affairs Committee of the National People's Congress and former deputy director of the Cyberspace Administration of China, serves as its president.
