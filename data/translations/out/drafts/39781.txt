@@QD_TITLE@@
After More Than 20 Years of Rivalry, Tingyi and Uni-President May Well Turn Into Two Very Different Companies
@@QD_EXCERPT@@
Uni-President is more aggressive in its product innovation experiments, while Tingyi has stronger distribution advantages, and the two are becoming increasingly different in temperament. But when it comes to the youth market of the future, both are playing defense right now.
@@QD_BODY@@
Uni-President is more aggressive in its product innovation experiments, while Tingyi has stronger distribution advantages, and the two are becoming increasingly different in temperament. But when it comes to facing the youth market of the future, right now both are on the defensive.

After more than 20 years of rivalry, will people still mention Tingyi and Uni-President in the same breath? Judging from where things stand today, they are already becoming two very different companies.

Facing a packaged-food market whose growth is slowing — and in which some categories are even shrinking — these old rivals have adopted entirely different playbooks. Uni-President is trying to meet the new market with a large number of new, high-priced, high-margin products; judging by the number and positioning of its launches, Tingyi relies more on its existing products and channels.

That said, 2016 was a hard year for both Uni-President and Tingyi. Uni-President's revenue came to 20.9 billion yuan, down 5.1% year over year; the larger Tingyi brought in US$8.3 billion (about 57.68 billion yuan), but fell harder, by 8%.

Neither made much money either. Uni-President's net profit last year was 607 million yuan, down 27.2% year over year; Tingyi's was US$213 million (about 1.47 billion yuan), down 24.3%.

Seen this way, the two look more like a pair of down-on-their-luck brothers.

Thanks to having moved early into mid- to high-end products for its instant noodle business — catering to "consumption upgrade" — Uni-President's overall slide was not as steep as Tingyi's. But if you turn your attention to the beverage business, which now accounts for 58% of both companies' revenue structures, the two face the same predicament. And beverages happen to be exactly where the two companies' performances are diverging most.

### New Products

Take a look first at the beverage products Uni-President and Tingyi launched in 2016.

In 2016 Uni-President introduced a total of 7 new beverage products to the market (12 in 2015) — not only a large number, but with rather bold concepts. The clearest display of its aggressiveness was three functional drinks retailing at close to 20 yuan: "Relax" and "Awaken" from the *Nature Lab* (《自然实验室》) series, and *Qingchang Jiaozhu* (《轻畅酵主》, a fermented digestive drink).

*Nature Lab* is a product unlike anything the company has done before. Its retail price of 19.9 yuan is the highest ever for a Uni-President beverage — the only products it can be benchmarked against are imported drinks. The bottle design and materials are also impressive: it uses the bottle-can format common among imported beverages, and the bottle is claimed to be imported from Japan.

Another important change: you have to look very carefully to spot the Uni-President logo — it sits in a corner of the bottle. Like the new products launched after 2012, it no longer follows the "Uni-President + category name" naming convention (such as Uni Iced Black Tea). This is a de-Unified product strategy; you can see similar traits in *Little Classmate Xiaoming* (《小茗同学》) and *Song of the Sea* (《海之言》), where the corporate identity is deliberately downplayed. Chen Wei, a section chief in Uni-President's PR department, once told QDaily this was intended to enable the "branding" of subsequent products, while the name of the parent company, Uni-President, would fade further into the background.

Besides *Nature Lab*, Uni-President's other new products — the *Sweet Light-Year* (《恬蜜光年》) mango pomelo sago, *Qingchang Jiaozhu*, and *Water Fun* (《水趣多》) — are all drinks retailing above 5 yuan per unit. And 2015's new products, such as *Tiny Food Moments* (《如微食刻》, a fruit-and-vegetable juice), *Little Classmate Xiaoming*, and *Assam Milk Tea*, mainly targeted young consumers in first-tier cities, who are willing to try new things and have decent spending power.

By comparison, Tingyi launched few new products. *Nong Nong* (《浓浓》), *Mango Yogurt* (《芒果小酪》), *Lychee Nectar* (《香凝荔枝》), and *Golden Orange Passion Fruit* (《金橙百香果》) are still tea and juice drinks — the traditional categories Tingyi is good at, and its two biggest beverage revenue streams. Tingyi has not ventured into new categories, nor has there been any fundamental rethink behind its new products. Take *Mango Yogurt*: compared with the earlier "Daily C" (《每日C》) juice series, its taste is richer — it follows the mixed-flavor route — but the positioning is not really different: still very feminine, with a touch of the "little fresh" sensibility.

Tingyi's new products are also relatively low-priced; only *Nong Nong* retails above 5 yuan per unit. In 2014 Tingyi launched *Ripple* (《水漾》), a lightly flavored vitamin water that was clearly positioned against *Song of the Sea* and Suntory's *Fresh Lemon Water* (《沁柠水》), but it priced the product lower than both, at 3 yuan. The product failed to achieve the success of *Song of the Sea* or *Fresh Lemon Water* and was pulled from the market in 2016.

Compared with Uni-President's bold de-Unification and its experiments with double-digit prices, Tingyi lacks breakthrough innovation. "Tingyi faces the awkward situation of aging traditional star products, while its new products cannot gain a foothold in the market," [Xiang Jianjun, a food-industry researcher at Zhongtou Consulting, said in an interview.](http://www.cb.com.cn/companies/2016_0606/1165156.html)

Tingyi has even fewer market-moving new products than Uni-President. Most of its 2014 and 2015 launches — such as *Ripple* (a vitamin water) and the *A Moment* (《一刻馆》) refreshing milk tea — failed the test of time. (*Sea Crystal Lemon* (《海晶柠檬》) did survive, but it looks more like a follower product to *Song of the Sea*.) What it distributes most widely in first-tier cities and new channels, and what it can still most proudly show off, remain the Jasmine Green Tea and Jasmine Honey Tea series, plus Tingyi Green Tea and Tingyi Black Tea. These products all have histories of more than 10 years.

You can see that Tingyi still follows the big-single-product model: target the most mass-market segment, scale up production of a single product to drive down costs, and use its well-developed distribution advantage to sell on thin margins at high volume.

Even in 2016, Tingyi was still "strengthening channel penetration into lower-tier markets" and trying to "seize the opportunities of urbanization" (the company's official wording in its financial reports), while Uni-President began setting its sights on the "middle class" — or "broad middle class" — the 250 to 300 million people in the top 20% of China's consumption pyramid. It intends to play in the consumption-upgrade market. This is also why, over the past five years, Uni-President's overall gross margin has stayed 2–5 percentage points higher than Tingyi's.

But in September 2016, [Tingyi was dropped from the Hang Seng Index](http://finance.qq.com/a/20160908/010127.htm), and several investment banks, such as HSBC and BOCOM International, downgraded their ratings on the company. A key reason was Tingyi's lack of product innovation. At its 2014 peak, Tingyi's market value exceeded HK$110 billion; now it stands at just HK$58.5 billion, down nearly 50%. Uni-President, by contrast, has been more stable: its market value is only half of Tingyi's, but its share price has fallen just 28.5%.

### Innovation and Failure

In fact, before the "Old Pickled Mustard Greens" flavor became a smash hit in 2003, Uni-President had been on the back foot in its rivalry with Tingyi. It entered the mainland market late, and it lagged behind Tingyi — which was born on the mainland — in channel development.

Uni-President has a larger market share and a longer history in Taiwan, and by comparison is stronger in product R&D. Its strategy of targeting the mid- to high-end market was already visible in 2008, when it turned its bottled-water business profitable by launching the slightly pricier Alkaqua. Building on this, Uni-President exited the low-end bottled-water market in 2014.

Phrases like "moving beyond the low-price competition pattern" and "continuously launching differentiated drinks" began appearing repeatedly in Uni-President's rhetoric around 2012. Nearly all of its new products were drinks priced above 4 yuan with a 40% gross margin, or instant noodles priced above 5 yuan per unit with a gross margin above 35%. These were regarded internally as "2.0 products" and described in financial reports as "personalized, diversified, and premium."

This premium push first showed up in product design. Uni-President has won the Pentawards — the "Oscars" of the packaging design world — six times. (Among comparable Chinese FMCG companies, perhaps only Nongfu Spring can match that.) Its *Bama Spring* water, *Ming Ming Is Tea* (《茗茗是茶》 — a product launched in Shanghai in 2013 that has since been discontinued), and *Little Classmate Xiaoming* earned it one silver and two gold awards. In 2016, *Assam Milk Tea* and "PLAN N" (a plant-protein drink) won gold awards, and *Ge Mian* (《革面》) is the award-winning product among Uni-President's instant noodles.

![Assam Milk Tea and PLAN N](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201704140130281ifaeKmUN6G0RgQL.png-w600)

The R&D process also changed. Beyond the traditional R&D department, Uni-President adopted a bottom-up new-product mechanism in 2004. Unlike the traditional FMCG development process, it does not rely so heavily on ingredient suppliers and market-research findings; every employee at Uni-President China can propose a product plan. The idea for *Little Classmate Xiaoming* reportedly came from a Uni-President product manager who tasted cold-brewed tea while traveling in Taiwan.

Proposals are reviewed by a creation committee made up of senior employees seconded from various departments. After the committee and the proposer settle on the product concept and gross margin, two independent teams develop the product simultaneously, and the committee finally picks one of the two to bring to market. Uni-President even invites university students into development decisions: a "pan-post-90s consumer review panel" participated in the development of *Little Classmate Xiaoming* and *Song of the Sea*.

Currently these "2.0 products" account for about 24% of Uni-President's total revenue, and Hou Ronglong, general manager of Uni-President Enterprises, aims to raise the share of high-margin products in sales to 40% within three years.

But Uni-President's innovation has not gone all that smoothly.

So far, its aggressive new-product strategy has not brought sustained success to its overall beverage business. Its beverage performance has been disappointing — beverage revenue was nearly flat in 2015 and fell 13% last year, a drop far bigger than the 6.49% decline at Tingyi's beverage business.

Last year Uni-President had no new hit on the scale of *Little Classmate Xiaoming*, which sold more than 500 million yuan in half a year. The success rate for new drinks in the industry is extremely low — only about 5% [survive beyond one year](http://www.chinanews.com/business/2015/12-22/7681499.shtml).

The products launched in 2016 that are still on sale today are *Water Fun*, *Sweet Light-Year* mango pomelo sago, and *Qingchang Jiaozhu*. "Only 1% of products can keep selling well for more than three years," Niu Ka, manager of the functional-beverage brand group in Uni-President's integrated beverage division, told QDaily in an earlier interview.

As for *Nature Lab: Awaken*, it has essentially exited the stage: you can no longer find the product in convenience stores or on e-commerce platforms.

Uni-President is still experimenting. It entered the functional-drink category as early as 2015 with Uni Sports. Tingyi has not yet entered this field (although, having acquired PepsiCo's China business, it can sell Pepsi's Gatorade). The reason Uni-President chose to move into this category is the rapid growth of China's functional beverage market. *Nature Lab* is its second attempt at functional drinks — and its second failure.

When a new product misses the market, the costs invested up to that point count as "sunk." From R&D to production to promotion, launching a single new beverage [costs roughly 60–80 million yuan](http://www.chinanews.com/business/2015/12-22/7681499.shtml). Frequent product launches can sap a company's strength. And when the new-product R&D process is too scattered, products that deserve support may not get enough resources. *Nature Lab*, for instance, had almost no budget for advertising or channel placement, which may be one reason it failed.

To address this sticking point, Luo Zhixian, chairman of Uni-President Enterprises Group, said at a recent analysts' meeting: "Uni-President needs more disciplined management of its attitude toward new products. We will keep developing new products, but they won't necessarily actually be launched... it will depend on market conditions." It looks like there will still be new products in 2017 — just probably not too many.

### Channels

Tingyi was among the first FMCG companies to push for channel penetration into lower-tier markets. Around 2000 it had already expanded its sales network to prefecture-level cities and townships, laying the groundwork for its later growth. Tingyi can reportedly complete nationwide distribution of a new product — from big cities down to mom-and-pop shops in townships — within two weeks.

By comparison, Uni-President's reach is not as wide or as dense; it essentially does business only in prefecture-level cities and above. To compete with Tingyi, Uni-President acquired stakes in Jinmailang Beverages (47.83%) and Jianlibao in 2006 and 2007 respectively — a move that also valued the two companies' distribution and shelf presence in lower-tier cities and rural markets.

According to [a 2014 research report by Guotai Junan](http://www.gtja.com/f//lotus/201410/%E5%9B%BD%E6%B3%B0%E5%90%9B%E5%AE%89_%E6%B5%B7%E5%A4%96%E8%A1%8C%E4%B8%9A%E4%B8%93%E9%A2%98%EF%BC%88%E9%A6%99%E6%B8%AF%EF%BC%89_%E6%B8%AF%E8%82%A1%E6%96%B9%E4%BE%BF%E9%9D%A2%E3%80%81%E9%A5%AE%E6%96%99%E5%B7%A8%E5%A4%B4%EF%BC%9A%E5%BA%B7%E5%B8%88%E5%82%85%E3%80%81%E7%BB%9F%E4%B8%80%E4%BC%81%E4%B8%9A%E2%80%94%E2%80%94%E9%A3%9F%E5%93%81%E9%A5%AE%E6%96%99%E2%80%9C%E6%B2%AA%E6%B8%AF%E9%80%9A%E2%80%9D%E4%B8%93%E9%A2%98%E7%B3%BB%E5%88%97%E4%B9%8B%E5%9B%9B_%E8%83%A1%E6%98%A5%E9%9C%9E%20%E9%97%AB%E4%BC%9F_20141008_addStamper_addEncrypt.pdf), although Tingyi falls short on product innovation, it commands a powerful distribution system and channel control that Uni-President cannot match.

From 2012 to 2014, Tingyi topped the China Brand Footprint report from market-research firm Kantar (in 2015 it was overtaken by Yili and slipped one place). This shows its purchase frequency and penetration — in other words, its channel capability — are extremely high. Uni-President has consistently trailed behind it, hovering between fourth and seventh place.

Around 2012, possibly buoyed by sales of two high-margin products — *Soup Master* (《汤达人》) instant noodles and Alkaqua — Uni-President began seeking to sell its stake in Jinmailang Beverages, effectively downgrading the importance of lower-tier city and rural markets. In May 2016, Uni-President formally sold its Jinmailang Beverages stake for 1.291 billion yuan. That September, it sold its Jianlibao stake to Chunxin Capital, under CITIC Assets, for 950 million yuan. Differences between Uni-President and the Jianlibao Group over [brand positioning — one high-end, one low-end —](http://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2016-11-29/doc-ifxyawxa2999110.shtml?cre=financepagepc&mod=f&loc=4&r=9&doct=0&rfunc=100) are seen as one reason for the sale.

Consumer habits have changed, and new channels matter more and more — especially small-format retail such as convenience stores and community supermarkets, as well as e-commerce. According to Nielsen data, in 2015 chain convenience stores and chain personal-care stores — benefiting from serving customers faster and more conveniently — contributed 7% and 5% respectively of growth in the beverage category.

Both Uni-President and Tingyi are also attaching growing importance to the convenience-store channel; the battle for shelf space for new products there may be even fiercer than in traditional supermarkets. Uni-President Enterprises Group's President Chain Store operates 7-Eleven stores in the Shanghai area, and it also holds a 55% stake in Uni-Mart, a convenience-store chain in Shandong. Tingyi's parent company, Ting Hsin International Group, holds a 50.5% stake in FamilyMart's mainland China business.

QDaily reporters randomly checked the beverage coolers at FamilyMart, Lawson, and 7-Eleven stores in Shanghai's Huangpu and Xuhui districts, and found Tingyi and Uni-President products fairly evenly matched on the shelves. Although retail and food are run as separate businesses, brands must pay channel fees to enter convenience stores or small supermarkets; being under the same group gives them some convenience and preferential tilt in shelf policy versus competitors. FamilyMart stores lean slightly toward Tingyi beverages (though there are still differences among individual FamilyMart stores).

Because Ting Hsin operates more convenience stores in the mainland, Tingyi should in theory hold the advantage. But launching products suited to convenience-store consumption may matter just as much as grabbing shelf space, and Uni-President does a better job of pushing mid- to high-end drinks. Consumers in first-tier cities are also more willing to pay a higher unit price for convenience than they would at big-box stores.

The other channel is e-commerce. Uni-President launched its Tmall official store in 2014; Tingyi formally entered Tmall in June 2016. Although official stores may not contribute much in sales (especially compared with online supermarkets), they are a convenient, low-cost channel for testing new products. And indeed, many of Uni-President's recent new products debuted on e-commerce.

### Market

Both Uni-President's and Tingyi's beverage sales peaked in 2013 and have been declining ever since. The year 2016 merely continued the post-2013 trend. The main reasons are the disappearance of the "channel" dividend and the rapid change in consumer habits.

The main driver of FMCG companies' rapid growth over the past decade has been [channel expansion — continuously stocking shelves and expanding into new regions](http://cn.kantar.com/%E6%B6%88%E8%B4%B9%E8%B5%B0%E5%90%91/%E8%B4%AD%E7%89%A9%E8%80%85/2016/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E9%A5%AE%E6%96%99%E5%B8%82%E5%9C%BA%E7%9A%84%E4%BA%94%E5%A4%A7%E6%9C%BA%E9%81%87/) (fundamentally, it was still the demographic dividend, digging into China's market layer by layer). As their dealers and channels approach saturation, this demographic and geographic dividend is nearly exhausted.

Tingyi's number of distributors stopped growing after 2014, which roughly coincides with the curve of its revenue growth — its revenue peaked in 2013.

"The importance of economies of scale and low-cost competitiveness to a company's overall operations is no longer what it once was," as Luo Zhixian put it — a challenge that Tingyi and Uni-President now face together. In other words, the big-single-product model no longer works, because consumption is becoming increasingly fragmented.

Beyond Uni-President and Tingyi, Coca-Cola is also having a rough time in China. It named China as a drag on the Asia-Pacific region's overall performance in [its 2016 annual report](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/37674.html). Mizone, which had held the No. 1 share of China's functional beverage market for more than a decade, has also begun to decline; its parent company, Danone, has had to launch new products — *Sui Yue* (《随悦》) and *Lemon's Here* (《柠檬来的》) — to try to lift sales.

This is actually a global problem: as consumers' health awareness grows, food companies see slowed growth or even decline in (relatively) mature markets. In the US market, both Coca-Cola and PepsiCo have launched drinks in smaller packages, invested in emerging food brands, or tweaked their formulas to introduce healthier products.

![Danone's "Lemon's Here" recruited members of SNH48 as endorsers](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170414014509Jml731CQd5TBqXKr.jpg-w600)

They also face more and more new competitors. Yu Limin of Euromonitor International told QDaily: "Chain fresh-squeezed juice shops are multiplying. The juice they offer tastes fresher and comes in richer flavors; compared with bottled juice, the price is not that much higher, and well-developed food delivery also makes it very convenient for people to get a cup of fresh-squeezed juice."

And it is not just juice — freshly made milk tea, coffee, and tea drinks are also grabbing a share of this market. According to [research data from Kantar and CTR](http://weibo.com/ttarticle/p/show?id=2309404091663994728372), two-thirds of people bought a freshly made drink at least once in 2016, and consumers in China's first- and second-tier cities now buy freshly made drinks an average of 14.3 times per year — which may, to some degree, be eating into packaged beverage sales.

Consumer preferences and the purchase-decision process have become more complex, and individual products in the beverage market now turn over faster.

"Before, a product would take at least ten years from launch to peak to decline. Now the average cycle for many products may be about three years," Niu Ka told QDaily in an interview six months ago. "Recently, whether in our own data or in Nielsen's data, the entire product upgrade-and-replacement cycle keeps shortening."

Products like Tingyi Iced Black Tea or Uni-President Green Tea can have life cycles as long as 20 years; *Assam Milk Tea* and Tingyi's jasmine tea series have life spans of around 10 years. But Suntory's *Fresh Lemon Water*, *Song of the Sea*, and the like were active for only about three years. Some star single products rose and fell even faster. For example, in 2011 both Uni-President and Tingyi launched pear juice drinks (both called "Rock Sugar & Pear"); they sold well at first, but by 2014 the overall pear juice market had begun to decline.

A recent example is Uni-President's *Song of the Sea*. It was a huge success right out of the gate: within eight months of launch, its sales entered the top 10 for single juice products, and Uni-President pressed its advantage by adding new flavors. In 2014 and 2015, this star single product was "named and praised as a success story" in financial reports; then in 2016 it suddenly began to decline. Uni-President's official explanation was "changes in consumption trends," along with adjustments to sales rhythm and distribution channels. Setting aside Uni-President's own subjective factors, one plausible explanation is that this lightly flavored drink — salty with a fruity note — had fallen out of favor.

Many new products, fierce competition, and consumers with little loyalty — this seems to have become a vicious circle. "Because consumer tastes change so quickly, the market cycle of beverage products keeps shortening, and manufacturers are actively launching new products to attract consumers. The flood of new products also intensifies competition in the beverage industry," Yu Limin said.

No one dares claim they understand consumers anymore.

So, facing a youth market that has already changed, whether it is Uni-President's aggressiveness or Tingyi's conservatism, in fact both are presenting a defensive posture.

![BIGBANG endorsing Tea π](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170414012924EQhucbJo5WYtLkTN.jpg-w600)

In 2016, neither Tingyi nor Uni-President won in the beverage market. The year's new product with sales exceeding 1 billion yuan came from Nongfu Spring: Tea π, which covered both bases with design (product form and concept) and traffic (recruiting BIGBANG as endorsers). This may be a story that neither Tingyi nor Uni-President saw coming.

Coincidentally, both Tingyi's and Uni-President's beverage revenue shares came to 58% this year, and instant noodles 39%. But behind the numbers, things are now completely different.

No wonder Uni-President executives say they don't pay much attention to Tingyi anymore. ["I'm watching Nongfu Spring," Luo Zhixian said.](https://wechat.kanfb.com/tech/4744)
