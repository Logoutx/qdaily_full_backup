New energy, new technology — but what automakers care about most is still how to sell more cars in the enormous Chinese market.

The first public day of the 2017 Shanghai Auto Show fell on a Friday, yet more than 120,000 visitors still streamed into the National Exhibition and Convention Center in Shanghai's Qingpu district. That was double the attendance of the 2015 show — and back then, the first public day was a Saturday.

The enthusiasm of the crowds fired up the automakers.

Whether measured by scale or by how much the automakers invested in it, this biennial event — China's grandest annual occasion in the auto world — holds its own against the four major international auto shows: Geneva, Detroit, Frankfurt and New York.

This year, brands from 18 countries and regions [exhibited 1,400 complete vehicles](http://www.autoshanghai.org/news), including 113 world premieres, 44 Asian premieres and 56 concept cars. Those figures are not markedly different from last year's Beijing show or the 2015 Shanghai show, but [compared with last year's Beijing show](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/25994.html), the "big names" on the ground in Shanghai this year were noticeably more numerous.

"The Chinese market matters" has been the theme of at least the last three Shanghai and Beijing shows. The only difference is the degree of courtship — more all-new models, more cutting-edge technology, better design, and of course a longer-term strategic outlook that goes beyond emphasizing product. Ford, for instance, plans that by 2025, 70% of the models it sells in China will be offered in new energy versions; Volkswagen says it will give the Chinese market greater autonomy, jointly carrying out localized development of new energy models with SAIC and FAW.

"[Mercedes-Benz has always strived to be the best](https://shuoke.autohome.com.cn/article/578111.html), and that never changes. You must stay humble, respect the Chinese market, focus on customers and on dealers — not a moment of slack is allowed," said Dieter Zetsche, chairman of Daimler's board.

"China must come first," [said Volkswagen Group CEO Matthias Müller](https://zh.cn.nikkei.com/industry/icar/24777-2017-04-20-09-36-06.html). Volkswagen was the world's best-selling automaker in 2016, and China is its largest single market.

Of course, they all know that the competition has escalated too.

### After the SUV, will electric cars be the next battleground?

[159](http://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2017-04-21/doc-ifyepsra5013563.shtml). That is the number of new energy vehicles officially announced for this year's Shanghai show. A car accented in blue or green — colors symbolizing electric power and clean energy — on its bodywork and badge, and a charging post strung with cables and connectors, became standard equipment on nearly every stand.

[Bloomberg projects that by 2040](https://about.bnef.com/blog/electric-vehicles-to-be-35-of-global-new-car-sales-by-2040/), new energy vehicles will account for 35% of global car sales. At present, that share is just 0.85%.

![Bloomberg's forecast for the new energy vehicle market. The blue line represents the share of electric vehicles, the dark green bars are battery-electric vehicles, and the light green is plug-in vehicles. Image/Bloomberg](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423221905JznNjDMKyEYXm75v.jpg-w600)

What people usually call new energy vehicles include hybrids like the Toyota Prius and the Corolla Levin hybrid; plug-in hybrids, such as the BYD Qin — the most common sight on Chinese streets nationwide; and battery-electric vehicles represented by Tesla.

Compared with the other two types, the biggest difference for hybrids is that they still rely mainly on a gasoline engine for propulsion. A rechargeable electric motor recovers leftover kinetic energy to charge the battery while the car is coasting or braking, and drives the vehicle directly off the battery and electric motor in pure-electric mode — but their pure-electric range is often only a few kilometers.

![An early Prius. Image/Wikipedia](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423222059JLBsg80caoIuOGpk.jpg-w600)

As the technology has spread, plug-in and battery-electric vehicles capable of running 60, 100, even 400 kilometers in pure-electric mode have become the new focus for consumers and automakers alike.

Toyota — which [sold 10 million cars worldwide in 2016](https://www.forbes.com/sites/bertelschmitt/2017/01/30/its-official-volkswagen-worlds-largest-automaker-2016-or-maybe-toyota/#77bc310d76b0), second only to Volkswagen in sales — has also begun developing battery-electric vehicles, targeting a launch around 2020. Previously, the company felt that electric vehicles using lithium-ion batteries were too costly, had too short a range, and were too inconvenient to refuel to gain traction at this stage.

[Toyota has already set up a dedicated team](http://cn.reuters.com/article/shanghai-auto-show-toyota-switch-to-ev-idCNKBS17M0J2) aiming to develop an electric vehicle with a range of over 300 kilometers per charge; the new model will be developed on the basis of existing models such as the Prius and the hybrid Corolla — primarily for the Chinese market.

"I think new energy vehicles will become a very important competitive point, both for the luxury category and for the non-luxury volume category," Amy Miu, president of Lincoln Asia Pacific, told QDaily. This time, the brand brought the MKZ Hybrid.

Lincoln's parent Ford has also introduced its first plug-in hybrid cars to China — the Mondeo Energi and the S-Max Energi — to be produced in Chongqing starting next year. Ford expects new energy vehicles to account for 15% of its passenger car sales in China by 2025.

![The Mondeo Energi.](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423222401mUV2Ht7O04PuBL6M.jpeg-w600)

The Velite 5, Buick's world-premiere car at the Shanghai show, is Buick's first plug-in hybrid, with a pure-electric range of 116 kilometers and a combined range of over 700 kilometers on a single tank. To promote the new car, Buick is offering an additional 36,000 yuan in new energy subsidies. Previously, the plug-in hybrid version of GM's Cadillac CT6, a high-end model, had a pure-electric range of 60 kilometers — only half that of the Velite.

Porsche, one of the standard-bearers of high performance, also carved out a roughly 50-square-meter space in a corner of its stand for two plug-in hybrids — the Panamera and the Cayenne. But Porsche's selling point is not how far the battery goes; it is that, with the combined effort of combustion engine and electric motor, the car's maximum output reaches 680 horsepower — a peak 20 horsepower higher than the Ferrari 488 Challenge.

![The plug-in hybrid Porsche Cayenne.](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423222504CuZeJlvEjrwoRgn6.jpeg-w600)

Although Tesla skipped this Shanghai show, any mention of electric cars has to include the company pushing the whole process forward. It is a competitor virtually no automaker can ignore, even if they do not yet believe it will upend the entire auto industry — despite annual sales and production of less than 1% of GM's, Tesla's market value recently surpassed both GM and Ford, making it the most valuable US automaker.

Back in China's new energy vehicle market, the automakers' rapid entry also faces a pricing question. Take Lincoln: "First of all, you have to make sure it has a fairly reasonable price in the new energy vehicle market," said Amy Miu. The Lincoln MKZ H starts at 330,000 yuan; by comparison, the Toyota-owned premium brand Lexus's ES300H starts at 378,000 yuan.

![The MKZ H hybrid. Image/Dazhong Kanche](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423231934HhTa0s9UFqmuZVlx.jpg-w600)

With lower brand premium and a deep understanding of China's three-tier subsidy system — national, provincial and municipal, down to the district and county level — domestic brands price their cars far more aggressively, which is also their advantage. Take the BYD Qin, priced at 200,000 yuan: at the peak of subsidies in 2015, an owner could enjoy 31,500 yuan from the national government, 30,000 yuan from Shanghai, and — for those with household registration in Minhang or Pudong — a further 20,000 yuan, plus apply for free for a Shanghai license plate worth around 80,000 yuan. Altogether, the effective price of the car came to just 38,500 yuan — cheaper than a Chery QQ.

But precisely for that reason, China's new energy vehicle market is still a policy-driven industry.

With the subsidy policy and roster adjusted early this year, new energy vehicle sales plunged 60% in January, falling back to 2015 levels. For many new energy vehicles, their greatest value lies in offering an option in first-tier cities that restrict new car registrations — for people who cannot win the license-plate lottery and are unwilling to spend 80,000 yuan bidding for a plate. They sell hot because of policy, and they are also the most affected by policy — models from companies like Emgrand, Zhidou, BYD, JAC and Denza, for instance. Fifteen of these models saw their sales drop straight to zero.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423222621KzL3oHQrJ1T6cgmN.jpeg-w600)

But you can also understand the automakers' current mindset as not wanting to miss out the way they missed out on the SUV, which became hugely popular in China. For now, the SUV star has not faded. [According to the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers](http://news.xinhuanet.com/auto/2017-01/22/c_1120359523.htm), about 24 million cars were sold in China last year, more than a third of them SUVs.

The safer bet for automakers is new energy SUVs: the Jaguar I-PACE battery-electric SUV concept, the BYD Dynasty concept, the SAIC Roewe Vision-E concept, the Chevrolet FNR-X and so on.

QDaily tallied the SUV share among 45 automakers: of the 334 cars on display, more than a third — 131 — were SUVs.

### Carmakers' anxiety about tech companies disrupting the whole industry seems to have eased, but their ambition in the "smart" direction is undimmed

The typical representative interested in intelligence is probably Ford. "Ford actually has many of the conditions and advantages to lead this autonomous driving revolution." Lei, Ford Asia-Pacific's director of smart mobility strategy, made no secret of their ambition in this direction.

Ford recently set up a smart mobility subsidiary, whose main purpose is to provide smart mobility solutions. Put simply, it is a service similar to Buick's OnStar or Volvo On Call. Ford has developed an app called FordPass that lets owners check fuel level, driving range and other information, unlock the doors and windows and book maintenance appointments within the app, with a modem placed in the car to provide connectivity.

The hallmark of this kind of service is that owners perform the most basic operations on their vehicle from their phone, and can reach a human customer service agent at the press of a button inside the car.

This is nothing new either. Other automakers at home and abroad — Jaguar, Porsche, GM; Changan, JAC, Roewe — are all showing off their connected-car and intelligent connected-vehicle systems. Hyundai arrayed more than a dozen smart wristbands on a plastic glass wall, saying you could use them to control a car.

![The wall beside Hyundai's booth, with wristbands on display that can unlock a car.](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423224233aBXhg0nw9kEHsp8Z.jpeg-w600)

As for the concept cars that represent automakers' vision of the industry's future direction, most have center consoles designed just like Tesla's: a single touchscreen of about 21 inches replaces the original layout dense with buttons and knobs. Chery's TIGGO SUV concept, meanwhile, does away with the traditional instrument panel entirely, using two display screens standing behind the steering wheel instead.

![The interior of the MG E-Motion concept car: no physical buttons on the center console, replaced by a Tesla-style full touchscreen.](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201704232242071qhglB5nfeIbLWHi.jpeg-w600)

NIO brought to the show the autonomous-driving version of its EP9 — of which only six were ever built, one for each of its founders. But you could not touch it or climb into the cockpit for a look; you could only guess from the black cylindrical bulge on the roof that it could drive itself.

Audi, Škoda, Volkswagen, JAC, Changan and other domestic and foreign automakers showed battery-electric autonomous concept cars with no front grille, no exhaust pipe and gull-wing doors — looking like they could not be road-legal for another 20 years.

What you could feel on the ground is that all the automakers were still talking about smart features and showing off autonomous driving functions — but they were not talking much about Apple and Google anymore.

This shift in sentiment is actually related to the cooling of the Silicon Valley carmaking fever. Apple announced it was no longer building a car, and Google turned its car project Waymo into a company that sells solutions — so the auto industry's anxiety about Silicon Valley disrupting the car business seems to have eased to some degree.

China's three tech giants — Tencent, Alibaba and Baidu — seemed to have lost their enthusiasm this year too. At the 2015 Shanghai show, Alibaba's two-year-old auto business group was still holding another "auto show" at the Hilton hotel about ten kilometers from the National Exhibition and Convention Center, pulling representatives of 40 domestic and foreign auto, parts and service brands into its conference hall. This year, you could pretty much only hear an emcee at the Roewe booth announce: "Order a Roewe i6 on-site today and enjoy a 7,000-yuan Alipay red-envelope discount!"

In another corner of the exhibition hall, Roewe had set up a "Banma Zhixing" experience area, which served more as corporate PR. There were no real cars in the area, only simulated demonstrations. Banma Zhixing is a connected-car company jointly founded by Alibaba and SAIC.

Last November, Baidu organized a fleet of Chery QQ autonomous cars to demonstrate the technology at the World Internet Conference in Wuzhen, which China's top leadership attended. At this year's show, Baidu announced a partnership with Chery to build driverless cars — but apart from the press release and each side touting its own AI technology and hardware and its industrial carmaking know-how, you could not see anything substantive on the ground.

![The in-car interface on the Roewe i6. The system is developed and provided by Banma Zhixang, the connected-car company jointly founded by Alibaba and SAIC.](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423224612BOpCitHou3GTv8qn.jpeg-w600)

Tencent's [biggest piece of auto-related news](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/39309.html) came before the Shanghai show opened. On March 27 this year, Tesla disclosed that Tencent had spent US$1.777 billion to buy a 5% stake in the company. Since electric car startups are all imitating Tesla anyway, Tencent simply went ahead and became its shareholder.

And earlier, when automakers were most interested in tech, they turned the Consumer Electronics Show into part of the auto show calendar — Ford fitted the Fusion (the US version of the Mondeo) with the same family of lidar used by Google's self-driving cars; Greg Hart, Amazon's vice president in charge of Echo and Alexa, remotely started a Ford from the stage using Alexa voice control.

GM came to CES with its electric vehicle plans, unveiling the production Bolt battery-electric — a direct competitor to the Model 3. The automaker, one of the world's top three by sales, chose CES over the Detroit show a few weeks later to premiere the new model.

But car companies remain wary of tech companies. On one hand, they may feel some relief at tech companies' "retreat in the face of difficulty" once they grasped the complexity of cars — yet this "dialogue," or "co-opetition," between car companies and tech companies will continue for quite some time.

### But right now, what matters most is still selling cars

Last year, Volkswagen Group sold 3.98 million cars in China, up 12% year on year — the best-selling auto brand in China, counting deliveries by Volkswagen Import, SAIC Volkswagen and FAW-Volkswagen in mainland China and Hong Kong.

Group CEO Matthias Müller described China as a "second home," stressing that "the Chinese market changes by the day and leads the world in electric vehicles." [Müller plans for Volkswagen to sell 1 million new energy vehicles globally by 2025](https://zh.cn.nikkei.com/industry/icar/24777-2017-04-20-09-36-06.html) — 60% of them in China.

Renault is a niche brand in China, [selling fewer than 40,000 cars in 2016](http://auto.gasgoo.com/News/2017/01/1906330533570005460C201.shtml). But globally, Renault sold 3.18 million cars, making it Europe's second-largest auto brand. Speaking about the state of the Chinese market and future plans, Stefan Müller, Renault's executive vice president, said the company attaches great importance to China, the world's largest auto market. Dongfeng Renault has already launched two highly competitive SUVs in the SUV market, the Koleos and the Kadjar. [The two models account for more than 80% of Renault's sales in China.](http://mini.eastday.com/a/170118115237534.html)

Before the Shanghai show opened, Ford held a brand event first. In the opening segment introducing the company's development — nearly 20 minutes long — almost 90% of the time was devoted to introducing the R&D center in Nanjing, China. After that, Ford global CEO Mark Fields discussed new plans for China, including localizing powertrain production in China by 2020 and investing US$650 million to build a new global testing center in China.

Rolls-Royce CEO Torsten Müller-Ötvös said 2016 saw the company's second-highest sales growth in its 113-year history, with the Chinese market up 23%. Rolls-Royce owners are mostly between 20 and 50 years old, and come from manufacturing, real estate, culture and entertainment, and IT — the latter two industries bringing the brand a younger crop of owners.

![Far left: Rolls-Royce CEO Torsten Müller-Ötvös.](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423224833128U46CQB9iYeTDs.jpg-w600)

So, facing Chinese consumers who are probably the most concerned in the world with the "luxury feel" of a car's exterior and the rear-seat space, even an ultra-luxury marque like Rolls-Royce has launched new long-wheelbase models specifically for this market.

![Honda Avancier](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20170423225406IgynNiosD4jdCBXG.jpeg-w600)

Likewise to cater to Chinese consumers' demands, Honda again showed China-only versions with ostentatious chrome trim, different from what it sells in the US and Japan; Audi, Mercedes-Benz and Infiniti simultaneously displayed both standard-wheelbase and long-wheelbase models, also for the sake of Chinese consumers.

After test rides and price inquiries, what visitors most loved to see were still the luxury car brands. In the 350,000-square-meter exhibition area, crowds thronged around the Ferrari, Lamborghini, McLaren, Bentley and Rolls-Royce stands, queuing up to wait for staff to let them in for photos.

![Beside the Ferrari stand, a crowd of visitors waits to be let in for a close look at the supercars.](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/2017042322492408dkX1SrZgtnu3Qs.jpeg-w600)

"I'm at the Lamborghini stand now — these cars all look the same. What else do you want to see? A Mercedes? That's a bit far. Give me some rockets and sports cars to cheer me up, come on," said one livestreamer after the Lamborghini press conference ended, addressing the viewers on the other end of the livestream app running on the phone clamped to his selfie stick.

On the first day the public days opened, Hall 8.1 — where the luxury cars were clustered — restricted entry.

Domestic brands trying to show off their design prowess and technological strength — Geely's sub-brand Lynk & Co, Great Wall Motor's sub-brand WEY, BYD, Changan, NIO and Roewe — all brought their near-production and concept cars.

But when it came time to explain a car's features to passengers, what sales staff and automaker employees talked about most was how long the wheelbase was, whether the interior used leather, whether there was a panoramic sunroof, and how big the new energy subsidies were. The concept cars placed in the most prominent positions on the stands were mostly roped off or off-limits for getting in to experience.

On the second media day, 4S dealership salespeople had already begun replacing automaker staff, explaining models to visitors at the stands and registering the details of prospective buyers.

At that point, you start to feel that nothing much has changed at all.
