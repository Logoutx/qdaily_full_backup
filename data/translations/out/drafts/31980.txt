You never have to worry about her stirring up scandals — affairs, drugs, drunken streaking, tax evasion, plastic surgery. Hatsune (at least officially, for now) is a good kid you can count on.

A goddess sashays past, flinging her flowing long hair with confidence, and the boys' hearts skip a beat... Yawn. For shampoo ads that follow this formula, you've probably long since gone visually numb.

So what if we swapped the lead for a 2D virtual idol?

Recently, Lux — the Unilever-owned brand — brought "virtual idol" Hatsune Miku together with Hollywood star Scarlett Johansson in Japan to shoot an ad, drumming up publicity for its newly launched Super Rich Shine Straight & Beauty haircare line.

The ad's premise is anything but complicated. Hatsune Miku, normally sporting twin tails, happens to catch Scarlett confidently endorsing shampoo on a street TV screen, and decides to "follow suit": she unties her twin tails and appears with a head of green, waist-length straight hair.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908131129UMl9VDSPmeRaoETY.png-w600)

What follows is a bling-bling transformation: the transformed Hatsune Miku even puts on a sexy pink slip dress and performs on stage for everyone, earning plenty of applause. At the end she even receives an encouraging video message from the goddess Scarlett.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908130827BrQZWo6jtwPLa7C1.jpg-w600)

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908131143vRJraPm4WHEbkwdq.png-w600)

Like most shampoo ads, the ending can't skip the formulaic "obligatory bit": Hatsune Miku strides like a princess in front of the Arc de Triomphe in France, her long hair enjoying the tender care of Lux shampoo......

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908131010bzmUhwdvI0jlBFtg.jpg-w600)

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908130838VA7QiKNI2Px1lYuB.png-w600)

Overall, the ad doesn't have much fresh creativity beyond Hatsune Miku's inclusion. [Lux, which claims an important position in Japan's shampoo market](http://baike.baidu.com/link?url=q8RSQnnUxlohnG6FMjpfifC1aSSK4dUtsyz6D4E-A3rMrnLDvKap_p1e2VJlc0mnVNbVgX9Y0lf6WXBvklivZ1bBpY-4YSf2MlcrQFGXe0i) mostly wants to ride on Hatsune Miku to raise its own profile another notch. The spot was also deliberately released on Hatsune Miku's ninth birthday (August 31).

For anyone into anime or games, this virtual songstress is hardly an unfamiliar "figure."

Hatsune Miku, born August 31, 2007, was launched by Japanese voice-production and music-software company Crypton Future Media; the name means "the first sound from the future" in Japanese.

In fact, it is [a female-singer software based on a sound bank](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/9483.html), easy to operate: music lovers only need to input a melody and lyrics into the software, and it sings a synthesized song. Users can also upload these songs to Niconico or YouTube; [to date, these platforms carry more than 100,000 user-posted songs](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s?__biz=MjM5ODA1NzI2MA==&mid=211976733&idx=2&sn=15e068ea0d03ff39c472b6bf8b7b000a&scene=1&srcid=0906hHYVjbO9T1BfdABHb6De&key=7b81aac53bd2393dcf9f0b46fa6924eab283cd75f8c3dd027ec34257f5cfee3b0aa097cbac741026574c7d1f3d809620&ascene=0&uin=Mjk0NTEwNjYwMA%3D%3D&devicetype=iMac+MacBookAir7%2C2+OSX+OSX+10.11.3+build(15D21)&version=11020201&pass_ticket=oC8GGerE%2FlN18YQhHCWM0FZ%2B8Gt4v0cMAWn9MQ6rEAIUOHO0XrKgwjFXre9kCQeJ).

But at first, the software didn't have the Hatsune Miku character image at all — [netizens complained it wore a "if you're not a professional, stay away from me" look](http://blackx.baijia.baidu.com/article/553610), and you can tell from the packaging......

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/201609081313142c18WVDMypsOBSiA.png-w600)

To better attract users, the company later packaged this voice into a "kawaii" female idol: forever 16, with a sweet smile, cool scallion-green extra-long twin tails ([on Zhihu, fans have even measured the lengths of her bangs and twin tails](http://www.zhihu.com/topic/19596639/hot)), often wearing miniskirts, short in height (158 cm) but with long, slender legs. Japan is already an anime superpower, and building this image looked very much like catering to otaku tastes.

On top of that, this singer needs no sleep and no food, has a wide repertoire — [everything from 1980s oldies to pop songs and dance tracks](http://www.zhihu.com/topic/19596639/hot) — and her signature song is the one netizens call "the song you've definitely heard even if you don't know its name": 《甩葱歌》 (*Ievan Polkka*).

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908133513Zz4TLNoriXp8ce5D.jpg-w600)

The brilliant thing about Crypton Future Media is this: because Hatsune Miku gave the software a concrete image, what was originally a software business became fan-driven. And it was welcomed all over the world.

In 2009, Hatsune Miku began holding multiple concerts in Japan as a "virtual singer." Projected onto a transparent panel with rear-projection technology, she could sing and dance for her fans. [Some fans sitting in the front rows even said](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s?__biz=MjM5ODA1NzI2MA==&mid=211976733&idx=2&sn=15e068ea0d03ff39c472b6bf8b7b000a&scene=1&srcid=0906hHYVjbO9T1BfdABHb6De&key=7b81aac53bd2393d1eb83d77c3402a57505390db8433e015e69f3590d73193aed9b0b488601719a6432b16fbdb7743dd&ascene=0&uin=Mjk0NTEwNjYwMA%3D%3D&devicetype=iMac+MacBookAir7%2C2+OSX+OSX+10.11.3+build(15D21)&version=11020201&pass_ticket=oC8GGerE%2FlN18YQhHCWM0FZ%2B8Gt4v0cMAWn9MQ6rEAIUOHO0XrKgwjFXre9kCQeJ) that, thanks to the holographic technology, they could occasionally catch a glimpse of her "pantsu" (the Japanese rendering of "pants," meaning underwear) when she danced.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908131652nvlqDkUjVdP4xSHe.jpg-w600)

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908131847jrYVt4bhamqQ83Tn.gif-w600Gif)

At Hatsune Miku's first Tokyo concert, [2,500 tickets sold out in an instant once sales opened, and another 30,000 people watched the paid livestream online](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/9483.html). The concerts then went overseas — New York, Los Angeles, Singapore, and elsewhere. Last year she performed in Shanghai ([all tickets for the four consecutive shows were snapped up within eight minutes of going on sale](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s?__biz=MjM5ODA1NzI2MA==&mid=211976733&idx=2&sn=15e068ea0d03ff39c472b6bf8b7b000a&scene=1&srcid=0906hHYVjbO9T1BfdABHb6De&key=7b81aac53bd2393dcf9f0b46fa6924eab283cd75f8c3dd027ec34257f5cfee3b0aa097cbac741026574c7d1f3d809620&ascene=0&uin=Mjk0NTEwNjYwMA%3D%3D&devicetype=iMac+MacBookAir7%2C2+OSX+OSX+10.11.3+build(15D21)&version=11020201&pass_ticket=oC8GGerE%2FlN18YQhHCWM0FZ%2B8Gt4v0cMAWn9MQ6rEAIUOHO0XrKgwjFXre9kCQeJ); some concertgoers said that [more than half the venue was bespectacled male otaku](http://www.vccoo.com/v/53a664?source=rss), and this December the concerts will land in Beijing.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908131540M4YwqIBb1TaP2jeh.png-w600)

As for why her concerts are so hot: beyond the support of the otaku boys, [some female fans also described](http://www.vccoo.com/v/53a664?source=rss) [this feeling](http://www.vccoo.com/v/53a664?source=rss): her music is unique — sounds no human can produce — and when people listen to such high-frequency music, it feels hypnotically catchy, with a strong looping quality.

Beyond concerts, her bookings, schedule, and daily routine are no different from a real star's.

Hatsune Miku began getting invited onto TV programs: last October, for instance, she guested on the American late-night talk show 《大卫深夜秀》 and performed the song "Sharing the World." On social media (Facebook, Weibo, Twitter, etc.) she has her own accounts, where she posts her own performance updates and occasionally her status — like playing April Fools' jokes and sending Valentine's greetings. When fans @ her to express their affection, she reposts with "thank you" and a cute emoticon: ＼(^o^)／.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908131718AD03p4u9ibLEVtjH.png-w600)

Thanks to the company's careful packaging, Hatsune Miku has amassed quite a few fans. [According to 2011 statistics](http://psp.duowan.com/1108/176727210370.html), her fan count had already reached 390 million. The same holds on social platforms: her Twitter has over 110,000 followers, her Sina Weibo nearly 400,000, and her Facebook page has over 2.52 million likes.

The other day was her birthday, and her Weibo comment section, plus the danmu on short videos on AcFun and Bilibili, saw a wave of fan blessings with cloying nicknames: wife, the world's No. 1 princess, Princess, Miku-chan, goddess, "oni, happy birthday!"

A considerable money-making ability naturally followed.

Within two years of launch, [the software sold more than 50,000 copies in total](http://zh.wikipedia.org/zh/%E5%88%9D%E9%9F%B3%E6%9C%AA%E4%BE%86#.E4.B8.8ELady_Gaga), [while music software without a virtual character typically averages sales of maybe only 200 to 300 copies](http://zh.wikipedia.org/zh/%E5%88%9D%E9%9F%B3%E6%9C%AA%E4%BE%86#.E4.B8.8ELady_Gaga). In 2014, it also announced on its official Twitter that total software sales [had surpassed 2.5 million copies](http://zh.wikipedia.org/zh/%E5%88%9D%E9%9F%B3%E6%9C%AA%E4%BE%86#.E4.B8.8ELady_Gaga). But the software isn't cheap: [according to its official Taobao store's pricing](https://shop59846142.taobao.com/category-250757955.htm?spm=a1z10.3-c.w4010-1202650406.10.RfpYZr&search=y&catName=%D5%FD%B0%E6%C8%ED%BC%FE#bd), the Japanese version costs 1,100 yuan, while the bundled English-and-Japanese version costs 1,300 yuan.

Partnering with celebrities and marketing to the fan economy has become a standard trick for brands. Compared with other methods, it can corral a batch of consumers much faster. Although Hatsune Miku is a virtual singer, that doesn't stop her from taking endorsements, shooting ads, or cooperating with marketing campaigns — plenty of brands have come knocking, and the Lux shampoo ad is not the first she's shot.

Back in December 2011, Hatsune Miku was invited to [serve as the face of Google's Chrome browser in Japan](http://www.hexieshe.com/637100/). Around the same time, Google [ran different promotional models in different regions](http://www.hexieshe.com/637100/): in its US home market, it chose chart-topping singers Lady Gaga and Justin Bieber.

And in Japan, [Google said at the time that IE held over 50% of the browser market there, far higher than in other developed countries](http://www.hexieshe.com/637100/). To better break into the Japanese market, it might need a breakthrough — a spokesperson who had to be a popular singer embraced by Japanese young people and with some influence globally. So in Japan, it ultimately chose Hatsune Miku.

The one-minute ad uses the theme song "Tell Your World" sung by Hatsune Miku, telling how music lovers around the world use the internet and Hatsune Miku software to create their own music; the film weaves in quite a few Google products, including the Chrome browser and Google Earth. The closing text reads "Everyone, creator," stressing that creators can't do without the internet — and, "more importantly," can't do without the Chrome browser.

After the ad aired, [it racked up 580,000 views in four days](http://tieba.baidu.com/p/1366764733), though there wasn't much discussion of the ad's content itself; instead, the theme song "Tell Your World" sparked heated discussion, so the company decided to [release the digital album in 217 countries worldwide](http://www.zhihu.com/topic/19596639/hot), making Hatsune Miku [the Japanese singer with the record for digital releases in the most countries](http://www.zhihu.com/topic/19596639/hot). Five years on, we can see that the video has now [accumulated over 14 million views on YouTube](https://youtu.be/PqJNc9KVIZE).

Beyond Google, car brands also weren't content with real-star endorsements and began to value Hatsune Miku's draw among fans. In 2011, Toyota launched the 11th-generation new model of its Corolla series in the US; to match the cool car, Hatsune Miku changed her usual look and handsomely sang her single "World Is Mine" in the ad (a song fans nicknamed 《世界第一的公主殿下》).

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908145854KChV4w2J3sdFuZkn.png-w600)

Though the endorsement [was called a bit "unprecedented" at the time](http://since1989.org/quotation/acg/corollamiku.html) — hiring a virtual idol, no less — [some commentators said](http://since1989.org/quotation/acg/corollamiku.html) Japanese manufacturers seemed to have ["a little too much confidence" in the number of American otaku](http://since1989.org/quotation/acg/corollamiku.html). But Toyota's move was mostly about hitching a ride on Hatsune Miku's concert then planned for Los Angeles. [According to reports](http://www.ed2000.com/ShowFile/274541.html), presale tickets for that concert sold out entirely within two weeks, with as many as 6,000 fans attending.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/2016090814582950nZCSBAEXFzsQVq.png-w600)

Seeing Toyota's move, BMW couldn't sit still either. Though it didn't shoot an ad, BMW put Hatsune Miku's image onto the body of a Z4. To reflect the race car's "beast performance," Hatsune Miku even got her hair permed into curls — though some fans [complained the look resembled a female "Transformer"](http://news.mydrivers.com/1/292/292875.htm).

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908150436gaurzZiL76dCvOHy.png-w600)

In China, Changan Automobile followed suit, printing the image of virtual idol Luo Tianyi onto a car body, but the livery didn't look very realistic and drew mockery from some netizens.

Even in the famously aloof luxury-goods world, Hatsune Miku has been in demand.

In 2013, the stage musical 《THE END》 starring Hatsune Miku held its premiere in Tokyo. It happened to coincide with LV's spring-summer show, where designer Marc Jacobs presented checkerboard dresses that season. In Europe and the US, LV had long since dressed a string of actresses (such as Jessica Alba and "Twilight" star Kristen Stewart) in this design. But in Asia, LV gave the new piece to Hatsune Miku instead, specially making some "alterations" for her petite figure so it would look more form-fitting.

![](http://img.qdaily.com/uploads/20160908132235QITKPZJGUbW9kFOl.jpg-w600)

Hatsune Miku in these checkered little dresses drew opposition from some fans, who felt the look was rather cold and didn't suit her; but other fans thought getting sponsorship from a big brand was "a validation of her popularity," while some onlookers merely wondered ["has some luxury brand been hexed by the 2D world to make a choice like this"](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s?__biz=MjM5OTY1MDMxMg==&mid=401458806&idx=1&sn=d9bfeadda711030d73c017a01377552b&scene=1&srcid=09062yGP8IzBKaiWXJK6deRk&key=7b81aac53bd2393d1a2745278f504b09416367f7de2d0807045a56963fb59fbc48b0636e308751bead8a2a0a9a3ee1b0&ascene=0&uin=Mjk0NTEwNjYwMA%3D%3D&devicetype=iMac+MacBookAir7%2C2+OSX+OSX+10.11.3+build(15D21)&version=11020201&pass_ticket=oC8GGerE%2FlN18YQhHCWM0FZ%2B8Gt4v0cMAWn9MQ6rEAIUOHO0XrKgwjFXre9kCQeJ)......

Although LV never explained publicly why it made custom clothing for Hatsune Miku, [from a media interview Nicolas Ghesquière gave after taking over as LV's creative director in 2013](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti), we can get some idea of it. He said: ["We're no longer in an era where making pretty clothes is enough; you also have to make women feel confident in them, give them something with individuality, something that shows they can handle whatever they love doing."](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti)

From past ambassadors like Angelina Jolie, Scarlett Johansson, and Madonna to Fan Bingbing over the years, [beyond looks and personal charisma, LV has always stressed that it values traits like women's confidence and independence](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti). On this point, Hatsune Miku could almost be called flawless.

And more importantly, it may be [about catering to young people's tastes, especially in the Asian market](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti). [According to LV's 2014 financial report](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti), Asia accounted for 36% of its total sales — this is very likely part of LV's Asia strategy.

Perhaps having tasted the sweetness of partnering with a 2D virtual idol, in its 2016 spring-summer campaign LV turned to another virtual idol — Lightning, the character from 《最终幻想13》, to shoot the ads. Creative director Nicolas Ghesquière posted the ad film on his personal Instagram account: Lightning wears the new spring-summer collection with a touch of lace trim and carries an LV bag. In just two days it received [over 3,000 likes](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti). [But some fans said](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti), "I think she should be in armor, holding a sword," and "Why put her in these lacy dresses, and that bag — it doesn't suit her at all!"

Asked "why Lightning as the new season's fashion ambassador," [Nicolas's answer seemed a bit perfunctory: "Reality and fantasy have become one, and Lightning is a true heroine."](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti)

But in fact, well before the official collaboration, Nicolas had once [hinted at](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti) the opportunity for working with Lightning, since he himself is an avid anime and game fan, with a "preference" for elements like "heroines," "the digital age," and "anime." [In an interview with 《Vogue》](http://www.qdaily.com/articles/19922.html?source=chouti), he also confessed that his design inspiration comes from games, anime, and fantasy films — like the game 《Minecraft》 and the sci-fi film 《Tron》.

Back to Hatsune Miku: in practice, her endorsements mostly concentrate on non-big-ticket goods that fans can easily get their hands on.

For example, a Sony over-ear headphone based on the MDR-1A. A singer endorsing headphones is no surprise at all — fans need headphones to listen to music, so why not pick a limited-edition model bearing their idol's livery? This fan psychology is exactly what Sony spotted.

In fact, this headphone had long been available in a regular edition, priced at 1,399 yuan, while the Hatsune limited edition cost 200 yuan more than the regular version. Beyond the custom paint job on the earcups, [the two had no difference in technical specs](http://news.mydrivers.com/1/456/456073.htm). The headphone was [also released in China](http://news.mydrivers.com/1/456/456073.htm) (including Sony's Tmall official flagship store, Sony's online store, and offline direct-operated stores), limited to 1,000 units. [According to reports](http://news.mydrivers.com/1/456/456073.htm), shortly after launch, both its Tmall official store and Sony's online store showed it as sold out, though reservations were possible.

The boy instantly turns into a smitten fanboy, nosebleed barely withheld......

The latter invited her to perform at the recently held Taobao Maker Festival. According to Taobao's [declared "transformation from shopping to making"](http://disansheng.baijia.baidu.com/article/555885), the venue displayed plenty of smart products involving VR, AR, drones, robots, and the like, and inviting Hatsune Miku — who holds concerts using rear-projection and holographic technology — clearly fit this theme.

[Taobao also revealed that current user research shows more than 30% of its users are post-90s, and 80% of its users are post-80s plus post-90s](http://disansheng.baijia.baidu.com/article/555885). Given this, in choosing singers to perform, the Maker Festival leans closer to young people's tastes — [for instance, it picked Hua Chenyu, Su Yunying, and others](http://news.mydrivers.com/1/490/490685.htm) — plus Hatsune Miku of the 2D world.

Jonathan Schenker, CEO of Bookmark Entertainment (a middleman company that helps brands and celebrity agencies connect, essentially a go-between), told us that choosing the right spokesperson is actually a complex process. The due diligence is extensive, including the person's history and past activities, from which to consider whether their reputation aligns with the brand's values; if a celebrity can enhance or improve the brand's existing image, that becomes an important selection criterion. Next is considering whether the endorsement can make the target audience feel resonance.

In addition, Jonathan added that, based on their work experience, brands are also quite interested in the following factors and may even run analyses on them: the celebrity's situation on social media or the internet (such as how often the public searches their name), whether movies, TV shows, concerts, and the like are coming up, and which brands they have endorsed before.

Measured against the criteria above, Hatsune could count as a quality idol.

[As one Zhihu user, @Jiang Jingbo, put it](http://www.zhihu.com/question/19685474): "What we pursue is an idol who never deteriorates, but real-life idols will inevitably decay — their skin may sag, wrinkles may appear, they may even smoke, or retire because of a scandal."

Compared with these real-life/traditional idols, signing Hatsune Miku as a spokesperson is obviously the safer choice for brands — and much less hassle, because [you never have to worry about her stirring up scandals — affairs, drugs, drunken streaking, tax evasion, plastic surgery](http://www.zhihu.com/question/19685474); [Hatsune (at least officially, for now) is a good kid you can count on](http://www.zhihu.com/question/19685474).

Moreover, Hatsune has no dark history (her history is whatever the company and users have given her), yet she has an unaging face and permanently online stamina. She still [maintains a pace of at least one concert a year](http://www.zhihujingxuan.com/12239.html), and lip-syncing is utterly out of the question. She is also an approachable idol — giving grassroots music lovers who can't afford a professional recording studio an easy chance to create. [A Bilibili danmu comment put it similarly](http://www.zhihujingxuan.com/12239.html) in evaluating her FamilyMart endorsement: she can wear big brands, and she can also carry off an ordinary uniform.

But endorsements of this kind may lack a human element; still, Jonathan points out that as a wildly popular fictional character, emotional bonds have already formed between her, consumers, and brands. Seen this way, based on this psychological effect, the endorsement effect may not be bad at all.

Although Hatsune has never disclosed the fees for these endorsements, adding in revenue from licensing all kinds of merchandise, [Akira Ibe, an analyst at Japan's Nomura Research Institute, once did a calculation](http://www.hexieshe.com/636241/): spending related to Hatsune Miku [has long since exceeded 10 billion yen (roughly 500 million yuan)](http://www.hexieshe.com/636241/) — a net worth that easily makes Hatsune Miku the most successful virtual singer in history.
