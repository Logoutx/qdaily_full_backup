### What the Agenda Setters Are Talking About

*National Geographic* magazine: [*For Decades, Our Coverage Was Racist. To Rise Above Our Past, We Must Acknowledge It.*](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/2018/04/from-the-editor-race-racism-history/)

The editors of *National Geographic* planned to devote their upcoming April special issue to the theme of "race." In preparing it, they realized that the 130-year-old publication itself might have a race problem, so they invited John Edwin Mason, a University of Virginia professor of African history and history of photography, to comb through the archives and examine whether the authoritative magazine had fallen short in its coverage of people of color in the United States and abroad.

The conclusions were disheartening.

"For much of its history, the magazine's writing and imagery reproduced a racial hierarchy, with whites at the top and yellow and black people at the bottom," John Edwin Mason told the editors.

The magazine made no mention whatsoever of any urban, well-educated Africans. Black people were always depicted as static, primitive and technologically backward — usually unclothed, or appearing ignorant. In a 1916 story about Australia, for example, a photo caption read: "South Australian Blackfellows: these savages rank lowest in intelligence of all human beings." This image persisted into the 1970s and shaped how *National Geographic*'s readers — mostly middle-class whites — perceived black people.

After John Edwin Mason submitted his findings, the editors did not object; instead they placed the findings prominently in the magazine under a blunt headline — *For Decades, Our Coverage Was Racist. To Rise Above Our Past, We Must Acknowledge It*.

The piece was written by *National Geographic*'s current editor-in-chief, Susan Goldberg. She said, "I am the tenth editor of *National Geographic* since its founding in 1888, and the first woman and the first Jewish person to serve as editor. Sharing the magazine's shocking discriminatory coverage of the past is painful for me. But since we are covering the topic of race, I felt we should examine ourselves before examining others."

So far, the story has received mostly positive reviews on social media. Two American online outlets, Splinter and The Root, called the editorial decision an encouraging "first step."

### News Makers of the Past 24 Hours

[Trump fires Secretary of State Tillerson](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/03/13/us/politics/trump-tillerson-pompeo.html).

On Tuesday, Trump announced on Twitter that he would remove Secretary of State Rex Tillerson and replace him with CIA Director Mike Pompeo. Gina Haspel, currently the CIA's deputy director, will be promoted to director, making her the first woman to lead the agency.

Tillerson learned of the news on Tuesday morning only after checking Twitter on an aide's phone. Last Friday, however, White House Chief of Staff John F. Kelly had called Tillerson, asked him to cut short his trip to Africa, and said "you may get a tweet."

Since taking office, Tillerson had publicly disagreed with Trump on numerous issues, including Saudi Arabia's severing of ties with Qatar and how the United States should respond to Russia's cyberattacks.

Trump explained to reporters at the White House: "We were not really thinking the same." A senior administration official said Trump had now decided to replace Tillerson in order to assemble a new team ahead of his May meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un and amid numerous ongoing trade negotiations.

On Tuesday afternoon, Tillerson spoke at the State Department, saying Trump had just called him personally to deliver the news — more than three hours after Trump tweeted it to his 49 million followers. Tillerson said he would hand over all duties quickly by the end of the day and formally step down on March 31. He also encouraged the deputy secretary, assistant secretaries and the policy team to carry on and keep doing their jobs.

"I'll now return to private life, as a citizen, a proud American," he said. "It has been a true honor to serve my country." He took no questions and promptly left the press briefing room.

### What Else Happened in the World

**The Trump administration plans to impose tariffs on $60 billion worth of Chinese imports**, aimed mainly at the technology and telecommunications sectors. China's trade surplus with the United States currently stands at $375 billion.

**Google plans to launch a free Wi-Fi hotspot service in Mexico**, setting up at least 56 hotspots through Google Station. Mexico is the first country in Latin America to get the service, and the third Google Station market after India and Indonesia.

**A prominent Iranian scholar has been sentenced to 18 months in prison**. Sadegh Zibakalam, a professor of political science at the University of Tehran, gave a phone interview to Deutsche Welle, Germany's international broadcaster, on January 1 this year, and was sentenced on Tuesday on charges including "giving an interview to foreign media in Persian" and "plotting against directives of the Islamic Republic and spreading false information."

Global financial leaders plan to **call on international standard-setting bodies to strengthen regulation of crypto-assets** at the G20 summit, and to assess whether a multilateral response mechanism is needed. This year's G20 meeting of finance ministers and central bank governors will be held in Buenos Aires on March 19–20.

**The global art market recovered somewhat last year, with high-end artworks growing fastest**. According to the latest data from UBS and Art Basel, total global art sales grew 12% in 2017 to $63.8 billion, still below the 2014 record of $68 billion. But looking only at artworks with auction prices above $1 million, sales in that category surged 125% year over year.

Students at some 3,000 schools across the United States plan to march on Wednesday local time to **commemorate the Florida high school shooting one month ago**. The February 14 shooting at Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School in Parkland left 17 people dead. The students hope the marches will press for new gun safety legislation, including bans on assault rifles and high-capacity magazines, as well as comprehensive background checks.

**Iran Air will open the job of pilot to women for the first time this year**. The airline's CEO, Farzaneh Sharafbafi, announced the news on Sunday local time. She herself is also the first female CEO in Iran Air's 74-year history.

### Someone Says

**["I am afraid that Facebook has now turned into a beast, and not what it was originally intended to be,"](https://qz.com/1228010/in-myanmar-facebook-has-now-turned-into-a-beast-un-investigators-say/)** said UN investigator Yanghee Lee.

Yanghee Lee and her colleagues came to Myanmar to investigate whether violence against the Rohingya — a Muslim ethnic group in Myanmar — had escalated into genocide. They found that social media had played a key role, and that ["in Myanmar, social media is Facebook, and Facebook is social media"](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-myanmar-rohingya-facebook/u-n-investigators-cite-facebook-role-in-myanmar-crisis-idUSKCN1GO2PN).

Research by a local digital marketing agency found that 20% of Myanmar's total population uses Facebook regularly. In this military-controlled country, Facebook has also become people's primary source of information. Ultranationalists have exploited the platform to incite hatred against the Rohingya, and the government has carried out "clearance operations" against the group; so far thousands have been killed or driven out of the country.

According to *The Washington Post*, "anti-Rohingya" campaigns on Facebook came from multiple accounts, the best known including the ultranationalist monk Ashin Wirathu, as well as accounts set up by the army and the government. [Some Rohingya activists told media last year that their own posts on social media were also censored by the platform](https://www.thedailybeast.com/exclusive-rohingya-activists-say-facebook-silences-them).

Two researchers at Dalhousie University in Canada, Robert Huish and Patrick Balazo, meanwhile described to [qz.com](https://qz.com/1228010/in-myanmar-facebook-has-now-turned-into-a-beast-un-investigators-say/) how the hatred spread on Facebook:

> Anti-Rohingya content included outright racist cartoons, fake images, and deliberately fabricated news reports. Widely shared, this content normalized hate speech and dominated public perception of events. People increasingly supported the use of violence against the Rohingya — and then went back online to celebrate it.

Facebook said it has begun trying to address the problems in Myanmar: publishing a localized version of its guidelines for using Facebook, and hiring more moderators to review posts, regularly removing hate speech — including that of Ashin Wirathu himself.

### You Might Also Be Interested In

A research project on green agricultural development, designed by a team from China Agricultural University with the participation of 25 teaching and research institutions nationwide including Hebei Agricultural University, Jilin Agricultural University and Northwest A&F University, has published its findings in the international academic journal *Nature*.

The study, titled [*Pursuing Sustainable Productivity with Millions of Smallholder Farmers*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature25785.epdf?referrer_access_token=5NIJMTV3onDdxD4hSE8f09RgN0jAjWel9jnR3ZoTv0Oj-5H164-GqNnBhBT1mpMNz7K7hfFpIkYI4REwCNssRHN7b8Y8WJs_3cEHCRinqzObTMyyBh9s4A9FqqKoyd3VoOxICpjRMhCu_ckJennj2wT4GIr1HHU8luLG6T6YXrGLBL5nqf3vxUQQiQcOp8lEi5hsXF0i7XRKhXDprFtMcAxxR4jTKzWNIsksXwixg1kqcCRsf1YfnoYgxMN9-LcaUzDC-EKMObFc7spgHKqcKQ%3D%3D&tracking_referrer=qz.com), took 10 years, involved $54 million in research funding, and engaged 1,152 researchers and 20.9 million farmers across 452 counties. The team conducted 13,123 field trials in China's major production regions for the three staple grain crops — wheat, corn and rice — **applying a new technique called "integrated soil-crop system management."**

The technique addresses a series of problems in intensive grain production: heavy fertilizer use, high resource and environmental costs, diminishing yield gains from fertilization, and low nutrient-use efficiency. Through quantitative design of high-yield crop communities that make efficient use of light and temperature resources, it taps the yield potential of crop varieties; by quantitatively regulating water and fertilizer supply in the root zone, it supports high-yield communities in achieving efficient resource use, ultimately reducing environmental pollution and reconciling high aboveground crop yields with efficient use of water and fertilizer in the soil.

The results showed that **corn, rice and wheat grown with the technique averaged 20.6% higher yields than local farmers' usual practices. At the same time, nitrogen fertilizer use was reduced by 14.5%, while emissions intensity of reactive nitrogen and of greenhouse gases fell by 34.8% and 27.0% respectively**.

[Over the past 10 years, the project saved farmers $12.2 billion in costs](https://qz.com/1228061/chinas-agricultural-miracle-that-could-feed-the-planet-without-destroying-it/).
